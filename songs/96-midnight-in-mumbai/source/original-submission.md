# Midnight in Mumbai — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:** One night in a city that never closes. A woman who has lived in
Mumbai for years walks out to the sea wall with no plan, meets a stranger,
and spends the hours until dawn on a promenade, a scooter and a crate beside
a chai stall — discovering that the place she has been tired of has been
awake and waiting for her the whole time.

**Mood:** Warm, unhurried, wide open. Not a love song about a person so much
as a love song about a night, with a person in it.

**Message:** "The city was never the thing that was asleep."

**Genre:** Bollywood-fusion pop — modern cinematic pop production carrying
tabla, sitar, bansuri and full Hindi-film strings. Think: A. R. Rahman x
Prateek Kuhad x Dua Lipa's cleaner side. Sung in neutral Indian English, no
slang anywhere.

**Tempo & Key:** 110 BPM, 4/4, B minor, opening toward its relative major in
the pre-chorus. The final chorus lifts a full tone.

**Vocal setup:** Female lead, solo. Clear neutral Indian English, unhurried
and warm, with light ornamental turns on chorus phrase endings rather than
pop runs. Stacked female harmonies on the hook, wordless echoes in the outro.

**Instrumentation:**
- Tabla (the heartbeat), dholak doubling it in the final chorus
- Warm modern electric bass and fingerpicked acoustic guitar
- Cinematic strings written in Hindi-film unison, from the first pre-chorus
- Sitar plucks answering the vocal; bansuri flute on the intro, the second
  chorus countermelody and the last figure of the break
- Tanpura drone under the whole record, felt more than heard
- Ambience: sea, distant traffic, a shutter coming down, a kettle, a scooter
  catching, wind, a ferry horn

**Mood arc:** Intro, the sea wall and no reason to be there. Verse one, the
street emptying and the stranger arriving. Pre-chorus, following him down a
shuttered road. Chorus, the whole city awake and celebrating. Verse two, the
scooter and the empty sea link. Pre-chorus, the chai stall that never closes.
Bridge, seven years in a city she is only now hearing. Final chorus, the lift.
Outro, the first ferry and the walk home.

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – sea ambience, one sitar pluck, no drums]

```
Salt in the air on the wall at Marine Drive,
A long curve of lamps like a necklace of light.
I came out for nothing, with no reason to be here,
And the city said, "stay—there is more to the night."
```

- Scene: The sea-facing promenade at midnight. The long curve of streetlamps running away around the bay.
- Shot: A wide from behind her, sitting on the sea wall, small against the whole curve of light.
- Cut to: Extreme close-up of sea spray hitting the concrete beside her hand; a strand of hair moving in the salt wind.
- Emotion: Not sad. Restless in a way she has not admitted to herself.
- Lighting: Sodium gold from the lamp curve, deep blue-black sea, everything slightly hazy with monsoon air.

### [verse 1 – soft tabla, bass, acoustic guitar]

```
The vendors are folding their tables away,
The taxis are humming in one yellow line.
A boy sells his jasmine to nobody at all,
And the smell of it follows me down to the tide.
I was not looking for anybody tonight,
I was counting the boats and the lamps on the sea.
Then a stranger came over and asked for the time,
And we laughed, because neither of us cared to know.
```

- Scene: The promenade emptying. Vendors packing up, a line of yellow-and-black taxis idling, a boy with a tray of jasmine strings and no customers.
- Shot: A slow lateral tracking move along the sea wall past all of it, her walking in the same direction on the other side of frame.
- Cut to: Her buying nothing from the jasmine boy but taking one string anyway; macro on the white flowers in her hand.
- Cut to: A young man arriving beside her at the wall, asking something, both of them laughing — the first genuine laugh of the film.
- Emotion: Surprise at herself for enjoying it.
- Lighting: Warm sodium on the pavement, cool sea behind, taxi headlights raking across.

### [pre-chorus 1 – rising strings, doubled tabla]

```
He said, "there is chai at the end of this road,
And the kettle sings louder than any radio."
So I walked with a stranger past the shuttered shops,
And the sea kept the time of the way we would go.
```

- Scene: A shuttered street running inland from the sea.
- Shot: The two of them walking away from camera down the middle of the empty road, shutters and closed signs on both sides.
- Cut to: A steel kettle at full boil on a stall burner at the far end of the street.
- Emotion: The decision, made without deciding.
- Lighting: One working tube light per shopfront, everything else dark; the stall glowing orange at the end of the road like a target.

### [chorus 1 – full strings, kit and tabla, sitar answers]

```
Midnight in Mumbai, and the city's wide awake,
Every window a lantern, every street a second chance.
Midnight in Mumbai, and the sea will not sleep,
So I am not sleeping while the whole town wants to dance.
Give me chai in a glass and a scooter and sea spray,
Give me warm monsoon air on my face.
Midnight in Mumbai, and the city's wide awake,
And a stranger's voice is turning into a place.
```

- Scene: The city as a single organism at midnight. This is the widest sequence in the film.
- Shots: A drone rising over the lamp curve until the whole bay is one line of gold. A tower block where every third window is still lit. A night market with cloth being folded and fruit being stacked for morning. Two boys playing cricket under a streetlight. A dog asleep across a doorway. Her walking through all of it with the jasmine string on her wrist.
- Emotion: Open, unguarded, delighted.
- Lighting: Saturated warm golds and greens, wet tarmac, monsoon haze catching every light source.

### [verse 2 – shaker and rim, strings withheld]

```
He wakes up the scooter and I hold on politely,
Then the road opens out and I hold on for real.
The sea link is empty and the wind takes my scarf,
And I laugh like a woman with nothing to conceal.
He tells me his mother still calls him at midnight,
I tell him my flat has one window and a plant.
Two strangers, two histories, one road and one country,
And the city is listening, and the city understands.
```

- Scene: The scooter ride. The emotional centre of the song.
- Shot: A kick-start, the engine catching, her hand hovering an inch off his jacket and then holding on properly as the road opens.
- Cut to: The sea link at speed, completely empty, the cables strobing past overhead; her scarf pulling straight out behind her.
- Cut to: The two of them stopped at the far side, talking, the sound of the sea under everything; a phone lighting up in his pocket and being ignored.
- Emotion: Freedom, then a quieter thing underneath it — being known slightly, by somebody with no stake in her.
- Lighting: Sodium strobing over both faces at speed; then still, cool, moonlit at the far end.

### [pre-chorus 2 – the stall]

```
He said, "there is a stall that stays open till morning,
Where the glasses are small and the fire never goes."
So I sat on a crate with my hands round a tumbler,
And I stopped making plans for the road leading home.
```

- Scene: The chai stall. One bulb, one burner, four crates, a wall of parked scooters.
- Shot: Macro of chai being poured from a height into a small glass, the steam catching the bulb light.
- Cut to: Her sitting on a crate with both hands wrapped round the glass, shoulders finally down.
- Emotion: Arrival, without having gone anywhere.
- Lighting: One warm bulb doing all the work; everything outside its circle in blue night.

### [chorus 2 – fuller, bansuri countermelody]

```
(as chorus 1)
```

- Scene: The same city, but she is inside it now rather than watching it.
- Shots: Night workers at the stall — a taxi driver, two delivery riders, an old man with a newspaper. The fire under the pot. Her laughing at something the stall owner says. A cat crossing the wall of parked scooters.
- Emotion: Belonging.
- Lighting: Warmer, closer, more faces in frame than any earlier chorus.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: No lyrics. Sitar, then strings and tabla, then bansuri alone.
- Shots: A slow motion pour of chai, from the pot to the glass, the whole length of the arc. The lamp curve of the promenade from a rooftop, shot as a single locked-off held frame. A monsoon shower beginning and stopping within ten seconds, everyone at the stall ignoring it. Her walking alone to the sea wall while he pays, the sitar taking the melody. The bansuri figure over the flat black water with no drums at all.
- Lighting: The rain shower is the only cool-graded moment in the sequence; everything else stays gold.

### [bridge – voice and one string pad]

```
I have lived in this city for seven long years,
And I never once heard it the way that I do.
The horns are a chorus, the waves keep the measure,
And a radio upstairs is in tune with them too.
I do not need an ending, I am here for the middle,
And the middle is the chai and the sea and you.
```

- Scene: The sea wall again, hours later, the two of them sitting apart on it, not touching.
- Shot: A locked-off wide, the whole bay behind them, both very small.
- Cut to: A window three floors up with a radio playing and a curtain moving; a horn from a distant road; a wave breaking exactly on the beat.
- Emotion: The turn. She stops wanting the night to become something and starts letting it be the thing it is.
- Lighting: The darkest, quietest grade in the film — deep blue, one warm window.

### [final chorus – up a tone, dholak, layered harmonies]

```
Midnight in Mumbai, and the city's wide awake,
Every window a lantern, every stranger a friend.
Midnight in Mumbai, and the sea will not sleep,
And I do not want a single thing here to end.
(remaining four lines as chorus 1, with "has turned into a place")
```

- Scene: The city tipping over into morning while refusing to admit it.
- Shots: Fishing boats coming in at the far end of the bay. The first bus of the day with its lights still on. A flower market being set up in the dark. Everyone from the stall dispersing, each with a hand raised. Her on the promenade with her arms out to the wind, the widest and happiest frame of the film.
- Emotion: Full, grateful, wide awake.
- Lighting: The very first grey coming into the top of the sky while the street stays gold.

### [post-chorus – call and response]

```
Awake, awake—the whole of the bay,
The lights on the water will not look away.
Awake, awake, and the morning can wait,
Because midnight in Mumbai is a place I want to stay.
```

- Scene: Four quick returns to the film's own images, each on the beat.
- Shots: The lamp curve. The kettle. The scooter mirror with the sea in it. The jasmine string on her wrist.
- Lighting: Gold, hard cuts, no fades.

### [outro – sitar, sea, voice]

```
The first ferry moves and the sky turns to pearl,
The vendors come back and the tables unfold.
I am walking home slowly with salt in my hair,
And a city behind me that never went cold.
```

- Scene: Dawn. The night shift handing the city to the day shift.
- Shots: The first ferry pulling out, a long horn across the water. The same vendors from verse one unfolding the same tables. Her walking home alone, unhurried, jasmine still on her wrist, salt in her hair. Final frame: the empty sea wall where she was sitting at the start, held, the sea moving.
- Emotion: Calm. Nothing resolved and nothing needed resolving.
- Lighting: Pearl-grey sky, the lamps switching off in sequence down the curve.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the drone rise over the lamp curve on the first *"Midnight in Mumbai"* — one shot, no context needed, instantly identifiable.
- **The pour:** slow-motion chai poured from a height into a small glass, cut to the beat under the instrumental. Copyable at any stall in the country.
- **The sea link:** the scarf pulling straight out behind her on an empty bridge at speed, with *"I laugh like a woman with nothing to conceal."*
- **The caption line:** *"The city was never the thing that was asleep."*
- **The quote:** *"I do not need an ending, I am here for the middle."*
