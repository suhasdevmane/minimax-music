# Ride or Die — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
The friends who show up. Not the ones who post about you — the ones who
answer at three in the morning, drive four hours in a bad car, sleep in a
plastic hospital chair, and never say I told you so. A friendship anthem
written like a love song, because that is what it is.

**Mood:**
Loud, warm, funny, unembarrassed. Big brass, gang vocals, a rap verse in the
middle and a bridge that stops the band and says the vow out loud.

**Message:**
"Nobody hands you a certificate for this. It is still the marriage that
raised me."

**Genre:**
Anthemic pop with punchy live drums, a brass section and a chant chorus,
with one rapped verse. Think: Lizzo x Anne-Marie x early Sara Bareilles horn
writing — a stadium chorus you can shout badly and still be right.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 120 BPM, 4/4
- Key: E flat major
- No modulation. The final chorus lifts by moving the brass up an octave and
  putting a full gang-vocal choir behind it.

**Vocal setup:**
- Singer A (female lead) throughout, solo.
- Verse two is fully rapped, fast and dry, over drums, sub-bass and a muted
  guitar with brass stabs answering every second bar.
- Backing: gang vocals doubling every chorus line, a shouted crowd layer on
  the chants, a choir on the second pre-chorus.

**Instrumentation:**
- Punchy live drum kit, big close kick and snare with a real room
- Brass: trumpets, trombone, baritone saxophone
- Bright piano, offbeats in the verses, straight chords in the choruses
- Plucked clean electric guitar, muted under the rap
- Warm electric bass dropping to a sub for the rap verse
- Claps recorded in a real room, tambourine
- Rising strings in the pre-choruses, a choir layer on the second
- Ambience: a ringing phone, a car door, an indicator, a key in a lock,
  airport tannoy, a distant crowd chanting the hook

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the three a.m. call, two words, no questions
- Verse 1: the record of it — the party, the hospital, the mattress, the
  photo on the fridge
- Pre-chorus: what kind of friend this is, in two words at a time
- Chorus: the anthem, brass and gang vocals
- Post-chorus: the chant, hands up
- Verse 2: the rap — group chat, spare key, arrivals hall, the secret kept
- Instrumental: brass shout chorus, then a clap breakdown with a crowd
- Bridge: the band stops and the vow gets said
- Final chorus: biggest, with forty years from now in it
- Outro: a three-minute voice note played twice, on a phone speaker

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – phone tone, slow kick, no groove]

```
Three in the morning and the ringtone is yours,
I'm out of the bed and I'm into my shoes.
No question, no why, no what did you do,
Just send me a pin and I'm coming to you.
Ride or die.
```

- Scene: A dark bedroom. A phone rings, face-down on a chair.
- Shots: The phone buzzing itself in a circle on a wooden chair; a hand out
  of the duvet; two feet finding trainers in the dark without turning a light
  on; a coat taken over pyjamas; a front door left on the latch.
- Emotion: No panic and no drama. Practised.
- Lighting: Streetlight through a blind, one hallway lamp, headlights
  swinging across a ceiling.

### [verse 1 – the record of it]

```
We met in a kitchen at a party neither of us knew,
I said, "I hate it here," you said, "thank God, me too."
Ten years and eleven addresses down the line,
You still know my order and I still know your sign.
```

- Scene: The origin, warm and grainy, filmed like a memory.
- Shots: Two nineteen-year-olds pressed against a kitchen counter at a
  crowded house party, both of them clearly wanting to leave; the two of them
  going out of a back door together; a fast montage of eleven front doors and
  eleven sets of keys.
- Emotion: The comedy of instant recognition.
- Lighting: Warm, over-exposed, slightly blown-out highlights.

```
You drove four hours in a car that barely ran,
Slept in a plastic hospital chair and held my hand.
You never said, "I told you so," about the guy,
You just brought up the mattress and you never asked me why.
There's a photo on your fridge of us at nineteen,
Two idiots in a garden with the whole thing still to see.
```

- Scene: The receipts.
- Shots: A tired car on a motorway at night with one headlight dimmer than
  the other; a plastic hospital chair with a coat over it and two hands
  joined on an armrest; a mattress being carried up a narrow staircase by two
  people who cannot see each other; a fridge door with one curling photograph
  under a magnet, held in close-up long enough to read the faces.
- Emotion: Gratitude with no violins under it.
- Lighting: Motorway sodium, hospital fluorescent, stairwell bulb, kitchen
  daylight. Four different light sources for four different kinds of showing
  up.

### [pre-chorus 1]

```
Ambulance friend, airport friend,
The one who reads the message that I didn't send.
If the world ends on a Wednesday afternoon,
I'm not calling anybody, I'm just calling you.
```

- Scene: Two-word portraits, cut fast.
- Shots: Blue light on a wall; an arrivals board; a typed message deleted
  before sending, screen composited; her looking at a phone and putting it
  down knowing it will ring anyway.
- Emotion: Rising certainty.
- Lighting: Cool blue into warm.

### [chorus 1 – brass and gang vocals]

```
You're my ride or die, and I'd ride for you,
Say the word and I'm outside, no questions, no excuse.
Ride or die, ride or die,
I'll be the phone call that you never have to try.
Nobody's coming, so we came for each other,
Nobody chose us, so we chose one another.
Whatever the year does, whatever it puts us through,
You're my ride or die, and I'd ride for you.
```

- Scene: Four friends in a car at night, and the years running underneath
  it.
- Shots: Four of them in one car, windows down, all singing the same wrong
  words; the driver's hand on the wheel with the indicator ticking; the back
  seat filmed from the front; the car pulling up outside a building and one
  of them already coming out of the door.
- Emotion: Full-volume joy.
- Lighting: Streetlights strobing through the car, dashboard glow, a petrol
  station forecourt.

### [post-chorus – the chant]

```
Hey, ride or die, ride or die,
Hands up if you've got one, hands up high.
Ride or die, ride or die,
That's my people, that's my ride.
```

- Scene: Four fast cuts, hands up.
- Shots: Four hands out of four car windows; a kitchen with four people
  dancing badly; a hand slapping a car roof twice; a fist against a fist.
- Lighting: Hard, bright, high contrast.

### [verse 2 – the rap]

```
Group chat got a name that we can never say out loud,
Four hundred unread and a blurry photo of a crowd.
You got a spare key, I got the same,
You know the exact wrong thing to say to make me okay.
Arrivals hall at a quarter to five,
Cardboard sign with my worst photo on the side.
I flew in a wreck and I walked out a joke,
You had gum and a coffee and you didn't make me talk.
Held my hair and my secret on the very same night,
Then buried it deeper than the reason for the flight.
You are the reason that I know I'm not too much,
You call me on my nonsense and it still feels like love.
```

- Scene: The rap verse is the fast montage of the whole friendship.
- Shots: A group chat scrolling at speed (composited); two identical spare
  keys on two different keyrings; an arrivals hall at dawn with one person
  holding a badly made cardboard sign with a printed photo taped to it; a
  hug in an arrivals hall that goes on slightly too long; a paper coffee cup
  handed over without a word; a hand gathering hair back at a kerb, filmed
  from behind and kept gentle; the two of them on a sofa the next day, never
  mentioning it.
- Emotion: Fast, funny, then quietly enormous.
- Lighting: Airport fluorescent, dawn through a terminal window, a kerbside
  streetlight, a normal sunlit living room.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
Bail money friend, bad haircut friend,
The one who says the true thing and then stays till the end.
If the world ends on a Wednesday afternoon,
I'm not calling anybody, I'm just calling you.
```

- Scene: More two-word portraits, funnier this time.
- Shots: A cash machine at night; a bathroom mirror and a genuinely bad
  home haircut being laughed at; someone saying something hard across a
  kitchen table and then not leaving.
- Emotion: Comedy and loyalty in the same frame.
- Lighting: Warm domestic.

### [chorus 2]

- Scene: The car again, and a house filling up.
- Shots: The same car at a different time of year; a kitchen with more
  people in it; the four of them singing at a table with the brass on the
  stereo.
- Emotion: Bigger, more people, same two at the centre.
- Lighting: Warmer, busier.

### [instrumental – brass shout chorus, clap breakdown]

- Scene: The friendship as a party, no lyrics.
- Shots: A brass section actually playing in somebody's front room, too big
  for it; a clap breakdown filmed as four pairs of hands in a circle; a slow
  push down a hallway lined with photographs from every year; the four of
  them on a kerb eating chips at four in the morning, feet in the gutter.
- Lighting: Bright interior, then kerbside streetlight.

### [bridge – piano only, the vow]

```
Nobody hands you a certificate for this,
No aisle, no ring, no photograph, no priest.
But if there were vows then I'd say them in the road,
I'd say them in the group chat, I'd say them in the cold.
For better, for worse, for whatever we go through,
You were never plan B, it was always me and you.
```

- Scene: The two of them, alone, and the song stops performing.
- Shots: Two people sitting on a kerb with a bag of chips between them,
  not looking at each other; a wide of an empty road with the two of them
  small in it; a hand on a shoulder held for four seconds; a phone typing
  something and then just putting it down.
- Emotion: The most sincere thirty seconds in the song. No music does the
  work; the words do.
- Lighting: One streetlight, cold blue sky beginning behind it.

### [final chorus – biggest]

- Scene: The whole crowd, and a flash-forward.
- Shots: A packed kitchen singing the hook; the brass section in the
  doorway; a two-second flash-forward to two women in their sixties in a
  garden with folding chairs and the same laugh; back to the kitchen, the two
  of them in the middle of it.
- Emotion: Full release.
- Lighting: Every light in the house on, then a bright garden, then back.

### [post-chorus 2]

- Scene: The chant, hands up, everyone.
- Shots: A room of raised hands; the car roof slapped again; the fridge
  photo, now with a second photo taped beside it.
- Lighting: Bright.

### [outro – phone speaker, a laugh]

```
Voice note at midnight, three minutes long,
Half of it laughing and the rest of it wrong.
Nothing important, and I play it twice,
That's the whole thing, that is the whole of my life.
You're my ride or die, and I'd ride for you.
```

- Scene: A quiet room, a phone on a table.
- Shots: A phone lying flat on a kitchen table playing a voice note
  (waveform composited); her listening while doing something else; her
  pressing play a second time; final shot, the phone on the table in a
  sunlit empty kitchen, the note still playing, a laugh in it, and the frame
  holding after she has walked out of it.
- Emotion: Ordinary, and the whole point.
- Lighting: Flat kind daylight.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** four hands out of four car windows on the first chant.
- **The challenge:** the arrivals-hall sign — post the worst photo of your
  friend you have ever held up at an airport, with the chant under it.
- **The caption line:** *"Nobody's coming, so we came for each other."*
- **The rap clip:** *"You know the exact wrong thing to say to make me
  okay."*
- **The quote:** *"Nobody hands you a certificate for this."*
- **The last shot:** a voice note playing to an empty kitchen, and the frame
  refusing to cut away from it.
