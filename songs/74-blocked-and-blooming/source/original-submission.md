# Blocked and Blooming — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
A woman blocks an ex in December and spends the next five months turning a small apartment into a garden. The plants are not a metaphor she reaches for — they are what she actually did with her hands while she got better. By May the balcony is a jungle and she is unrecognisable to the person who left.

**Mood:**
Warm, bouncing, quietly triumphant. Sunlight after a long cold. Joy that was earned rather than handed over, with one honest bruise in the bridge.

**Message:**
"I didn't get over you, I grew over you."

**Genre:**
Afro-pop with modern pop polish. Think: Tems x Tyla x Lianne La Havas — log drums and highlife-leaning guitar under a conversational Western pop vocal, celebratory without ever being a party record.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 108 BPM, 4/4
- Key: F major (bright, open, singable)
- The final chorus widens rather than modulates; the lift is in the arrangement and the harmony stack, not the key.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, low and unhurried in the verses, sung in the pocket rather than belted in the chorus.
- Backing vocals: stacked female harmonies in the choruses, a low chant under the second chorus, crowd-width claps and layered voices in the post-chorus.

**Instrumentation:**
- Bright clean electric guitar (the signature figure, first bar to last)
- Log drums
- Round melodic bass
- Shakers, congas, talking drum
- Warm horn-like synth stabs
- Second counter-melody guitar from chorus two
- Light marimba doubling the hook in the final chorus
- Ambience: room tone, a blind rolling up, distant street, birdsong at the end

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: a cold December room, the block button, one blind going up
- Verse 1: the first purchases, soil and pots, the first thing that stays
- Pre-chorus: the advice she was given and what she actually did with it
- Chorus: the hook, wide and physical, joy in the pocket
- Verse 2: March, the window fully open, running into his friend
- Bridge: the honest part — cereal at the sink, roots working in the dark
- Final chorus: the biggest arrangement, the door swinging open
- Outro: May, the balcony a jungle, the opening guitar figure in full sun

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – guitar, shaker, one late log-drum hit]

```
December, and my thumb was on your name,
One little button and the noise went away.
No last speech, no argument, no scene,
Just a quiet little room and a girl and a screen.
I didn't cry, I just opened a blind,
And let the cold light in for the first time.
```

- Scene: A small apartment in December, blinds down, everything grey and tidy.
- Shot: Extreme close-up of a thumb resting on a phone screen, not moving, then pressing once.
- Cut to: The room in silence, the phone face-down on a bare table, her sitting very still on the edge of the bed.
- Her reaction: No tears. A long breath, then she stands and walks to the window.
- Lighting: Flat, cold, blue-grey. The only warm thing is the strip of light when the blind goes up.

### [verse 1 – the groove settles]

```
Bag of soil on a Saturday morning,
Three flights up with the sun coming warm in.
Terracotta pots and a secondhand rail,
Basil in a mug and a mint that won't fail.
```

- Scene: A garden shop and then a stairwell.
- Shots: Her paying for a heavy bag of compost and shouldering it. Her carrying it up three flights, stopping on the landing to breathe, laughing at herself. Terracotta pots lined up on newspaper on the floor.
- Cut to: Her wedging mismatched pots onto a rusty balcony rail, a mug with basil in it on the sill.
- Emotion: Practical, slightly amused, doing something with her hands for the first time in weeks.
- Lighting: Cold winter sun, low and long through the window.

```
Cuttings in a jam jar, roots like white thread,
Something in this apartment is finally getting fed.
The neighbour's grey cat comes and sits on my chair,
First thing since December that showed up and cared.
```

- Scene: The kitchen counter and the balcony chair.
- Shots: Macro on white roots in a jam jar of water on the windowsill. Her filling a watering can at the sink. A grey cat jumping onto the one balcony chair and settling as if it lives there.
- Her reaction: She looks at the cat for a long beat, then quietly moves her cup so it can stay.
- Lighting: Warm lamp inside, cold blue outside — the first shot where the inside is warmer than the outside.

### [pre-chorus]

```
They told me give it a season, give it time,
So I gave it water and a place in the light.
Now the balcony's greener than the day that you left,
And nothing out here has asked about you yet.
```

- Scene: A time-lapse feel without literal time-lapse.
- Shots: Three quick cuts on the beat — the same pot in January, February, March, the plant a little bigger each time, the light a little higher on the wall. Her hand moving a pot from shade into sun.
- Emotion: Steadiness turning into pride.
- Lighting: Each cut a step warmer than the last.

### [chorus – wide, physical, joy in the pocket]

```
Blocked and blooming, look at me now,
Dirt on my hands and a sun in the house.
You were a winter I finally survived,
Now every window in here is alive.
Blocked and blooming, I water the light,
Everything green that I grew out of spite.
Call it a garden, call it a crown,
Blocked and blooming, look at me now.
```

- Scene: The apartment at full green, and her moving through it.
- Shots: Her dancing barefoot between pots with a watering can, water arcing in the sun. Close-up of soil under her fingernails, hands open to camera. A wide of the balcony from outside, every rail crowded with leaves and one woman in the middle of it.
- Emotion: Delight, not defiance. She is not performing for anyone.
- Lighting: Full, warm, saturated. Green and gold.

### [verse 2 – March, the window fully open]

```
March came in and I opened the whole window,
Bougainvillea leaning pink on the road below.
Learned the names from a book at the library,
Learned which ones wilt when you love them too heavy.
```

- Scene: Spring arriving.
- Shots: Both window panels pushed wide, curtains moving. Bougainvillea spilling pink over the railing and down toward the street. Her cross-legged on the floor with a library book open, mouthing a long Latin name.
- Cut to: A yellowing leaf being lifted off a plant, her frowning at a saucer of standing water and tipping it out.
- Emotion: Learning something, and quietly hearing the second meaning of it.
- Lighting: Bright spring daylight, high contrast, real.

```
Ran into your friend by the fruit stand on Sunday,
Said that you'd been asking how I was doing lately.
I had dirt on my knuckles and a fig on my hip,
Both hands were full and I liked it like this.
```

- Scene: A Sunday street market.
- Shots: Her at a fruit stall, a young fig tree in a pot balanced on her hip. A faceless young man stopping in front of her, talking, gesturing. Close-up of her knuckles on the pot, dirt in the creases.
- Her reaction: Polite, warm, entirely unbothered. She shifts the fig tree, says something short, and walks on before he has finished.
- Lighting: Flat bright market daylight, colour everywhere.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
They told me give it a season, give it time,
So I gave it water and a place in the light.
Now the whole place smells like something that grew,
And not one single leaf in here is for you.
```

- Scene: Back in the apartment, arms full.
- Shots: Her carrying the fig tree in through the door. Crushed mint between her fingers, held up near the camera. A slow pan across the room: every surface has something growing on it.
- Emotion: Ownership. This is hers.
- Lighting: Late afternoon gold.

### [chorus]

(repeat)

- Scene: Repeat of the chorus world, fuller and busier.
- Shots: Her watering with the window open and the street sound coming in. The grey cat asleep in a sunbeam among the pots. Her feet on the tile between pots, dancing on the beat.
- Emotion: Ease.
- Lighting: Brighter and more saturated than chorus one.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: A wordless growing montage, the only stretch with no vocal.
- Shots: Macro of a seedling breaking the surface of soil. Water going into dry earth and darkening it. Her repotting something too big for its pot, both hands deep in compost. Her asleep on the sofa in daylight with a book on her chest. A wide of the balcony at dusk, the pots silhouetted, one string light on.
- Lighting: Golden through to blue hour, warm to cool and back.

### [bridge – guitar, soft bass, voice, almost spoken]

```
I thought the block button was the end of the story,
Turns out it was only the ground cracking open.
Some love is a drought that you walk away thirsty,
Some love is the rain that you're owed and get late.
I won't pretend that I did all of it pretty,
There were weeks I ate cereal standing at the sink.
But under the dirt the small roots kept working,
Quiet as anything, further than you think.
```

- Scene: The honest part. Night, one lamp.
- Shots: Her sitting on the kitchen counter eating cereal from the box, lit by the open fridge. A close-up of her face, tired, no makeup, not sad exactly — flat. Cut to: a pot on the windowsill in the dark, and a cross-section illustration feel — soil in a glass-sided pot with roots visibly threading through it.
- Emotion: Honesty. The one place the song admits how long it took.
- Lighting: Single warm lamp, deep shadow, the fridge light as a second source.

### [final chorus – biggest arrangement, door swinging open]

```
Blocked and blooming, look at me now,
Green on the railing and the door swinging out.
You were a winter I finally survived,
Now every window in here is alive.
Blocked and blooming, I water the light,
Everything golden that I grew out of spite.
Call it a garden, call it a crown,
Blocked and blooming, look at me now.
```

- Scene: Full daylight, the apartment door open to the hallway, friends arriving.
- Shots: Two friends coming in with more pots, laughing. Her handing one of them a cutting in a jam jar to take home. A wide from the hallway looking in: green, light, people.
- Emotion: Abundance. It has outgrown her and she is giving it away.
- Lighting: The brightest of the video, doors and windows all open.

### [post-chorus – chant, claps]

```
Look at me now, look at me now,
Feet on the tile and the speaker turned loud.
Look at me now, look at me now,
Everything I planted in the cold came round.
Look at me now, look at me now,
Blocked and blooming and I'm never coming down.
```

- Scene: A small kitchen party.
- Shots: Bare feet on tile between pots, moving on the beat. A speaker on the counter. Hands clapping. Her spinning once with her arms out, hitting a hanging plant, laughing.
- Emotion: Joy, unguarded.
- Lighting: Warm evening, string lights on the balcony now lit.

### [outro – opening guitar figure, full sun]

```
December, and my thumb was on your name,
That one little button and the noise went away.
Now it's May and the balcony's a jungle in the sun,
Blocked and blooming, and the growing's just begun.
```

- Scene: May morning, the same window as the intro.
- Shots: The same frame as shot two — the blind, the window — now blazing with green and light. Her hand going up to the same blind, this time to open it wider. Final shot: the balcony from outside, overgrown, the cat on the chair, her stepping out with a coffee.
- Emotion: Peace, then a small private grin.
- Lighting: Clean May morning, warm, no filter.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** her hands held open to camera with soil in every crease, on *"dirt on my hands and a sun in the house."*
- **The knife line:** *"Both hands were full and I liked it like this."*
- **The caption line:** *"Everything green that I grew out of spite."*
- **The challenge:** the December-to-May split — post the same window on the day you blocked someone and again five months later, cut on the hook. The grey neighbour cat, in the first verse and again in the last shot, is the small running joke people will look for on a rewatch.
- **The quote:** *"I didn't get over you, I grew over you."*
