# Wrong Number — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:** He texts after six months, at ten to one in the morning, opening with a compliment. She has moved city, moved flat and changed her number, and she answers exactly the way a stranger would: sorry, wrong number. Not a revenge song and not a wounded one — a funny, hard, completely unbothered record about being genuinely unreachable.

**Mood:** Sharp, dry, northern, funny. Confidence without cruelty. Built to be shouted in a taxi on the way to a night out.

**Message:** "The girl you're texting moved out in the spring, and she left no forwarding address."

**Genre:** UK pop-rap with live brass and trap drums. Think: Little Simz x RAYE x Jorja Smith — rapped verses with heavy internal rhyme, a fully sung chorus, and a chant that a room can shout back.

**Tempo & Key:** 120 BPM, 4/4, G minor. No modulation; the final chorus grows by the full horn section, group vocals and doubled drums. Uptempo pacing class, so the lyric carries the larger word budget and `render.json` sets the length guard at one hundred and forty words per minute.

**Vocal setup:** Lead: young adult female. Both verses are pop-rap — fast, conversational, British, consonants forward, landing the last word of every bar with the brass. The choruses drop the rap completely and are sung wide and melodic. The post-choruses are a crowd chant. The bridge starts sung and soft and hardens back into spoken-edged delivery.

**Instrumentation:**
- Live brass section (the signature): muted in the intro, punching the rap bars, fat under the choruses, one trumpet in the outro
- Heavy sliding sub-bass, eight-oh-eight kicks, rattling trap hi-hats, claps and a layered snare on the choruses; warm electric piano alone for the first half of the bridge; group and crowd vocals on the chants and final chorus
- Ambient: a phone buzzing on a hard surface, a tram rail hum, doors closing, a busy street, a phone lock sound

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the text arrives on a tram at ten to one; verse one, the reply and an inventory of everything that has changed
- Pre-chorus: sending it and not checking the phone again; chorus, the hook sung wide; post-chorus, the chant
- Verse two: his follow-ups, the flatmate, the phone shop, the new terms; bridge: one honest soft second for the good version of him, then the door; final chorus, full horns and group vocals
- Outro: the new flat, the new number, and who is allowed to ring it

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro — no drums, phone buzz, tram hum, one muted brass note]

```
Six months of nothing, then your name on my screen at ten to one,
Same three dots you always did when you were working out a lie.
I let it sit a minute while the tram went through the dark,
Then I typed three small words and put the whole thing by.
```

- Scene: A late tram, almost empty, going through a dark cutting. She is the only person in the carriage who is awake.
- Shot: A phone face-up on her knee, screen lighting her chin from below, three dots appearing and disappearing (composited).
- Cut to: Her face doing almost nothing; her thumb typing three short words; the phone going into her coat pocket.
- Emotion: Recognition, then boredom. The whole song's attitude is set in this shot.
- Lighting: Tram interior white-green, black windows, her face the only warm thing in it.

### [verse 1 — full trap beat, brass punching the bars]

```
You said long time, hope you're keeping well,
Like half a year of silence was a holiday you spent.
I read it on the tram, coming into Piccadilly,
With my own bags in my hands and my own name on the rent.
You got the number off a mate who got it off a mate,
And you opened with a compliment, which is peak, and it's late.
I've moved, I've changed the locks, I've changed the postcode too,
There's a chippy on my corner and it knows my name, not you.
You want the girl who sat up waiting on a maybe and a might,
She got made redundant, mate, they shut it down one night.
```

- Scene: The tram arriving into Piccadilly, and her walking out into the city that is now hers.
- Shot: Her performing straight to camera from a tram seat, then from an escalator, then from a station concourse — one bar per location, cut hard on the brass.
- Cut to: The chippy on her corner with the man behind the counter greeting her by name; a new key going into a new lock; her own name on a tenancy agreement (composited).
- Emotion: Delivery, not anger. She is enjoying herself.
- Lighting: Station sodium and neon, the chippy's fluorescent, all of it warm and specific.

### [pre-chorus 1 — hats and claps, bass filtering up]

```
So I typed it out slow with a smile on my face,
Read it twice, sent it, put the phone away.
The tram went under the bridge and the signal cut,
And I never checked it once for the rest of the day.
```

- Scene: The send, and the not-caring afterwards.
- Shot: Macro of a thumb hovering, then sending. The message going up the screen (composited, and gone before it can be read).
- Cut to: The tram going under a bridge and the signal bars dropping to nothing; the phone going into a bag and staying there.
- Emotion: The pleasure of a decision already made.
- Lighting: Dark tunnel, then the city coming back all at once.

### [chorus 1 — sung, full band, layered horns]

```
Sorry, wrong number, she doesn't live here anymore,
New city, new keys, new name on the door.
The girl you're texting, mate, moved out in the spring,
She left no forwarding address for anything.
Sorry, wrong number, this is not who you think,
She doesn't wait up and she doesn't lose a wink.
Sorry, wrong number, she doesn't live here anymore,
Try somebody else, like you did before.
```

- Scene: The new flat, mid-move, boxes half unpacked and the good light already found.
- Shot: Her walking backwards through the flat ahead of the camera, arms out, the whole place hers.
- Cut to: A new key on a new fob; a name on a buzzer panel; a window with a northern skyline in it.
- Emotion: Big, sung, delighted.
- Lighting: The brightest interior in the video, late afternoon, everything gold.

### [post-chorus — crowd chant, brass stabs]

```
Wrong number, wrong number, wrong girl, wrong year,
Wrong number, wrong number, she isn't here.
Wrong number, wrong number, wrong girl, wrong year,
Sorry, wrong number, she isn't here.
```

- Scene: Four girls in a kitchen, shouting the chant into a mirror and a hairbrush.
- Shot: Hard rhythmic cuts, one per line, all of them on the beat, all of them handheld and slightly too close.
- Cut to: A single wide of the four of them lined up against the kitchen units.
- Emotion: A group, not a woman alone. This is the shot the video is sold on.
- Lighting: Kitchen overheads and a ring light someone has propped on a bread bin.

### [verse 2 — bass and hats only, rebuilding]

```
Three dots, then a laugh, then a who's this, are you serious,
Then a paragraph at two in the morning, and it's furious.
I read it with my coffee and my flatmate on the Sunday,
And we laughed like it was telly and I binned it before Monday.
Went down to the phone shop on a Saturday in spring,
Got a number on a receipt and it felt like a new thing.
Same face, same laugh, same ring in the same nose,
Same girl, brand new terms, and the first term is, no.
I'm not angry and I'm not owed and I'm not keeping score,
I just don't answer numbers that I deleted before.
```

- Scene: The escalation, and how little it lands.
- Shot: The phone on a kitchen table between two coffees, buzzing repeatedly while two women ignore it and keep talking.
- Cut to: A phone shop on a Saturday — a ticket number, a plastic chair, a new SIM, a receipt with a number on it held up like a certificate.
- Emotion: Amusement. The follow-ups are content, not events.
- Lighting: Sunday kitchen daylight, then phone-shop retail white.

### [pre-chorus 2 — brighter, busier]

```
Typed it out again with the very same grin,
Three small words, and I let them do the rest.
The tram came over the bridge with the whole town lit,
And I went to meet the girls in the loud red dress.
```

- Scene: The second send, and the night beginning.
- Shot: The same thumb, the same three words, the same absence of drama.
- Cut to: The tram coming over a bridge with the city lit below it; her getting off in a loud red dress with the doors closing behind her.
- Emotion: Momentum.
- Lighting: Blue hour going to neon.

### [chorus 2 — octave vocals, horn counter melody]

```

Sorry, wrong number, this is not who you think,
```

- Scene: Out. A bar, a booth, a dance floor, all four of them.
- Shot: Tracking through a busy room behind her, people she knows greeting her the whole way.
- Cut to: The phone face-down on a sticky table with drinks around it, buzzing, and nobody looking at it.
- Emotion: The song at full size, in a room full of people.
- Lighting: Red and amber bar light, hard shadows, movement everywhere.

### [instrumental — brass and drums trading, beat switch, one bar of tram hum]

- Scene: The night at its best, and one held breath.
- Shot: A brass-led dance sequence — the four of them, no choreography, the real thing, cut on the horn phrases.
- Cut to: Her outside in the cold with a coat over her shoulders, laughing at something off-camera; the door of the bar swinging and cutting the music in and out.
- Final beat: The hard beat switch drops to one half-time bar of tram hum on a static shot of the empty tram platform at night.
- Lighting: Bar heat, then cold blue street, then the empty platform.

### [bridge — electric piano, soft, then hard]

```
For a second I remembered the good version of you,
The one that made me laugh in a kitchen in the rain.
Then I put my coat on and I got the tram again.
It isn't that I hate you, it's that I'm not free labour,
I did the whole rebuild and you want the finished flat.
The girl who lived here loved you and she paid for it in years,
And she's happy, and she's gone, and you cannot have her back.
```

- Scene: One honest soft second, and then the argument.
- Shot: Her alone on the last tram, the memory arriving as a two-second warm flash of a kitchen in the rain, his face out of frame.
- Cut to: Her face going level again; then straight to camera for the last four lines, brass returning behind her.
- Emotion: The only vulnerable moment in the record, and it lasts exactly two lines.
- Lighting: Tram window reflections, then a hard clean light on her for the delivery.

### [final chorus — full horns, group vocals, doubled drums]

```
Sorry, wrong number, she doesn't live here anymore,
New city, new keys, new name on the door.
The girl you're texting, mate, moved out in the spring,
She left no forwarding address for anything.
Sorry, wrong number, this is not who you think,
She's out with her girls and she's on her second drink.
Sorry, wrong number, she doesn't live here anymore,
And I'm not sorry, and I'm not sorry, and I'm sure.
```

- Scene: All of it at once — the flat, the street, the bar, the tram.
- Shot: The widest shots of the video, the four of them walking a street abreast, brass on the soundtrack and a city behind them.
- Cut to: Her stopping and delivering the last line to camera with the others carrying on ahead of her.
- Emotion: Total release, and the smallest amount of steel under it.
- Lighting: Neon and streetlight, saturated, the most colour in the piece.

### [post-chorus — crowd chant, shouted]

```
Wrong number, wrong number, wrong girl, wrong year,
Wrong number, wrong number, she isn't here.
Wrong number, wrong number, wrong girl, wrong year,
Sorry, wrong number, she isn't here.
```

- Scene: The chant, but outside and bigger.
- Shot: The four of them shouting it on a street corner while a night bus goes past behind them.
- Cut to: Hard cuts of feet, hands, a chip carton, a taxi door.
- Emotion: Joy, loud.
- Lighting: Bus headlights and takeaway signage.

### [outro — bass, one trumpet, one voice]

```
New flat, new key, new light in the hall,
New number nobody's got but the people that I call.
If it rings after midnight it's my sister or my mate,
And whoever you're texting, she left. It's too late.
```

- Scene: Home, alone, and pleased about it.
- Shot: Her letting herself into the new flat, shoes off in the hall, the hall light on.
- Cut to: The phone on the counter, screen dark, and her not touching it.
- Final frame: The flat's window with the northern skyline in it, the phone locking with a small sound, held. No text.
- Emotion: Settled, unhurried, entirely done.
