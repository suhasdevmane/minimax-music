# Video Call Slow Dance — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
Two people in two time zones have run out of ways to be close, so they invent one. Same song queued on both machines, press play on 3, pick the laptop up and hold it against your chest, and slow dance in two separate bedrooms at the same time. It is ridiculous and they know it. It is also the closest thing they have, and by the end of the song it turns out to be enough: the picture freezes, she keeps swaying anyway, and when he comes back he never stopped either.

**Mood:**
Warm, intimate, unhurried. Long-distance ache without self-pity. Two lamps, two rooms, one groove. The romance is in the effort: she does her hair for a camera, he moves the lamp, both of them are trying.

**Message:**
"Video call slow dance — hold your screen like it's me."

**Genre:**
Modern R&B / slow-jam pop duet. Think: Daniel Caesar x H.E.R. x Giveon warmth, keys and snaps rather than drums, stacked harmonies doing the emotional work.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 94 BPM, 4/4
- Key: A♭ major (warm, round, easy on both voices)
- No key change; the final chorus lifts through the fullest harmony stack, a small string section and a brighter top-line, not a modulation.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal (Singer A): young adult female, playful and close in verse 1, open and warm in the choruses, near-spoken at the top of the bridge.
- Singer B: male, smooth R&B tenor with a soft grain low down; verse 2 is his alone and should sound like a confession made at midnight.
- Both: the pre-choruses alternate line by line, every chorus is close two-part harmony with her on top, the post-chorus is a layered chant, the outro trades goodnights.

**Instrumentation:**
- Warm electric piano (the spine, first bar to last)
- Deep round bass and a soft 808 sub
- Finger snaps on two and four; brushed kick, light rimshot, soft clap layer
- Mellow clean electric guitar (answers the vocal in chorus 2, leads the instrumental)
- Soft synth pads; a small string section from the bridge on
- Stacked backing vocals from both singers on every chorus
- Ambient textures: room tone from two different rooms, the chime of a call connecting, a laptop fan, a digital stutter, a buffering glitch, the soft tone of a call ending

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: two clocks, two lamps, press play on 3
- Verse 1 (her): getting ready for a date that happens through a laptop
- Pre-chorus (both): the count-in, the mirroring, lift the screen
- Chorus (both): the hook, wide and slow, hold the screen like it's me
- Verse 2 (him): the stack of books, the red pen on the calendar, the hand flat on the glass
- Instrumental: the guitar takes the hook, then the picture freezes
- Bridge: the freeze, and the discovery that he never stopped dancing either
- Final chorus: fullest harmonies, the countdown line replaces the buffering line
- Post-chorus: hold it like it's me, till the gate
- Outro: 3% battery, goodnight said twice, still dancing after the call ends

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – electric piano, pad, room tone, a call connecting]

```
9 your time and midnight mine,
Same song queued up, we press play on 3.
Pick the laptop up and hold it to your chest,
Tonight the distance don't get to me.
Two lamps, two rooms, one slow groove,
Baby, don't blink, and don't you move.
```

- Scene: A vertical split screen. Left: her bedroom, midnight, curtains closed, one lamp. Right: his bedroom, nine in the evening, blue still in the window.
- Shot: Two clock faces, one per side, composited. Then two hands hitting the space bar at the same instant.
- Cut to: Him lifting his laptop off the desk and pressing it flat against his chest, the screen light on his jaw.
- Her reaction: A small laugh she doesn't let him see, then she does the same.
- Lighting: One warm practical lamp per room, nothing else. The two rooms are deliberately different temperatures — hers amber, his cooler.

### [verse 1 – her]

```
I put my hair up like it's a real date,
Lipstick for a camera, that's how far gone I am.
Moved the lamp so the light hits right,
Cleared the laundry off the bed like you'd notice, damn.
```

- Scene: Her room, twenty minutes earlier. The getting-ready sequence, played straight, not as a joke.
- Shot: Close-up of her twisting her hair up and pinning it; lipstick in a hand mirror.
- Cut to: Her dragging the lamp across the floor by its cord, checking the result on the laptop preview, dragging it back six inches.
- Her reaction: A fast sweep of laundry off the bed into a basket, then a beat where she looks at the empty bed.
- Lighting: The lamp move is visible in-shot — the light on her face changes as she moves it.

```
You're in that grey shirt with the collar bent,
Ceiling fan turning slow behind your head.
2,000 miles of signal and delay,
And you freeze on the frame where you're smiling my way.
```

- Scene: What she sees on her screen: him, framed slightly too low, the fan turning behind him.
- Shot: A screen-within-screen, her face reflected faintly in her own monitor glass.
- Cut to: Macro on his bent collar; the fan blades; then the picture stuttering and holding on his smile.
- Her reaction: She reaches out and touches the frozen frame with one finger before it unfreezes.
- Lighting: Screen glow as the key light on her for the first time.

### [pre-chorus – both, alternating lines]

```
So put the laptop on your palm, lift it slow,
Count me in, 1, 2, here we go.
Sway to the left when I sway to the right,
Mirror me, baby, we can get it right tonight.
```

- Scene: The set-up for the dance. Both of them balancing a laptop on one flat palm like a tray.
- Shot: Matched pair of shots, one per side of the split, both from the same low angle.
- Cut to: Her mouth counting; his feet stepping on the count; the split-screen line down the middle of the frame for the first time feeling like a wall.
- Emotion: Half laughing at themselves, half serious.
- Lighting: Both rooms dim slightly as they reach off-frame to their lamps.

### [chorus – both, wide and warm]

```
Video call slow dance, hold your screen like it's me,
Two bedrooms, one song, closest we can be.
Turn down the lights, I'll turn up the sound,
Spin me on the carpet, I'll spin you around.
Video call slow dance, cheek against the glass,
Buffering forever, I don't need it fast.
If a signal is all we get tonight,
Hold your screen like it's me, and hold it tight.
```

- Scene: The dance proper. Two people slow dancing alone, each holding a laptop, in perfect time with each other.
- Shot: The split screen starts to behave like one room — when she turns left he turns left, and the two halves line up so their hands meet across the divide.
- Cut to: Her cheek resting against the screen edge; his palm flat on his own screen; a slow spin on carpet in socks, hers clockwise, his anticlockwise so the split reads as one turn.
- Emotion: Silly, tender, completely sincere.
- Lighting: Both rooms now lit only by their laptops. This is the first time the two halves match colour.

### [verse 2 – him]

```
I put the laptop on a stack of books
So your eyes line up with mine and stay.
Marked the calendar with a red pen, look,
22 more squares and I'm on my way.
```

- Scene: His room, earlier. The engineering of the shot: three books, then four, then three again.
- Shot: Close-up of his hands stacking hardbacks under the laptop, checking the preview, removing one.
- Cut to: A wall calendar, a red pen, a hand crossing off today's square. Pull back: a block of crossed squares and a circled date.
- His reaction: He taps the circled date twice with the pen cap, then turns the calendar toward the camera.
- Lighting: Desk lamp, cool window light dying behind him.

```
You do that thing where you say you're fine,
Then you look off frame and your voice goes thin.
So I press my hand up flat to the screen,
Put yours on mine, baby, let me in.
```

- Scene: The moment the call stops being fun for a second.
- Shot: Her on his screen, saying she's fine, then her eyes going off to the left of frame.
- Cut to: His hand rising into his own shot and pressing flat against the monitor; then, on her side, her hand rising to meet it, the two palms aligned exactly across the split-screen line.
- Emotion: The nearest thing to touching that they have.
- Lighting: The screen glow through the gaps between his fingers.

### [pre-chorus – both]

```
So put the laptop on your palm, lift it slow,
Count me in, 1, 2, here we go.
Sway to the left when I sway to the right,
Mirror me, baby, we're dancing tonight.
```

- Scene: The second set-up, faster and more confident. No laughing this time.
- Shot: Both of them already standing when the section starts, laptops already up.
- Cut to: The counting mouths again, but now in sync to the frame.
- Lighting: Warmer both sides; the temperature difference between the two rooms is closing.

### [chorus]

(repeat)

- Scene: The dance again, wider and looser. He knocks a lamp with his elbow; she nearly drops the laptop and catches it.
- Shot: More movement, more floor. The split line drifts off-centre for the first time, giving whichever of them is moving more the bigger half.
- Cut to: A guitar-line moment where both of them mouth the hook at each other rather than sing it.
- Emotion: Comfortable. This is a thing they do now.
- Lighting: Fully matched; both rooms are the same amber.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: The guitar takes the hook; no lyrics.
- Shots: The two of them dancing in wide, each alone in a room that suddenly looks large. Her socks on floorboards. His shadow on a bare wall. A slow push in on each laptop from behind, so we see the back of the screen and the person beyond it. Then the picture stutters, blocks up, and the sound drops out entirely.
- Lighting: Warm to start, then the freeze drains one side to a flat blue.

### [bridge – electric piano, her alone, then both]

```
Then the picture froze with your hand on your heart,
That little wheel spinning, my screen went dark.
I could've hung up, could've called it a night,
But I kept on swaying in the lamplight.
```

- Scene: Her side only. His half of the frame is a frozen, blocky still with a spinning wheel composited over it.
- Shot: Static wide of her room. She stops. Looks at the frozen frame. Waits.
- Cut to: Her hand on the trackpad, hovering over the end-call button, not pressing it. Then her feet, starting to sway again with nothing on the other side of the screen.
- Emotion: The whole song's argument in one shot: she dances to a frozen picture.
- Lighting: Cold blue from the frozen frame, warm from her lamp, the two colours meeting on her face.

```
And when you came back you were laughing at me,
Cause you never stopped either, out of time, out of key.
So it's not the signal, and it's not the screen,
It's that you keep dancing when you can't see me.
```

- Scene: The connection returns mid-movement — he is already dancing, badly, half a bar behind.
- Shot: The frozen frame snapping back to live motion on a beat.
- Cut to: Both of them laughing; a quick two-shot where the split line is barely visible.
- Emotion: Relief that turns into something much bigger.
- Lighting: Both sides warm; the strings enter and the rooms brighten together.

### [final chorus – fullest harmonies, strings, the countdown line]

```
Video call slow dance, hold your screen like it's me,
Two bedrooms, one song, closest we can be.
Turn down the lights, I'll turn up the sound,
Spin me on the carpet, I'll spin you around.
Video call slow dance, cheek against the glass,
22 more squares and this screen is the past.
If a signal is all we get tonight,
Hold your screen like it's me, and hold it tight.
```

- Scene: The biggest version of the dance, and the split screen begins to close.
- Shot: The dividing line moves — not a wipe, a physical narrowing, until the two rooms are two halves of one frame with no gap.
- Cut to: The calendar again on his side, the circled date; her red pen crossing a square on her own calendar, which we have not seen until now — she has been counting too.
- Emotion: Not sadness. A countdown.
- Lighting: The fullest, warmest light of the video; both lamps plus practical string lights that were always there.

### [post-chorus]

```
Hold it like it's me, hold it like it's me,
Till the airport, till the gate, till it's really me.
Hold it like it's me, hold it like it's me,
Same song, same sway, till it's really me.
```

- Scene: Four fast cuts that break the split screen for the first time: an airport gate sign (composited), an empty arrivals rail, a suitcase by a door, a hand on a boarding pass.
- Shot: Each one 1–1.5 s, cut on the repeat of the line.
- Emotion: The future arriving in the middle of the present.
- Lighting: Airport shots deliberately harsh and public after eleven bedroom-lit minutes.

### [outro – electric piano, two voices trading goodnights]

```
9 your time and midnight mine,
Battery blinking, 3%.
Say goodnight slow, then say it again,
I'll still be dancing when the call has ended.
22 squares, then no more screen,
Till then, baby, hold it like it's me.
```

- Scene: The end of the call. The low-battery warning on her machine.
- Shot: The battery indicator (composited), her ignoring it. Two goodnights, his and hers, overlapping.
- Cut to: The screen going black. Her room lit only by the lamp again. She keeps swaying, alone, in silence.
- Final shot: The split screen closes completely and holds on one frame — her room, one lamp, one person still dancing. No text.
- Lighting: Lamp only, warm, falling off to black at the edges.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the laptop held flat against the chest, both of them at once, *"hold your screen like it's me"* on screen.
- **The challenge:** long-distance couples queue the same song, press play on three, and post the split screen; the palms meeting across the divide is the frame everyone will copy.
- **The caption line:** *"Two bedrooms, one song, closest we can be."*
- **The turn:** *"It's that you keep dancing when you can't see me."*
- **The countdown:** the red pen and the crossed squares — a ready-made format for anyone counting down to a flight.
- **The last frame:** the split screen closing to one room, one lamp, one person still swaying.
