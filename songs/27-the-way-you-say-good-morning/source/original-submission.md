# The Way You Say Good Morning — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
A slow, sensual R&B ode to first light. Not to sex and not to romance in the abstract — to two specific words said badly, in a wrecked half-asleep voice, before either person is really awake. The whole song holds still inside about ninety seconds of a real morning and refuses to let the day start.

**Mood:**
Warm, unhurried, close-miked, adult. Sensual but entirely tasteful — heat comes from the sound of a voice and the weight of an arm, never from anything explicit.

**Message:**
"Everything out there can wait. Say it again."

**Genre:**
Slow modern R&B. Think: SZA x Cleo Sol x H.E.R. — finger snaps instead of a snare, electric piano, deep harmony stacks, a groove that sits behind the beat on purpose.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 92 BPM, 4/4
- Key: D♭ major, warm and slightly veiled, no modulation
- The lift comes from harmony stacks and width, never from volume

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: female, close to the microphone, breath left in, almost whispered in the intro
- Backing vocals: full stacks under the chorus, an octave-up harmony added in the second, a low sung counter-line in the final
- The bridge is voice and electric piano alone with all harmonies removed

**Instrumentation:**
- Warm electric piano (the spine, first chord to last)
- Finger snaps carrying the whole rhythm in place of a snare
- Deep round sub-bass from verse one; a soft muted kick on the one
- Silky synth lines answering the vocal in the choruses; muted electric guitar in the break
- Cinematic strings only for the rebuild out of the bridge and the final chorus
- Ambience: ceiling fan, room tone, a street waking up, a phone buzzing on wood, vinyl crackle

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: ten to seven, blinds, a fan, two words
- Verse 1: his voice, his eyes still shut, the list of things she meant to be
- Pre-chorus: asking him to say it again before the room gets older
- Chorus: the day losing the argument
- Verse 2: the world pulling at them — a meeting, a phone, cold coffee — and losing
- Instrumental: the room without either of them talking
- Bridge: she used to hate mornings, and the turn that ended that
- Final chorus: forty years of mornings, widest arrangement
- Post-chorus: the two words alone
- Outro: one piano chord, one voice, a held breath

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – pad, one piano chord, no drums]

```
Ten to seven and the room goes gray to gold,
Blinds laying stripes across the both of us.
The fan is turning slow, your arm across my back,
You say two words and I lose the whole day to that.
```

- Scene: A bedroom at ten to seven, the very first minute of usable light.
- Shot: Locked-off wide from the foot of the bed, the room still mostly blue, sunrise beginning to warm the top of the frame.
- Cut to: A ceiling fan turning slowly against the ceiling; then blinds letting light through in hard stripes across a shoulder and a forearm.
- Emotion: Half-asleep contentment. Nobody is performing anything.
- Lighting: Gray-blue turning gold over the length of the section, motivated entirely by the window.

### [verse 1 – his voice, her list]

```
There's a strip of light across the ceiling and your jaw,
And your voice comes out like it's been dragged across a floor.
You say it into my shoulder with your eyes still shut,
Like the day is a rumor that you haven't heard about.
I have got a whole list of things I meant to be,
And not one of them is stronger than you saying it to me.
```

- Scene: Him talking with his eyes closed; her fully awake and listening to the sound rather than the words.
- Shot: A hard stripe of light lying across the ceiling and continuing across his jaw; extreme close-up on his mouth and beard, his eyes never opening.
- Cut to: Her eyes open in the half-dark, blinking once, entirely still; then a corkboard or a phone notes list glimpsed out of focus on the nightstand.
- Her reaction / Emotion: Amusement first, then something much softer she does not say out loud.
- Lighting: Single hard window source, deep shadow everywhere it does not reach. Skin texture visible, no diffusion.

### [pre-chorus – asking for it again]

```
So say it slow, say it into the pillow,
Say it before the room gets any older.
Two words, no rush, don't clear your throat,
Say it like you mean the whole day that you wrote.
```

- Scene: Her asking him to repeat it, without opening her own eyes now.
- Shot: Both faces on one pillow, framed tight, neither looking at the other.
- Cut to: The light on the wall visibly moving up one stripe as the sun climbs.
- Emotion: Playfulness with a low, real current under it.
- Lighting: Gold now dominant, blue only in the corners of the room.

### [chorus 1 – the day loses]

```
The way you say good morning makes me wanna stay in bed,
Half a word, all gravel, and it goes right through my head.
You haven't opened both eyes and you've already won,
Say it one more time and the day can come undone.
The way you say good morning, low and slow and rough,
Sunday doesn't start until you say enough.
Pull the blinds, let the light come down in lines,
The way you say good morning makes the morning mine.
```

- Scene: The bed as the only place in the world, filmed slow.
- Shot: A slow lateral dolly along the length of the bed, sheets and light and two people passing the lens in one unbroken move.
- Cut to: A hand pulling the blind cord and the stripes of light widening across the bed all at once.
- Emotion: Wide-open warmth. Sensual, unhurried, never explicit — everything stays above the shoulder or is shot as texture.
- Lighting: Full gold through the blinds, dust visible in the beams, one bounce card for the faces.

### [verse 2 – the world pulls, and loses]

```
There's a meeting at nine and a car that needs the shop,
And a phone on the nightstand that has buzzed and will not stop.
Your hand finds the small of my back like it knows the way,
And the outside world can take a number and wait.
Coffee went cold on the counter, that's twice this week,
And I'd let it go cold again for the sound of you half asleep.
```

- Scene: Everything outside the bedroom asking for attention and being refused.
- Shot: The phone on the nightstand buzzing face-up and going dark, buzzing again; a car on a driveway with a flat tire, seen through the window, out of focus.
- Cut to: A full mug of coffee going cold on a kitchen counter with nobody in the room; then his hand at the small of her back, close, deliberate, and her decision written on her face.
- Emotion: A small, delighted refusal.
- Lighting: Fully warm now. The kitchen shot is the only frame in the video without a person in it.

### [pre-chorus 2 – same words, closer]

```
So say it slow, say it into the pillow,
Say it before the room gets any older.
Two words, no rush, don't clear your throat,
Say it like you mean the whole day that you wrote.
```

- Scene: The same request, an hour later and much less sleepy.
- Shot: Repeat the pillow two-shot in identical framing; this time her eyes are open and on him.
- Cut to: The stripes of light now high on the wall, almost off the bed.
- Emotion: Deliberate, awake, choosing it.
- Lighting: Higher, harder sun; the stripes are thinner and brighter than before.

### [chorus 2 – wider]

```
The way you say good morning makes me wanna stay in bed,
Half a word, all gravel, and it goes right through my head.
You haven't opened both eyes and you've already won,
Say it one more time and the day can come undone.
The way you say good morning, low and slow and rough,
Sunday doesn't start until you say enough.
Pull the blinds, let the light come down in lines,
The way you say good morning makes the morning mine.
```

- Scene: The same room, more air in it, both fully awake.
- Shot: Overhead from directly above the bed, both on their backs, sheets and light making the composition.
- Cut to: Slow-motion of a sheet lifting and settling; her laughing at something he said that the audience does not hear.
- Emotion: Ease. The heat of the first chorus turned into comfort.
- Lighting: Gold and white, wide dynamic range, blown highlights allowed at the window.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: No lyrics. The room without anybody talking in it.
- Shots: The fan; dust in the light beams; two mugs on a nightstand, one full and one empty; his shirt over the back of a chair; her hand flat on the sheet with his fingers arriving on top of it; the blinds, the light on the wall a full hand-width higher than the opening frame.
- Emotion: Time passing pleasantly and nobody minding.
- Lighting: Pure window light, no fill, deliberately simple.

### [bridge – piano and voice, no drums]

```
I used to hate the morning, set six alarms in a row,
Wake up already tired with nowhere good to go.
Then a Sunday in the spring and a voice in the dark,
And the first thing in the world stopped being the worst part.
Now I wake before the sun just to be early to you,
And I lie here in the quiet till you move.
```

- Scene: The turn. Who she was before this, in three cold flashes.
- Shot: Cold inserts — six alarms stacked on an old phone screen (composited); a bare mattress in a different, colder apartment; her sitting on the edge of a bed with her head down, fully dressed, at six in the morning.
- Cut to: Hard back to the warm room: her awake before him, in the dark, watching the window for the light to start.
- Emotion: The only sadness in the song, held for eight bars and then let go.
- Lighting: The inserts are cold, gray and evenly lit. The return is warm and directional. The contrast carries the whole bridge.

### [final chorus – strings, widest]

```
The way you say good morning makes me wanna stay in bed,
Half a word, all gravel, and it goes right through my head.
Forty years of mornings and I'd take every one,
If it starts with your voice and it ends with the sun.
The way you say good morning, low and slow and rough,
Sunday doesn't start until you say enough.
Pull the blinds, let the light come down in lines,
The way you say good morning makes the morning mine.
```

- Scene: The same morning, and a two-second glimpse of the same morning much later.
- Shot: The lateral dolly from chorus one repeated in the opposite direction, wider, more light in the room.
- Cut to: A flash-forward of two older hands on the same sheet in the same light, two seconds, no faces.
- Emotion: A promise made without saying anything that sounds like a promise.
- Lighting: Fullest light of the video; strings arrive with the widest frame.

### [post-chorus – the two words]

```
Good morning, good morning,
Say it low, say it slow, say it just to me.
Good morning, good morning,
And the rest of the day can be what it wants to be.
```

- Scene: The two words alone, over the smallest possible images.
- Shot: Four tight cutaways cut on the beat — his mouth, her eyes, the fan, the light on the wall.
- Cut to: The blinds being tilted almost shut again by a hand reaching up without looking.
- Emotion: A shared decision to stay exactly where they are.
- Lighting: Dimming as the blinds close; the room going warm-dark rather than cold-dark.

### [outro – one chord, one voice, a breath]

```
Blinds down, light in lines across the sheet,
Your voice in the dark before the day begins.
Don't get up yet, don't say anything else,
Just say it to me one more time.
```

- Scene: The blinds closed, the room back to stripes, the day formally cancelled.
- Shot: Return to the locked-off wide from the foot of the bed that opened the video, identical framing, now warm instead of blue.
- Cut to: Final shot — a close-up of her face with her eyes closing, one held breath on the soundtrack, the frame holding through the fade.
- Emotion: Peace, and a small private win.
- Lighting: Warm stripes, everything else in soft shadow.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the blind cord pulled and the stripes of light widening across the bed all at once, cut on the downbeat of "good morning."
- **The caption line:** "You haven't opened both eyes and you've already won."
- **The sound clip:** the two words themselves, isolated over the post-chorus beat, built to be used as an audio on morning-routine and couple content.
- **The turn quote:** "The first thing in the world stopped being the worst part."
- **The flash-forward:** two older hands on the same sheet in the same light, on screen for two seconds in the final chorus and pausable.
