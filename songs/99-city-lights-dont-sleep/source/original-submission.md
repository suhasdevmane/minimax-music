# City Lights Don't Sleep — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:** An insomniac's love letter to the city that stays up with her.
Not a song about being unable to sleep and suffering for it — a song about
going outside at three in the morning and finding a whole second population
who are also awake, and deciding that this is a perfectly good way to live.

**Mood:** Wide, warm, euphoric-melancholy. Neon calm. The record is never sad
about being awake; the only ache in it is other people's insistence that
something must be wrong.

**Message:** "I'm not lonely, I'm just on a different clock."

**Genre:** Modern synthwave. Think: The Midnight x Chvrches x the calmer end
of The Weeknd's eighties records. UK northern register, Manchester detail.

**Tempo & Key:** 114 BPM, 4/4, F minor. No modulation; the final chorus lifts
through an added choir pad and an octave-up vocal instead.

**Vocal setup:** Female lead, solo, in a UK northern register — warm, flat
vowels, conversational. **The bridge is spoken word, not sung**: unrhymed,
unhurried, over a single pad with no drums, closer to a monologue than a rap.
Stacked female harmonies on the choruses.

**Instrumentation:**
- Sixteenth-note analogue bass arpeggio (the engine of the record)
- Analogue polysynth pads, present from the first bar to the last
- Gated reverb kit, biggest in the choruses; rim and hat in verse two
- Wide chorused electric guitar: the soaring chorus line and the whole break
- Saw lead doubling the arpeggio in the choruses; second pad up an octave in
  the pre-choruses; choir pad on the final chorus only
- Ambience: room tone, a passing tram, a ring-road hum under both
  pre-choruses, cafe cutlery and a tinny radio, a distant siren, tape hiss

**Mood arc:** Intro, three in the morning and a coat off the back of a door.
Verse one, the walk: the empty tram, the fox, the canal. Pre-chorus, the nod
between night people. Chorus, the skyline. Verse two, the all-night cafe on
Oldham Street. Pre-chorus, the sky going grey. Bridge, spoken: the argument
with everyone who tells her to get some rest. Final chorus, past tense and
biggest. Post-chorus, the chant. Outro, half five, the streetlights going out.

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – pad and one arpeggio, no drums]

```
Three in the morning and my ceiling knows my face,
Not sad, just wired, just awake.
I take my coat off the back of the door,
And go and see who else is still here.
```

- Scene: A small flat above a shop. A bed, a ceiling, a coat on a hook.
- Shot: Overhead, locked off: her lying on top of the covers fully awake, eyes open, hands behind her head, entirely calm.
- Cut to: The same ceiling from her point of view — a water stain, a lampshade, the reflection of a passing headlight crossing it.
- Cut to: Her hand taking a coat off the back of a door; the door swinging shut on a dark hallway.
- Emotion: Not distress. Restlessness with somewhere to put itself.
- Lighting: Sodium orange through a thin curtain; everything else in the room a deep blue.

### [verse 1 – arpeggio, soft kit, one held chord]

```
The tram goes past empty with its lights all on,
Like it's doing somebody a favour for nothing.
There's a lad hosing down the front of a chip shop,
And a fox on the corner that won't move for me.
The canal holds the orange of all of Ancoats,
And the water doesn't shiver, it just carries it.
Everybody I know is asleep in a postcode,
And I've never in my life felt less alone.
```

- Scene: The walk out. Northern quarter streets, brick and iron and old signage.
- Shot: A tram passing with every interior light on and nobody inside it, shot from a fixed position on the far pavement.
- Cut to: A young man in waders hosing down the tiled front of a takeaway, steam rising off the pavement.
- Cut to: A fox standing dead centre of an empty road, looking straight at the camera, refusing to move; hold it long.
- Cut to: A canal at night holding the whole district's orange light on a completely flat surface.
- Emotion: Delight, quietly. She is a tourist in her own postcode.
- Lighting: Sodium, brick red, one green shop sign. Wet ground everywhere, no rain falling.

### [pre-chorus 1 – gated snare, filter sweep]

```
The night shift and the sleepless and the lads just clocking off,
We nod like a handover, we don't ask a thing.
There's nothing to explain at half past three,
And the ring road hums a note I can sing.
```

- Scene: The second population. The people who are out at this hour.
- Shots: A cleaner wheeling a trolley out of an office lobby. Two chefs on a step in whites. A nurse walking to a car park. A security guard behind glass. Each of them exchanging a nod with her, four seconds each, no dialogue.
- Emotion: Membership.
- Lighting: Each shot has exactly one dominant light source and it is a different colour every time.

### [chorus 1 – full widescreen synthwave]

```
City lights don't sleep, and neither do we,
Sodium gold and a window still green.
Not lonely, just running on a different clock,
And the whole of the skyline is up with me.
City lights don't sleep, they just turn themselves down,
They keep one eye open for whoever's around.
Put your hand on the glass and count them with me,
City lights don't sleep, and neither do we.
```

- Scene: The skyline. The widest sequence in the film.
- Shots: A rooftop or a footbridge with the whole city in front of her. Long-lens compression of a tower with a scatter of lit windows, one of them green. A slow drone drift across the ring road with headlights streaming. Her hand flat against a window with the lights beyond it. A single office floor, fully lit, absolutely empty.
- Emotion: Euphoric. She is not sad and the video must not imply she is.
- Lighting: Neon and sodium at full saturation, deep blue sky, everything wet and reflective.

### [verse 2 – rim and hat, arpeggio filtered]

```
There's a caff on Oldham Street that opens at four,
And the woman on the counter knows I take it strong.
Two builders, a driver, a girl in last night's dress,
And a radio that only plays songs nobody wants.
Nobody here is anybody's problem,
Nobody here is going to ask how I've been.
We're just the people that the daylight hasn't counted,
And I like the arithmetic that leaves us in between.
```

- Scene: An all-night cafe. Formica, a hot counter, a wall clock, a radio on a shelf.
- Shot: The door opening from inside, her coming in with cold on her coat.
- Cut to: The woman behind the counter already pouring before she asks.
- Cut to: Four separate portraits at four separate tables — two builders sharing a plate, a taxi driver with his phone face-down, a young woman in last night's dress and somebody else's jacket, all of them completely at ease.
- Cut to: The radio on the shelf, an old one with a bent aerial.
- Emotion: The specific comfort of being unremarkable.
- Lighting: The warmest sequence in the film. Interior tungsten, condensation on the window, blue night pressing on the glass outside.

### [pre-chorus 2 – warmer, wider]

```
The gritters and the bakers and the ones who cannot stop,
We nod like a handover, we don't ask a thing.
There's nothing to explain when the sky goes grey,
And the ring road hums a note I can sing.
```

- Scene: The shift turning over. The first grey.
- Shots: A gritter lorry with orange beacons going past a shut pub. A bakery back door open with flour light spilling out and trays going in. A window cleaner setting up a ladder at half four. Each with the same four-second nod.
- Emotion: The handover, made literal.
- Lighting: Sodium losing its fight with a grey sky at the top of every frame.

### [chorus 2 – wider, octave counter-line]

```
(as chorus 1)
```

- Scene: The city from the outside, moving.
- Shots: The ring road from a bridge, a long exposure feel with headlights as lines. A car park roof with the whole grid below it. The canal again with the reflection now broken by a barge. Her walking a footbridge with the skyline behind her, tracking from the side.
- Emotion: Bigger, freer, faster cutting.
- Lighting: More saturated, more sources, everything moving.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: No lyrics. The guitar solo, then the filter-down, then the build.
- Shots: A long slow drift along an empty motorway slip road. The green window from chorus one, closer, a single lamp on a desk with nobody at it. The filter-down: near black, one pad, one distant siren, one blue light crossing a wall two streets away. The build: five fast returns to the fox, the tram, the canal, the cafe window and the flat lit office floor.
- Lighting: The near-black passage is the only time this film is dark.

### [bridge – spoken word, no drums, one pad]

```
People say "get some rest," like rest is somewhere you drive to.
I've tried. I've laid there and listened to my own blood.
Out here the city is doing the same thing I am,
holding a light up because somebody has to.
The offices are empty and the windows are still on—
which is either a waste or a kindness, and I've decided it's kindness.
So no, I'm not broken because I'm awake at four,
I'm just keeping the same hours as everything I love.
```

- Scene: A single continuous shot. This is the moment the record stops moving.
- Shot: Her sitting on a low wall on a footbridge over the ring road, facing camera, city behind her, talking. One take, locked off, no cuts for the whole spoken passage except two: a cutaway to the lit empty office floor on the word *kindness*, and a cutaway to her own dark bedroom window on *awake at four*.
- Emotion: Direct address. She is not confessing, she is settling an argument.
- Lighting: Underlit by the road below, pale grey coming in behind her; the first light in the film that is not artificial.

### [final chorus – choir pad, octave vocal]

```
City lights don't sleep, and neither do we,
Sodium gold and a window still green.
Not lonely, just running on a different clock,
And the whole of the skyline is up with me.
City lights don't sleep, they just turn themselves down,
They kept one eye open till the morning came round.
Put your hand on the glass, you were counting with me,
City lights don't sleep, and neither do we.
```

- Scene: Everyone from the film, at once, at dawn.
- Shots: The cleaner, the chefs, the nurse, the security guard, the gritter driver, the baker and the cafe woman, each in their own frame, each finishing. The tram again, now with three people on it. A drone rising off the footbridge until the whole grid is visible with the sky going pale.
- Emotion: Collective, generous, not triumphant.
- Lighting: The last of the neon against the first real daylight; both at once for about ninety seconds and then never again.

### [post-chorus – chant over a stripped arpeggio]

```
Leave it on, leave it on—out to the ring road,
Leave it on, leave it on, till the morning takes over.
Somebody out there is watching the same light,
Leave it on, leave it on, we'll get there together.
```

- Scene: Lights, one at a time, refusing to go off.
- Shots: A tower window. A shop sign. A bedroom lamp. A phone screen face-up on a windowsill. Cut hard on every beat, all the same size in frame.
- Lighting: Each shot is one light in a dark frame, and they get progressively less necessary as the sky lifts behind them.

### [outro – filter down to one pad]

```
Half five and the sky turns the colour of a bruise,
Then it fades like a dimmer and the streetlights go out.
I'll sleep when the buses are full and the city has company,
City lights don't sleep, and neither do we.
```

- Scene: Half past five. The handover completes.
- Shots: The sky over a terrace going deep purple-grey. A whole street of streetlights cutting out at once, in one take, no cut. Her walking home with the light behind her. Final frame: her flat window from the street, the curtain closing, the sodium already off; hold on the building as the first full bus goes past in the foreground.
- Emotion: Peaceful. She won the argument and nobody had to lose.
- Lighting: Purple-grey into flat morning; not one warm source left by the last frame.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the fox in the middle of an empty road refusing to move, held for a full four seconds under the first *"city lights don't sleep."*
- **The streetlights:** an entire street of lamps cutting out at once in a single uncut take, in the outro. Nobody films this and everybody has seen it.
- **The challenge:** post the view from wherever you are at three in the morning, cut to the post-chorus chant. One light, one window, one caption.
- **The caption line:** *"I'm not lonely, I'm just on a different clock."*
- **The quote (spoken bridge):** *"The offices are empty and the windows are still on, which is either a waste or a kindness, and I've decided it's kindness."*
- **The nod:** four seconds, no dialogue, six times in the film — the whole social contract of the night shift in one gesture.
