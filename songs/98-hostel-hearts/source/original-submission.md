# Hostel Hearts — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:** Four days in a hostel with six or seven people whose surnames she
will never learn, and who become family anyway. The song is about the kind of
intimacy that only happens between strangers who know they are temporary — and
about refusing to let anyone call it less real for being short.

**Mood:** Warm, crowded, slightly chaotic, quietly moving. Loud in the
choruses and very quiet twice.

**Message:** "It doesn't have to be long to be family."

**Genre:** Warm indie pop with a gang-vocal chorus. Think: The Lumineers x
Maisie Peters x Vampire Weekend's brighter side. Untrained voices left
untuned on purpose.

**Tempo & Key:** 106 BPM, 4/4, D major. No key change; the final chorus lifts
through arrangement — stomp, tambourine and a low brass swell.

**Vocal setup:** Female lead, solo, with a **mixed gang vocal** underneath
every chorus, recorded live in one room with the bleed left in and
deliberately a little out of time. Verses close and conversational, almost
muttered at the start. The bridge near-spoken.

**Instrumentation:**
- Two acoustic guitars: one fingerpicked throughout, one strumming from
  verse one, ringing suspended chords in the pre-choruses
- Round electric bass, brushed kit, shaker; rim pattern in verse two
- Room-recorded claps with the whole stairwell reverb left on
- Tambourine from the first pre-chorus; stomp and low brass on the last
- Countermelody electric guitar from chorus two; a whistled melody in the
  break
- Ambience: a ceiling fan, voices two rooms away, a knife on a board, a
  kettle, a lighter, night-market noise, a suitcase on stairs, a bus leaving

**Mood arc:** Intro, arriving tired and not wanting to talk. Verse one, the
kitchen and the relief of nobody asking. Pre-chorus, the noticeboard.
Chorus, the whole room singing. Verse two, the night market, the roof at four
in the morning, and the thing she says out loud. Pre-chorus, checkout at ten.
Bridge, the argument with everyone who says it was not real. Final chorus,
past tense and louder. Post-chorus, the singalong. Outro, the bus, and the
next tired person in the same bunk.

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – fingerpicked guitar, room tone, no drums]

```
Bunk nineteen, second floor,
A ceiling fan that only turns one way.
I came in tired and I didn't want to talk,
By ten I was laughing in a language I don't speak.
```

- Scene: A hostel dorm at dusk. Eight beds, four of them made, one bare mattress with a number stencilled on the frame.
- Shot: Her dropping a backpack on the bare mattress and just standing there for a beat, too tired to unpack.
- Cut to: A ceiling fan turning slowly overhead, shot from directly beneath.
- Cut to: A hard cut to the same face three hours later, mid-laugh in a crowded common room, no explanation offered.
- Emotion: Depleted, then ambushed by other people.
- Lighting: Dorm dim and blue; common room warm, one working lamp doing all the work.

### [verse 1 – brushed kit, round bass, second guitar]

```
There's a Dutch girl teaching us all to cook with one pan,
An Argentine guy who lost his passport and his mind.
Somebody's playlist, somebody's cheap red wine,
Somebody's mother on a video call waving at us.
Strangers in a kitchen with one lighter and no plan,
And nobody asks me what I do or where I've been.
First time all year that nobody wants my history,
Just my half of the table and my name.
```

- Scene: The shared kitchen. The most crowded frames in the film.
- Shot: One pan on one working hob, four people crowded round it, a tall blonde woman directing everybody with a wooden spoon.
- Cut to: A young man tearing his rucksack apart on the floor looking for a passport, everyone else stepping over him.
- Cut to: A phone propped against a sauce bottle with an older woman on video call, the whole table waving at her at once.
- Cut to: A lighter passing hand to hand round the table for a candle stuck in a bottle.
- Emotion: The specific relief of being nobody in particular.
- Lighting: One strip light and one candle; steam on the lens; warm and green and unglamorous.

### [pre-chorus 1 – ringing guitars, tambourine, humming]

```
The door never locks and the kettle never cools,
The noticeboard is covered in the names of people gone.
Somebody writes mine on it with a dying marker,
And that's how you know that you belong.
```

- Scene: The hallway. A corkboard covered in layers of paper, tape and marker.
- Shot: A slow push-in on the board: dozens of names, dates and drawings, some faded, some fresh.
- Cut to: A hand writing her name into the last free corner with a marker that keeps skipping; the writer shaking it and carrying on.
- Emotion: The exact second she stops being a guest.
- Lighting: Corridor fluorescent, unflattering and honest.

### [chorus 1 – gang vocal, stairwell claps]

```
Hostel hearts, strangers for a night, family by the morning,
Four days is a lifetime when you're living without warning.
We don't know each other's last names and we never will,
But you sat up when I was sick, and I'd do it again.
Hostel hearts, no address and no goodbye that lands,
A rooftop, a lighter and eleven pairs of hands.
Strangers for a night, family by the morning.
```

- Scene: The rooftop. Fairy lights on a washing line, plastic chairs, a low wall, the whole group.
- Shots: The group singing badly and enthusiastically, all talking over the song. Hands clapping out of time. A wide from the far side of the roof with the city behind them. One person asleep in a chair through the entire chorus, undisturbed.
- Emotion: Belonging, loudly.
- Lighting: Fairy lights, one orange streetlamp from below, the sky still holding a little blue.

### [verse 2 – rim pattern, one guitar]

```
Night market, second night, we ate something we couldn't name,
And the Argentine found his passport in his coat.
On the roof at four in the morning somebody's crying,
And nobody makes it weird, we just move closer.
The Dutch girl says she isn't going home in September,
The German plays the only slow song he knows.
And I told a room of strangers what I never told my sister,
And the sky went the colour of a peach and nobody spoke.
```

- Scene: A night market, then the roof at four in the morning. The film's emotional centre.
- Shot: The market in one long handheld take — steam, tongs, a plastic stool, everyone pointing at food nobody can identify.
- Cut to: The Argentine finding his passport in his own coat pocket and holding it up; the group's reaction, unreasonable joy.
- Cut to: The roof, hours later, most of the fairy lights off. Somebody crying quietly. Nobody comforts them theatrically; two people just shift their chairs closer.
- Cut to: A guitar being handed across; the slow song.
- Cut to: Her talking, at length, to a group of people she met on Tuesday. We never hear a word of it.
- Emotion: The particular honesty of four in the morning with people who cannot use it against you.
- Lighting: Market: hot neon and steam. Roof: almost nothing, then a peach-coloured sky arriving behind them.

### [pre-chorus 2 – warmer, fuller]

```
The door never locks and the kettle never cools,
And checkout is at ten and we know what that means.
Somebody's bus goes first, somebody's on the stairwell,
And the marker on the board has run out.
```

- Scene: The last morning. Packing.
- Shot: The same kettle, boiling for the last round.
- Cut to: A suitcase being dragged down a stairwell, badly; somebody sitting on the stairs beside it not helping and not leaving.
- Cut to: The dead marker on the noticeboard, tried once, tried again, put down.
- Emotion: Everyone pretending this is normal.
- Lighting: Flat honest daylight; the least flattering grade in the film, on purpose.

### [chorus 2 – countermelody guitar, fuller gang]

```
(as chorus 1)
```

- Scene: The goodbye at the bus station, intercut with the four days.
- Shots: Hugs that go on too long. Numbers exchanged on paper because someone's phone is dead. The bus door closing. Cut against: the kitchen, the roof, the market, the board.
- Emotion: Bigger and sadder than chorus one, sung exactly as loudly.
- Lighting: Bus station: hard morning sun through a canopy, hot and clean.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: No lyrics. Guitar solo answered by whistling, then the clap-and-stomp breakdown.
- Shots: A slow-motion push along the empty rooftop with the chairs still where they were left. The kitchen, cleaned, one pan drying. A new group of strangers arriving at the front desk with the same backpacks. The ceiling fan again. The noticeboard, from the same angle as the pre-chorus, with new names already over the old ones.
- Lighting: The empty-room shots are the only cool-graded frames in the film.

### [bridge – voice and guitar, near-spoken]

```
People say it wasn't real because it only lasted four days,
As if a thing must be long before it's allowed to be true.
I've known people ten years who never asked me how I sleep,
And a stranger from Osaka carried my bag up two flights.
So call it what you want, I know what it was,
It was family, and it fit in a room with eight beds.
```

- Scene: Back home. A different country, a different light, a small flat.
- Shot: Her alone at a kitchen table, one mug, a laptop closed. The most static shot in the film.
- Cut to: A single flashback with no sound: a young man carrying her rucksack up a narrow stairwell, two flights, unasked.
- Cut to: Her phone face-up on the table with a group chat lighting up (composited), and her smiling at it.
- Emotion: Defiance, not nostalgia. She is arguing with someone offscreen and she is right.
- Lighting: Cold northern daylight through one window; the warmest thing in frame is the phone.

### [final chorus – past tense, biggest gang vocal]

```
(as chorus 1, with "We won't know each other's last names and we never did")
```

- Scene: A split world: her flat, and the six of them wherever they are now.
- Shots: Six quick portraits, each in a different city, each doing something ordinary — a tram, a lecture hall, a kitchen, a beach, a night shift, a bus. All six singing the chorus in their own rooms, badly, in sync.
- Emotion: The gang vocal made literal.
- Lighting: Six different times of day at once; the cut is what holds it together.

### [post-chorus – singalong]

```
Write your name on the board, write your name on the board,
Somebody after you will read it and feel less alone.
Write your name on the board, we were here, we were loud,
And a wall in a hallway is the only proof we own.
```

- Scene: The noticeboard, over months.
- Shots: A time-lapse feel achieved by cuts: layers of names going up, being covered, being uncovered. A stranger reading it and laughing at something written there. A hand adding a new name in a new colour.
- Lighting: Corridor fluorescent, unchanged all the way through — the one constant in the film.

### [outro – fan, room tone, one guitar]

```
The bus pulls out at six, the fan still turns one way,
And bunk nineteen has a new tired person in it now.
I hope she doesn't want to talk, and I hope she does by ten,
And I hope somebody hands her half a table and a name.
```

- Scene: The dorm again, the same bed, a different woman.
- Shots: A bus pulling out of a station at dawn, seen from inside the hostel window. The ceiling fan, same angle as the intro. A new traveller dropping her bag on the same bare mattress and standing there, too tired to unpack. Final shot: through the kitchen doorway, somebody sliding a chair out for her; hold on the empty half of the table before she sits.
- Emotion: The loop, and the invitation.
- Lighting: Dawn blue in the dorm, warm lamp in the kitchen beyond the doorway.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the rooftop gang vocal on *"family by the morning"*, shot wide, everybody out of time, one person asleep through all of it.
- **The board:** the marker running out mid-name, and the post-chorus time-lapse of layers of names — the most re-postable image in the video.
- **The challenge:** film the four-day family you made — the kitchen, the bunk, the goodbye — and cut it to the singalong post-chorus.
- **The caption line:** *"It was family, and it fit in a room with eight beds."*
- **The quote:** *"I've known people ten years who never asked me how I sleep."*
- **The last shot:** the chair sliding out for the next tired person. Wordless, and it lands.
