# Two Time Zones — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
A couple eight hours apart, living the same day twice. Her morning is his night. The whole song is built out of the arithmetic of that gap — two clocks on one wall, a weather app for a city she has never stood in, a voice note recorded at his two in the morning and heard at her seven. It ends with an aisle seat booked for a Tuesday.

**Mood:**
Warm, patient, quietly aching, then released. Not a sad long-distance song — a competent one. These two are good at this, and they are also done with it.

**Message:**
"There's an hour on the ocean that belongs to nobody. That one's ours."

**Genre:**
Cinematic pop ballad, female and male duet. Think: Sam Smith x Sasha Alex Sloan x Dermot Kennedy — upright piano, soft analogue pads, and a full first chorus that arrives with no drums under it at all.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 90 BPM, 4/4
- Key: C major throughout, no modulation
- **The drums do not arrive until the second chorus.** That is the production decision the whole arrangement hangs on: the first chorus lifts with strings and harmony alone, so the second one lifts off the ground.

**Vocal setup:**
- Singer A (female): close and unhurried, conversational in the verses.
- Singer B (male): a warm unpolished tenor, slightly sleepy in the low register, sitting just behind the beat, harmonising a third below her.
- The intro and outro trade line by line. The choruses are unison, then close harmony, then full width with the male an octave below.

**Instrumentation:**
- Warm upright piano (alone in the first bar and the last)
- Soft felt bass, becoming a warm synth bass in the choruses
- Low analogue synth pads
- Soft rhodes doubling verse phrase endings
- Cinematic strings from the first pre-chorus
- Bright bell-like synth on the final hook
- Live kit from the second chorus only: soft kick, rim, rolling toms
- Ambience: a faint clock tick, dawn birdsong and a kettle on one side of the stereo field, night traffic and a distant siren on the other

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: two rooms, two clocks, ten past six and ten past two
- Verse 1: her side — the weather app, the empty chair, the dropped line
- Pre-chorus: counting the hours out on a hand
- Chorus: the hook, wide and weightless, no drums
- Verse 2: his side — the voice note, the calendar in pen, the moon
- Second chorus: the drums arrive
- Bridge: the refusal of the rectangle of light, and the ticket
- Final chorus and outro: full width, then back to the two clocks, resolved

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – piano, pad, clock tick, voices trading lines]

```
It's ten past six and the kettle's going on,
It's ten past two and I'm nowhere near asleep.
I'm watching a sunrise you already had,
I'm holding a night that you don't get to keep.
There's a clock for me and a clock for you,
Hung on the same wall, both of them true.
Same song playing, different light,
Good morning, love. And good night.
```

- Scene: Two flats, split screen, from the first frame.
- Shots: Left frame — a kettle going on in cold blue pre-dawn, her in a jumper over pyjamas. Right frame — a lamp still burning at two in the morning, him at a kitchen table, unable to sleep.
- Cut to: A tight two-shot of the split line itself, so the two rooms appear to share a wall.
- Emotion: Familiar routine. They have done this a hundred times.
- Lighting: Left cold blue turning gold; right warm lamp against black.

### [verse 1 – her side, sparse]

```
I keep your city on my weather screen,
A place I've never stood but check for rain.
You send me photos of your street at midnight,
I send you the first bus and the light on the lane.
```

- Scene: Her morning, alone, full frame.
- Shots: A phone lock screen with two cities and two temperatures on it (composited). Her at a bus stop in first light. A photo arriving of an empty street at midnight, which she looks at for longer than she means to.
- Emotion: Tenderness worn into habit.
- Lighting: Blue hour going warm, real streetlight, breath visible.

```
There's a chair in here that's yours and nobody sits there,
There's a mug in yours that nobody else can use.
We've got the whole round world turning in between us,
And a line that drops out just when I need the news.
```

- Scene: The two absences, cut against each other.
- Shots: A chair at her table with a coat still hanging on it. A mug on his shelf that he moves aside to reach another one and then puts back. A globe or a departures board turning. A call freezing and dropping, both frames going still at once.
- Emotion: The small daily cost.
- Lighting: Warm interiors both sides, matched exposure for the first time.

### [pre-chorus]

```
So I count the hours out on my hand,
Take away eight and I know where you are.
There's an hour on the ocean that belongs to nobody,
And I think that's the hour that we share.
```

- Scene: The arithmetic, made physical.
- Shots: Her counting on her fingers at a desk, quietly, in the middle of a working day. A world map on a wall with two pins. An aerial of open ocean at the moment where it is neither of their days.
- Emotion: The idea that carries the whole song.
- Lighting: Flat office daylight, then vast open sea light.

### [chorus – wide, no drums]

```
Two time zones, one heartbeat,
Your midnight leaning on my morning light.
I'll say good night while you say good morning,
And somewhere in the middle we get it right.
Two time zones, one heartbeat,
Eight hours out and still in time.
Set the clocks however you want to,
Two time zones, one heartbeat, one line.
```

- Scene: The split screen used properly for the first time.
- Shots: Both of them lying down, heads at the split line, so they appear to share a pillow across two cities. Two curtains opening and closing in sync. Two hands flat against two windows, matched in the frame.
- Emotion: The wide, weightless one. No drums, so nothing pushes.
- Lighting: Left gold sunrise, right deep blue midnight, both equally beautiful.

### [verse 2 – his side, piano and brushed pulse]

```
You left a voice note at your two in the morning,
I heard it at seven with my coffee going cold.
You sounded tired and honest and unguarded,
And I played it twice again out on the road.
```

- Scene: His night and her morning, chained together.
- Shots: Him recording a voice note at a dark kitchen table, talking too long, deleting nothing. Her hearing it at seven, headphones in, walking, coffee going cold in her other hand. Her stopping on a pavement to play it again.
- Emotion: The song's warmest moment.
- Lighting: His warm and low; hers bright and cold.

```
I marked the calendar in pen and not in pencil,
Because pencil's for the maybes and I'm sure.
There's a moon out here that's already been above you,
And it's carrying your evening to my door.
```

- Scene: A calendar and a moon.
- Shots: A pen pressing hard on a wall calendar, a date circled twice (the marks illegible, composited). A pencil put down and not used. The same moon shot from both cities, cut back to back, matched in size and position in frame.
- Emotion: Certainty.
- Lighting: Kitchen practical, then night sky both sides.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
So I count the hours out on my hand,
Take away eight and I'm already there.
There's an hour on the ocean that belongs to nobody,
And I've built a whole house for us in the air.
```

- Scene: The arithmetic again, from his side.
- Shots: Him counting on his fingers on a night bus. The same wall map, the same two pins, a thread now between them. The open ocean again, this time at dawn.
- Emotion: Anticipation replacing endurance.
- Lighting: Night bus sodium, then first light on water.

### [chorus]

(repeat)

- Scene: The chorus world with the drums in it — the video should visibly gain energy here.
- Shots: Both of them moving through their days in matched motion: two coats going on, two doors closing, two sets of stairs. The split line starts to feel like a seam rather than a wall.
- Emotion: Momentum.
- Lighting: Both sides warmer than the first chorus.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: The world between the two of them, no lyrics.
- Shots: Two clocks on one wall, both second hands moving, slowly drifting out of sync in the frame. An empty aeroplane cabin at night. A departures board flicking over. The dawn side and the night side of the planet from very high up. One held bar of silence: the split screen, both frames empty, both rooms lit.
- Lighting: Gold on the left, blue on the right, meeting at the seam.

### [bridge – piano and voice, her half then his]

```
I don't want to love you in a rectangle of light,
I want to hear you breathing in the same dark room.
I want an argument about who left the door open,
And the boring middle bit, and I want it soon.
So I booked it. Aisle seat. Tuesday. Bag packed.
Your morning and my morning on the same floor.
One more night of two sunrises and never again,
And then it's one time zone, one door.
```

- Scene: The decision.
- Shots: Her closing a laptop mid-call, deliberately, and sitting in the dark with it closed. Her booking a flight, one tap, and putting the phone face-down. Him not knowing yet, asleep with the lamp still on. A bag being packed on a bed. A passport going into a coat pocket.
- Emotion: The turn. The song stops being about coping.
- Lighting: Dark room lit only by a laptop, then that light going out; his side warm and unaware.

### [final chorus – full width, drums, both voices]

```
Two time zones, one heartbeat,
Your midnight leaning on my morning light.
I'll say good night while you say good morning,
And somewhere in the middle we get it right.
Two time zones, one heartbeat,
Eight hours out but not for long.
Set the clocks however you want to,
Two time zones, one heartbeat, one song.
```

- Scene: Travel, and the split screen collapsing.
- Shots: An aisle seat, a window going light, a wing over cloud. The split line moving slowly across the frame as her side takes more and more of it. Arrivals doors. Him at a barrier, checking a phone, not looking up yet.
- Emotion: Joy arriving.
- Lighting: Bright above cloud, then hard airport light, then daylight.

### [post-chorus – gentle, alternating]

```
Good morning, good night,
Same moon, different light.
Good morning, good night,
Eight hours apart and holding tight.
Good morning, good night,
Two time zones, one heartbeat, one life.
```

- Scene: The meeting.
- Shots: Him looking up. The split line gone entirely — one frame, two people, one hug that lasts three full seconds. A bag dropped on the floor and left there.
- Emotion: Release.
- Lighting: One light source for both of them for the first time in the video.

### [outro – piano, voices trading lines again, resolved]

```
It's ten past six and the kettle's going on,
It's ten past two and I'm packing up my room.
Same song playing, same light finally,
No more counting backwards from the moon.
Two time zones closing into one,
Good morning, love. I'll see you soon.
```

- Scene: One kitchen, one morning, both of them in it.
- Shots: A kettle going on, filmed exactly like the opening shot, but two mugs come down instead of one. The two clocks on the wall behind them, and a hand reaching up to change one of them. Final shot: both clocks reading the same time, the two of them out of focus behind, and the kettle steaming.
- Emotion: Ordinary, and enormous.
- Lighting: Clean morning light, one source, no split.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the split-screen shared pillow — two heads at the seam, two cities, one bed, on *"two time zones, one heartbeat."*
- **The knife line:** *"I don't want to love you in a rectangle of light."*
- **The caption line:** *"There's an hour on the ocean that belongs to nobody."*
- **The challenge:** the two-clocks edit — film your wall with a second clock set to someone else's city, cut to the intro, and post the day you take it down.
- **The quote:** *"Pencil's for the maybes and I'm sure."*
