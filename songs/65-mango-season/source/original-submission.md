# Mango Season — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
Childhood summers at a grandparents' house in a small Indian town: the train that slows at a one-lamp station, a grandfather waiting with his cycle, cousins hanging off the gate, a mango tree they climbed before anyone could stop them, cots on the terrace, a grandfather's radio and the cricket score, and a monsoon that arrives early. Years later, in a city office with a supermarket mango wrapped in plastic, she wants to go back. In the bridge she does.

**Mood:**
Warm, golden, homesick in the sweetest way. A memory song that ends as a homecoming.

**Message:**
"Take me back to mango season."

**Genre:**
Bollywood-folk pop with a modern kick. Think: the warmth of a Shankar-Ehsaan-Loy summer ballad x Prateek Kuhad's intimacy x the wide, hand-clapped choruses of an Arijit Singh road song, sung in plain Indian English with no slang.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 104 BPM, 4/4
- Key: D major, sunny and open
- Final chorus lifts a whole step to E major on the words "I am going back"

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, close and unhurried in the verses, wide and golden in the choruses, near-spoken in the bridge.
- Backing vocals: stacked female harmonies in the choruses, a wordless choir on the post-chorus chant.

**Instrumentation:**
- Nylon-string acoustic guitar (fingerpicked verses, strummed choruses)
- Dholak (the main groove) with tabla-style fills
- Bansuri flute (lead melodic voice, intro and outro)
- Modern kick and warm sub-bass in the choruses
- Shaker and hand claps
- Soft string pad, low cello, harmonium-like drone in the bridge
- Textures: train wheels, a station bell, a ceiling fan slowing, transistor radio crackle, dust and wind, crows, pouring rain

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the train slows, the flute, the taste of summer from a mile away
- Verses: the house, the cousins, the tree, the radio, the pickle jars, the grandmother who saved the sweetest mango
- Pre-chorus: the city present, then the sky about to break
- Chorus: golden, wide, the plea to be taken back
- Instrumental: the first rain, drums and flute
- Bridge: the house smaller, the tree taller, the decision to buy the ticket
- Final chorus: "I am going back", a whole step up
- Outro: the station again, nobody waiting, she knows the way

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – flute and guitar, the train]

```
The train slows down at a station with one lamp,
My grandfather waiting with his cycle and a smile.
The dust smells sweet and the air is heavy,
I can taste the summer from a mile.
```

- Scene: A small railway station at dusk. One lamp, a painted name board (never legible), a slow train.
- Shot: A child's hand on the window bars of a train carriage, then the platform sliding in, an old man with a bicycle raising his hand.
- Cut to: Adult Mahima on a city train at night, forehead on the glass, eyes closed. The memory is hers.
- Emotion: Anticipation, the sweetness before arrival.
- Lighting: Amber platform lamp against a violet sky; the city train is cold and fluorescent.

### [verse 1]

```
Steel trunk tied to the roof of a rickshaw,
Cousins hanging off the gate before we stop.
Grandmother counting heads at the doorway,
Seven children, one house, one water pot.
```

- Scene: A cycle rickshaw pulling up at an old house with an iron gate and a courtyard.
- Shot: Six children hanging off the gate, shouting, waving; a steel trunk being lifted down.
- Cut to: A grandmother in a cotton sari at the door, counting with her finger, laughing.
- Emotion: Chaos and welcome.
- Lighting: Late afternoon sun, saffron and green, dust in the air.

```
We climbed the tree before they told us not to,
Raw mango, salt and chilli, sitting on the wall.
The fan went off, the power came and went,
And nobody minded at all.
```

- Scene: The courtyard mango tree, children in the branches; a low wall in the sun.
- Shot: Small hands passing slices of raw mango sprinkled with salt and red chilli, mouths puckering.
- Cut to: A ceiling fan slowing to a stop, the children on the floor beneath it, nobody moving, then laughing.
- Emotion: Freedom, the long afternoon.
- Lighting: Dappled leaf light in the tree; the room dim and hot.

### [pre-chorus]

```
Now the city keeps me busy, keeps me tired,
Every summer looks the same from here.
The office window only shows me concrete,
A mango in the supermarket, wrapped in plastic, never sweet.
But I close my eyes and I am nine years old,
And the sky is about to break, I can feel it near.
```

- Scene: Present day. A glass office, a supermarket aisle at night.
- Shot: Adult Mahima at a desk by a window that shows only the next building; then her hand lifting a single mango sealed in plastic from a supermarket shelf.
- Cut to: Her eyes closing in the aisle; a flash of the courtyard sky going dark with cloud.
- Emotion: Tired longing, then the first pull of memory.
- Lighting: Flat office white; supermarket fluorescent; the flash is warm and overcast.

### [chorus – wide, golden, everything in]

```
Take me back to mango season,
Yellow hands and a sticky chin.
Take me back to mango season,
Bare feet in the courtyard, let me in.
Cousins on the terrace, cots in a row,
Counting every star we did not know.
The first rain came early that year,
Take me back to mango season, take me there.
```

- Scene: The best of the summer in a montage: yellow-stained hands, a child's sticky chin, bare feet running across a hot courtyard, cots lined up on the terrace under a sky full of stars.
- Shot: Child Mahima with a mango in both hands, juice down her arms, grinning.
- Cut to: The terrace at night, seven children lying on cots, one pointing up.
- Emotion: Pure joy, the plea sung as a smile.
- Lighting: Golden hour in the courtyard; deep blue night on the terrace with a warm lantern.

### [verse 2]

```
Grandfather's radio and the cricket score,
The whole street leaning in to hear.
An afternoon so long it had no ending,
Kites above the roof, a string cut, a cheer.
```

- Scene: The verandah, a transistor radio on a stool, neighbours gathered.
- Shot: The grandfather turning the dial, men and children leaning in, a cheer at a boundary.
- Cut to: The roof, kites in a white sky, a string going slack, children running to the edge to watch it fall.
- Emotion: Community, the endless afternoon.
- Lighting: Harsh bright afternoon, bleached sky.

```
Grandmother's hands and the smell of pickle jars,
Drying in the sun on the top step.
She said the mangoes know when you are coming,
So she saved the sweetest one, a promise that she kept.
```

- Scene: The top step of the house, glass pickle jars drying in a row.
- Shot: A grandmother's hands, wrinkled, spooning mango pickle into a jar, then wiping the rim.
- Cut to: Her pressing one perfect ripe mango into child Mahima's hands, a finger to her lips: their secret.
- Emotion: Tenderness, the small sacred.
- Lighting: Warm, soft, morning light on the step.

### [pre-chorus]

```
Then one evening the wind turned over,
Dust rose up and the crows went quiet.
Grandmother calling from the kitchen doorway,
Bring the clothes in, bring the clothes in, run.
We ran out to the courtyard laughing,
Faces up, we caught the first of it.
```

- Scene: The courtyard at dusk, the wind rising, the light changing.
- Shot: Dust lifting off the ground, the mango leaves turning silver-side up, crows leaving a wire.
- Cut to: The grandmother pulling saris off a washing line, shouting, children running the other way into the open.
- Emotion: Electric anticipation, then release.
- Lighting: Sky turning slate grey, the last sun cutting under the cloud.

### [chorus]

```
Take me back to mango season,
Yellow hands and a sticky chin.
Take me back to mango season,
Bare feet in the courtyard, let me in.
Cousins on the terrace, cots in a row,
Counting every star we did not know.
The first rain came early that year,
Take me back to mango season, take me there.
```

- Scene: The first rain in the courtyard, seven children with their faces up.
- Shot: Slow motion of rain hitting hot dust, a child spinning with arms out.
- Cut to: The grandfather standing under the verandah roof, smiling, not calling them in.
- Emotion: Joy that is bigger than the first chorus.
- Lighting: Rain-dark grey with warm light from the house doorway.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: The monsoon proper. No lyrics, drums and flute.
- Shots: Rain sheeting off the tin roof; the courtyard flooding an inch deep and children stamping in it; the mango tree shaking; a paper boat in the gutter; the grandmother watching from the kitchen door with a steel cup of chai; the rain thinning and a single drop on a leaf.
- Lighting: Silver-grey rain light, then the first clearing.

### [bridge – quiet, near-spoken, then the decision]

```
The house is smaller now, the tree is taller,
His cycle still leans against the wall.
The cousins live in seven different cities,
Nobody writes a letter, we just call.
But every June when the heat gets heavy,
And the first cloud comes in slow,
I stand out on my balcony in the city,
Face up, and I am nine, and I still know.
So this year I am buying the ticket,
This year I am going home.
```

- Scene: Present day. Adult Mahima on a small city balcony in June, the heat visible.
- Shot: A photograph on her phone of the house, then the bicycle against the wall, rust on the frame.
- Cut to: A group chat lighting up (composited), seven names, seven cities; then her face turning up to a single cloud.
- Emotion: Ache, then resolve.
- Lighting: Hazy city heat, then a shadow crossing her face as the cloud arrives.

### [final chorus – whole step up, fullest]

```
I am going back to mango season,
Yellow hands and a sticky chin.
I am going back to mango season,
Bare feet in the courtyard, let me in.
Cousins on the terrace, cots in a row,
Same old stars, I still do not know.
The rain will come early, I can feel it,
I am going back to mango season, back to the beginning.
```

- Scene: Adult Mahima on the train, the same window bars, the same countryside.
- Shot: Her hand on the bars where the child's hand was; fields, then the outskirts of the town.
- Cut to: Intercut the child on the terrace and the adult on the train, both looking up.
- Emotion: Homecoming, certainty.
- Lighting: Golden late afternoon through the train window.

### [post-chorus – chant with claps]

```
Sweet on the tongue, salt on the skin,
Sun on the roof and the rain coming in.
Sweet on the tongue, salt on the skin,
Take me back, take me back, let me in.
```

- Scene: Fast cuts of the summer's textures.
- Shots: Mango juice, salt on a slice, a hot tin roof, rain on the courtyard, an iron gate opening.
- Emotion: Delight, rhythm.
- Lighting: Saturated saffron, green, grey-blue.

### [outro – flute and guitar, the station again]

```
The train slows down at a station with one lamp,
Nobody waiting, but I know the way.
I walk the lane with my shoes in my hand,
Somebody's children hanging off the gate.
The dust smells sweet and the air is heavy,
And the mango tree is still there today.
```

- Scene: The one-lamp station at dusk, empty platform.
- Shot: Adult Mahima stepping down with a small bag, looking at the spot where the bicycle used to wait, then walking.
- Cut to: The lane, her sandals in her hand, a new set of children on the same gate; the gate opening; the mango tree, taller, in the courtyard.
- Emotion: Peace, gratitude.
- Lighting: The amber platform lamp, then the last violet light in the courtyard.
