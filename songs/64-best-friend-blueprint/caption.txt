Global Metadata
Basic Attributes: bpm is 118. key is F major, and scale is major. Bright Uptempo Pop with plucked synths, live claps, a four-on-the-floor kick and a stadium-sized friendship-anthem chorus.
Global Emotional Progression: The track opens with a filtered plucked synth arpeggio and a single hand clap on the backbeat, the lead voice close and conversational as if she is counting years off on her fingers, warm and dry with no reverb at all. The first verse drops into a four-on-the-floor kick, a bouncing muted bass and a bright staccato synth answering every second line, quick and funny and detailed, the drums deliberately smaller than they want to be. The pre-chorus strips the kick out and pushes claps, a rising filter sweep and a stacked vocal count into the top of the phrase, all tension. The chorus lands with the full kit, real claps, a wide plucked-synth hook, a low sine bass and octave-doubled vocals, unashamedly euphoric, the sound of a room full of people shouting one name. A chanted post-chorus follows over a stripped clap-and-kick groove with a filtered vocal loop of the title word. The second verse returns smaller and slightly sharper, an eighth-note synth line and a rim-click groove, then opens two bars early as the story turns. The second pre-chorus is bigger than the first with a full clap build, and the second chorus adds a synth countermelody and a low brass stab on the downbeats. The instrumental break is sixteen to thirty-two bars of the plucked-synth hook traded against a live clap breakdown, a filtered vocal chop of the title, a hard half-time drop and a rising white-noise sweep. The bridge falls almost to nothing, a warm electric piano, a soft pad and the voice alone at half feel, the reversal where the favour comes back the other way, then builds with claps and a tom crescendo. The final chorus is the biggest moment on the record with two clap layers, a gang vocal on the title, a synth countermelody and the roles in the lyric swapped, and the last post-chorus is a full crowd chant. The outro drops the drums entirely to the opening plucked arpeggio and one dry voice, ending mid-thought on a single held note.

Dynamics are contained in the verses and open dramatically in the choruses. Generous plate and hall reverb on atmospheric elements, with the lead vocal kept close and dry for intimacy.

Arrangement

Real hand claps, recorded in a room rather than sampled, are the second lead instrument of this record and appear in every section but the outro.
