# Keys to the Same Door — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
Moving in together, treated with the weight a culture usually reserves for a proposal. Two keys cut from one blank, a buzzer panel with two names in two handwritings, a couch that does not fit up the stairs, pizza on a packing crate, and a bag half packed in a closet that finally gets unpacked. A duet: her verse is the count of everywhere she has lived alone, his verse is the couch, and both voices meet in unison on the hook.

**Mood:**
Warm, funny, quietly enormous. A domestic song sung like an anthem. Nothing here is a grand gesture and all of it is.

**Message:**
"It's the first place in my life I'm not rehearsing leaving."

**Genre:**
Warm contemporary pop with a live-room feel. Think: Vance Joy's domestic detail x the clap-and-piano communal lift of a Lord Huron chorus, with the plain duet writing of a Ben Rector record.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 104 BPM, 4/4. Key: C major, plain and open, an upright piano with the room mic left in
- Final chorus lifts a whole step to D with a group vocal and the fullest clap layers

**Vocal setup:**
- Singer A (female): intro, verse 1, the pre-choruses, the first four lines of the bridge, hook throughout. Singer B (male): verse 2, two lines of the bridge, unison on every hook, the hallway line.
- Backing: a six or seven voice group vocal on the final chorus and a chanted call and response on the post-chorus.

**Instrumentation:**
- Upright piano with the room mic open (the spine, first note to last)
- Layered handclaps, four or five takes deep, the second primary element
- Warm synth bass, muted acoustic guitar, bright ringing electric
- Low string pad under the pre-choruses, rising string line in the instrumental
- Soft kick, no cymbals anywhere in the verses, tambourine only in the last chorus
- Ambience: a key-cutting machine, a hardware-store hum, a door buzzer, footsteps on bare boards, the hollow sound of an unfurnished room

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: aisle four, a key cutter, two warm blanks of brass
- Verse 1: eight moves in nine years, and a buzzer panel with two names on it
- Pre-chorus and chorus: moving night, then the promise, communal and clapped
- Verse 2: the couch, and the first night on the floor
- Bridge: the bag half packed in the closet, and the spare keys coming off a ring
- Final chorus and outro: the bowl of keys, and the man in the green apron

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – piano and a key cutter]

```
The machine at the hardware store screams for eleven seconds,
Then it drops a warm piece of brass in my hand.
Two of them, same cuts, same jagged promise,
And I'm standing in aisle four about to cry.
```

- Scene: A big-box hardware store, mid-morning, fluorescent and unromantic.
- Shot: The key-cutting machine in extreme close-up, the wheel biting a blank, sparks and brass dust, the noise of it filling the frame.
- Cut to: A palm receiving two freshly cut keys, still warm, the metal bright at the cut edges; then her face, holding it together badly, in an aisle of paint samples.
- Her reaction / Emotion: Blindsided by the size of a small thing. She did not expect this to be the moment.
- Lighting: Flat overhead retail fluorescent, no key light, no warmth — the whole point is that nothing about the room is helping.

### [verse 1]

```
I've moved eight times in nine years by myself,
I know how to tape a box and lie about the weight.
I've handed back a key at the door of every place,
And I've never once been given one back.
The buzzer downstairs has a slot for two names now,
Yours in your handwriting and mine underneath in mine.
It's the smallest piece of paper in the building,
And I keep going down to check it's still there.
```

- Scene: Eight moves, told in eight seconds, then one buzzer panel.
- Shots: A packing-tape gun run across a box lid, three times, in three different rooms; a box being handed up a stairwell and the person taking it grimacing at the weight.
- Cut to: A montage of hands returning keys — to a landlord, into a letterbox, onto a kitchen counter — always leaving, never receiving; then the buzzer panel by a front door with a small printed card, two names in two different handwritings (composited).
- Her reaction / Emotion: The list is delivered without self-pity and then the card undoes her.
- Lighting: Every moving-out shot flat and grey; the buzzer panel lit by one entryway bulb, and it glows.

### [pre-chorus]

```
Boxes to the ceiling and the lamp's still in the car,
A pizza on a packing crate and nowhere left to sit.
The neighbors heard us laughing at eleven,
And I don't care, I'm not going anywhere.
```

- Scene: Moving night in an apartment with nothing in it.
- Shots: Boxes stacked to the ceiling in one corner; an empty lamp-shaped gap on a side table; a pizza box open on a wooden packing crate with two people sitting on the floor around it.
- Cut to: A hallway outside the door, a neighbour's light coming on, the sound of laughing through a wall.
- Her reaction / Emotion: Giddy, sleep-deprived, entirely at home in an empty room.
- Lighting: One bare bulb overhead and a phone torch propped in a mug — the ugliest and happiest lighting in the video.

### [chorus – claps, piano, both voices in unison]

```
Two keys to the same door, that's what I've been waiting for,
Not the ring, not the toast, not the ninety second speech.
Just a hallway with our shoes in it and a light we both forget,
And a lock that finally knows the two of us.
Two keys to the same door, one welcome mat, one floor,
That's what I've been waiting for.
```

- Scene: The apartment, over its first month, in one hallway.
- Shots: A key turning in a lock on the downbeat; the same hallway filling up across cuts — one pair of shoes, then three, then six, in a growing heap by the door.
- Cut to: A hall light left on all night in a shot that dissolves into the same shot in daylight, still on; a doormat being kicked straight with a foot.
- Emotion: Communal, clapped, domestic. The song's whole argument is a heap of shoes.
- Lighting: Warm interior practicals, a rectangle of daylight from the open door, dust in it.

### [verse 2 – male lead]

```
The couch didn't fit up the stairs and we knew it at the landing,
And neither of us said it for twenty solid minutes.
You wanted it against the window, I wanted it against the wall,
So we fought about a couch we couldn't physically get in.
Then we left it on the sidewalk with a sign and ate on the floor,
First night in a house with nothing in it but us,
And I never slept better on a worse mattress.
```

- Scene: The couch. Played completely straight, which is what makes it funny.
- Shots: A two-seater wedged diagonally in a stairwell at a hopeless angle; both of them on either side of it, red-faced, saying nothing, for a long uncut take.
- Cut to: An argument in a doorway conducted entirely about furniture placement, with hand diagrams; then the couch on a sidewalk with a piece of cardboard on it (blank, composited), seen from an upstairs window.
- Emotion: Frustration collapsing into laughing, then into the quietest shot in the film.
- Lighting: Harsh stairwell fluorescent for the struggle; then warm low lamp light on the floor for the meal and the mattress.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
Two toothbrushes leaning on each other in a glass,
My coat on your hook and your book on my side.
I woke at four and heard you in the kitchen,
And I wasn't scared, I just went back to sleep.
```

- Scene: The small evidence of two lives merging, at night.
- Shots: Macro of two toothbrushes in one glass, leaning together; a coat rack with one coat on the wrong hook; a paperback face down on the wrong side of a bed.
- Cut to: A dark bedroom, her eyes opening at the sound of a cupboard in another room, and closing again; a strip of kitchen light under a door.
- Her reaction / Emotion: The precise moment a noise in the house stops meaning danger.
- Lighting: Almost black, one strip of warm light under a door, nothing else.

### [chorus 2]

```
Two keys to the same door, that's what I've been waiting for,
Not the ring, not the toast, not the ninety second speech.
Just a hallway with our shoes in it and a light we both forget,
And a lock that finally knows the two of us.
Two keys to the same door, one welcome mat, one floor,
That's what I've been waiting for.
```

- Scene: The apartment becoming a home, faster.
- Shots: A shelf filling across cuts — his books, her books, then no way to tell; a fridge door acquiring photographs; a chair finally arriving to replace the packing crate.
- Cut to: The hallway again, the shoe heap now permanent, the two of them stepping over it without looking.
- Emotion: Ordinary and enormous at once.
- Lighting: Warmer than chorus one, more practicals lit, evening.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: Sixteen bars. The flat, empty, at the exact moment it is finished.
- Shots: A slow move through the whole apartment with nobody in it: the shoes, the shelf, the bowl by the door, the couch-shaped absence in the living room, a rug where a couch should be.
- Cut to: The four-bar drop — claps and one hum — over a single locked-off frame of the front door from inside, closed; then the rebuild as it opens and two people come through it carrying groceries.
- Emotion: Held breath, and then relief. The room is only right when they walk into it.
- Lighting: Daylight through one window moving across the floor, then the door opening and adding a second light source.

### [bridge – piano and voice, no percussion]

```
I kept a bag half packed at the bottom of the closet,
Just in case the room got small, just in case I had to go.
I unpacked it Tuesday, there's nothing left I could run with,
And I had spare keys on my ring for people who moved out.
I took them off this morning, there is only one now.
So the door does what a door does, it opens and it holds,
And we are the two who are allowed in.
```

- Scene: The closet, and the ring.
- Shots: A closet floor, a duffel bag half packed and pushed to the back, shot from low and close, torch-lit; her pulling it out and emptying it onto a bed, slowly, item by item.
- Cut to: The empty bag folded and put on a high shelf; then a keyring on a kitchen table with four keys on it, and three of them being worked off the ring, one at a time, and set aside.
- Her reaction / Emotion: The most private passage in the film. Nobody is watching her do either of these things.
- Lighting: One bedroom lamp for the closet; flat morning kitchen light for the keyring, which is the plainest and most decisive shot in the video.

### [final chorus – modulated, group vocal]

```
Two keys to the same door, that's what I've been waiting for,
Not the ring, not the toast, not the ninety second speech.
Just your keys in the bowl and mine on top of them,
And a lock that finally knows the two of us.
It's the first place in my life I'm not rehearsing leaving,
Two keys to the same door, one welcome mat, one floor,
That's what I've been waiting for.
```

- Scene: A housewarming, months later, in the same apartment.
- Shots: A bowl by the door with two keys in it and then a dozen as guests arrive; the hallway full of coats and shoes and people; the shelf now completely mixed.
- Cut to: The two of them across a crowded room, one look, nothing said; then the front door standing open with people coming through it continuously.
- Emotion: The biggest and warmest frames in the video, and nobody makes a speech.
- Lighting: Every practical in the flat on at once, warm, overfull, a little too bright.

### [post-chorus – chanted call and response]

```
Same door, same door, same little brass and steel,
Same door, same door, and I'm still not used to it.
Same door, same door, turn it twice and come on in,
Two keys to the same door.
```

- Scene: The door itself, doing its job.
- Shots: Four quick cuts of the same lock from the same angle — her key, his key, her key with shopping bags on her arm, his key one-handed with a coffee.
- Cut to: The door swinging open into the lit hallway and the party noise arriving with it.
- Emotion: Chant energy, entirely domestic, which is the joke and the point.
- Lighting: The stair landing dim, the doorway bright, a hard rectangle of warm light each time.

### [outro – piano and the key cutter]

```
The machine at the hardware store screams for eleven seconds,
And a man in a green apron says, good luck to you both.
He has no idea what he just handed me,
Or maybe he does, and that's why he said it like that.
```

- Scene: Back to aisle four, and the reveal.
- Shots: The key cutter again from the intro angle, the same eleven seconds; then a wider shot revealing an older man in a green apron behind the counter, sliding the two keys across.
- Cut to: His face, which is kind and completely unsurprised; then hers, catching it. The final shot is the two keys on the counter between them, close, warm, held through the fade.
- Emotion: The whole song lands on a stranger's small kindness.
- Lighting: The same flat retail fluorescent as shot one, and this time it looks warm, because nothing about it changed except her.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the key cutter biting the blank, cut to the two warm keys landing in a palm, under *"two keys to the same door."*
- **The caption line:** *"It's the first place in my life I'm not rehearsing leaving."*
- **The challenge:** the shoe-pile challenge — one fixed hallway shot, day one versus day one hundred, cut to the first chorus. Anyone who has ever moved in with anyone can shoot it in ten seconds.
- **The quote card:** *"I've handed back a key at the door of every place, and I've never once been given one back."*
- **The shareable ten seconds:** the couch, wedged diagonally in the stairwell, and twenty seconds of neither of them saying anything.
