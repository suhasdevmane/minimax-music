# Hold My Hand in Public — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
Six months of a real relationship conducted entirely off-camera: separate stairwells, a car parked round the block, a contact saved under the wrong name, an elbow at the edge of somebody else's kitchen photograph. She is not asking for a proposal or a public post. She is asking to be a person he knows, in daylight, in front of people, with a name. The song is the moment she says it out loud, and the moment he answers on a pedestrian crossing.

**Mood:**
Direct, wounded, unembarrassed. Not bitter. The chorus is a request, not an ultimatum, until the bridge — where it quietly becomes one.

**Message:**
"I don't want a secret. I want a front door."

**Genre:**
Modern pop with hard piano stabs and a very large chorus. Think: Sam Fender's plain-speech verses x Sigrid's chorus lift x the dry percussive piano of an early Adele record, produced for radio.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 104 BPM, 4/4
- Key: E flat major (bright but grounded; the piano sits in its best register here)
- No modulation. The lift is the final chorus resolving its middle lines instead of repeating them.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, flat and tired of explaining herself in the intro, wry and specific in verse one, colder in verse two, near-spoken in the bridge, full and open in every chorus.
- Backing vocals: stacked harmonies in the choruses only; the verses and the bridge are one unstacked voice, which is what makes the choruses land.

**Instrumentation:**
- Piano: dry percussive stabs on the offbeat in the verses, wide sustained chords in the choruses, alone in the intro, bridge and outro
- Finger snaps carrying the verses in place of drums
- Full kick and clap backbeat in the choruses; deep sub-bass entering only at the first chorus and never under a verse
- Bright synth pad in the choruses, high synth line in the last one
- Low string bed rising under the second chorus and the bridge
- Brass-style synth answering the piano in the instrumental break; tambourine in the final chorus only
- Textures: vinyl hiss, stairwell reverb, a written silent beat after verse two and at the end of the bridge

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the mechanics of hiding, said flatly
- Verse 1: the evidence, delivered wry
- Pre-chorus: the small size of what she is actually asking for
- Chorus: the request, enormous
- Verse 2: the birthday, the word *friend*, the hallway, the cab
- Instrumental: the city that does not know either of them
- Bridge: the real reason, no drums
- Final chorus: he answers, on a crossing
- Outro: the same kitchen photograph, with her in the middle

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – one dry piano chord, stairwell reverb]

```
Six months of back stairs and the side of the building,
Six months of your car parked around the block.
I know the code to your door and the names of your sisters,
And nobody knows mine.
```

- Scene: A concrete back stairwell in an apartment building, lit by one wired-glass window.
- Shot: Mahima on a half-landing with her back against the wall, still, waiting for a text.
- Cut to: A car parked round a corner from a building entrance, seen from a first-floor window; a keypad by a door and her fingers entering a code from memory.
- Her reaction / Emotion: Not angry. Practised, which is worse.
- Lighting: Gray-green stairwell light, no warmth anywhere.

### [verse 1a]

```
You take the elevator, I take the stairs and count to twenty,
We arrive at the same party like a coincidence.
There's a photograph of everybody in that kitchen,
And I am the elbow at the edge of it.
```

- Scene: A house party, seen entirely through the logistics of not being seen together.
- Shots: Split action — an elevator door closing on him; her going up a stairwell two steps at a time counting under her breath; the two of them entering a kitchen ninety seconds apart and greeting each other like strangers; a group photo being taken with a phone, and her at the very edge with only a shoulder and an elbow in the frame.
- Emotion: Practised, efficient, hollow.
- Lighting: Warm party interior, but she is always the least-lit person in the frame.

### [verse 1b]

```
I'm saved in your phone as somebody from work,
Which is funny, because you don't work.
And I laughed the first time, honestly I did,
But the joke has got a little older every month.
```

- Scene: The contact name.
- Shots: Extreme close-up of his phone face-up on a table, her name showing as something generic (composited); her seeing it from across the table and laughing genuinely; a fast series of the same phone in the same position across several months, the same wrong name each time, her expression flattening a little each cut.
- Emotion: A joke wearing out in real time.
- Lighting: Warm, then progressively cooler across the repeats.

### [pre-chorus 1]

```
I don't need a ring, I don't need a speech,
Don't need your whole life rearranged.
I just want to walk two blocks and be a person that you know,
In the daylight, in front of people, with a name.
```

- Scene: The smallness of the ask, made visual.
- Shots: Two other couples on an ordinary street doing entirely unremarkable things — one holding hands at a bus stop, one arguing about a menu — shot plainly and given real time; then Mahima walking alone on the same street, half a step behind him, the gap between them exactly one person wide.
- Emotion: Longing for something completely unspectacular.
- Lighting: Flat afternoon daylight.

### [chorus 1 – enormous]

```
Hold my hand in public,
Let the whole street know.
Not a message, not a maybe, not a coat on the back of a chair,
Just your hand and my hand and the ordinary air.
I don't want a secret, I want a front door,
I want to be the one you're walking in with, not the one before.
Hold my hand in public,
Let the whole street know.
```

- Scene: The chorus is imagined, not real — this is what she pictures.
- Shots: A pedestrian crossing full of people with two hands joined in the middle of it; a market street with them moving through it side by side; a front door opening; her introduced to a room and the room turning. Every one of these is shot warm and slightly wide and is over too quickly — cut back on the last line to her alone on the real street.
- Emotion: Want, at full volume.
- Lighting: Golden, generous, and revealed as imaginary by the cut back.

### [verse 2a]

```
Friday at your birthday you said, this is my friend,
And I said it back and shook your mother's hand.
I stood out in the hallway with a coat over my arm,
And I heard my own voice being polite from far away.
```

- Scene: The birthday. The video's worst night.
- Shots: A living room full of people; his mouth forming an introduction; Mahima's face receiving the word *friend* and continuing to smile; a handshake with an older woman, warm on the woman's side; Mahima in the hallway holding a coat over her arm, filmed from behind, the party audible and unreachable through a doorway.
- Emotion: The sound of your own politeness from outside your body.
- Lighting: Warm party light in the room, cold hallway, and she is in the hallway.

### [verse 2b]

```
Then I walked to the corner and I called myself a car,
And you messaged me at midnight, are you mad.
And I typed out a paragraph and sent a single word,
And I never felt so small in a room I helped set up.
```

- Scene: Leaving.
- Shots: Her walking to a corner in a coat, alone, a car pulling up; a phone lighting on her knee in the back seat (message composited); a long paragraph typed and then deleted down to one word (composited); a flashback of an hour earlier, her hanging decorations in that same living room while nobody was there; a written silent beat on the last word.
- Emotion: Small. Deliberately, specifically small.
- Lighting: Sodium street light through a moving car window; the flashback warm and bright.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
I don't need the whole world, I don't need a post,
Don't need your friends to love me by the spring.
I just want to stand beside you in a room with the lights on,
And be a person, not a rumor, not a thing.
```

- Scene: Quieter than the first, in her own flat.
- Shots: Her sitting on the edge of her bed in the coat she has not taken off; a single lamp; the same two ordinary couples from the first pre-chorus, briefly, now shot from further away.
- Emotion: Clarity arriving.
- Lighting: One lamp, everything else dark.

### [chorus 2]

Repeat of chorus 1 — the imagined shots return, but colder and shorter, and the cut back to reality comes earlier.

- Scene: The same four images, now unmistakably fantasy — slightly slowed, slightly desaturated, ending on her alone in the apartment rather than on the street.
- Emotion: The same want with less hope under it.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: The city carrying on without either of them.
- Shots: A crossing filling and emptying with strangers, timelapse; a market being set up at dawn; a stairwell with nobody in it; her walking a long block alone in daylight; his car parked round the corner again with nobody in it.
- Lighting: Cool morning, then flat noon.

### [bridge – piano and voice, no drums]

```
It isn't about the street, it isn't about the photograph,
It's that I have started making myself smaller in the door.
I check the window before I laugh, I check the room before I lean,
And a girl who used to take up space is holding still.
So if you can't, then say you can't, and I will understand,
But I am not shrinking anymore to fit inside a hand.
```

- Scene: The most honest thirty seconds in the video. One location, one subject.
- Shots: Mahima standing in a doorway in her own flat, filmed straight on, not moving; then three two-second inserts of her doing exactly what she describes — glancing at a window before laughing, checking a room before leaning toward him, physically stepping back; then back to the doorway and a hard silent beat on the last word.
- Emotion: Not a threat. A boundary, said kindly.
- Lighting: Daylight from one side, half her face dark.

### [final chorus – he answers]

```
Hold my hand in public,
Let the whole street know.
And you did, on a Thursday, on a crossing full of strangers,
With the light about to change and your fingers finding mine.
I don't want a secret, I want a front door,
And you knocked on your mother's and you said my name out loud.
Hold my hand in public,
Let the whole street know.
```

- Scene: It happens, and it is smaller and better than the fantasy.
- Shots: A real crossing, real strangers, the light counting down; his hand finding hers without ceremony and neither of them looking at it; the two of them crossing with the crowd; a front door in a suburban street; his knuckles on it; the door opening on his mother; his mouth saying her name; the two of them going in.
- Emotion: Relief, and the total ordinariness of it.
- Lighting: Real Thursday afternoon light, no golden filter — deliberately less pretty than the fantasy choruses.

### [post-chorus]

```
Let the whole street know, let the whole street know,
Take the long way home and take it slow.
Let the whole street know, let the whole street know,
It was never about the street, but let them know.
```

- Scene: The walk home, taking the long way.
- Shots: Four images on the claps: their hands, still joined, at hip height; a store window with both of them reflected; a street they have never walked together; the same corner where his car used to hide, empty.
- Lighting: Late afternoon going gold, for real this time.

### [outro – dry piano, the room sounding full]

```
Six months of back stairs, and then a Thursday in the sun,
Your car out at the front and both the windows down.
There's a photograph of everybody in that kitchen,
And I'm the one in the middle, and I've got a name.
```

- Scene: The bookend. Every intro image, answered.
- Shots: The back stairwell, empty and unused; his car parked at the front of the building with the windows down; a kitchen, another party, another group photo being taken — and this time she is in the middle of it; final shot on the photograph being taken, the flash, and the frame holding on the room afterwards.
- Emotion: Settled. Ordinary. Enormous.
- Lighting: Warm kitchen light, the fullest-sounding room in the film.

## Viral moments

- **The hook.** *"Hold my hand in public, let the whole street know"* — a hard-launch caption, ready made.
- **The line for captions.** *"I don't want a secret, I want a front door."*
- **The knife line.** *"I'm saved in your phone as somebody from work, which is funny, because you don't work."*
- **The turn.** *"A girl who used to take up space is holding still."*
- **The challenge.** The crossing shot: film the moment your hands join on a pedestrian crossing with the light counting down, cut on the beat.
- **The bookend.** The kitchen photo with her at the edge, then the same photo with her in the middle — the two frames cut back to back.
