# Pretty When You Laugh — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
She fell for the sound before she ever saw the face. The song is a list of every time it has happened since: the birds off the wire, the stranger on the bus, the hand clamped over his mouth, the funeral where he laughed first and let a whole room breathe. It is a love song addressed to a laugh, and by the last line it has quietly become a song about how you keep somebody.

**Mood:**
Warm, funny, generous, and in two places genuinely serious. Pop-soul with a live band in a room. The comedy is entirely in the specifics; nothing here is a gag.

**Message:**
"Everybody falls for a face. I fell for a sound, and the sound is the part that lasts."

**Genre:**
Warm pop-soul with a live horn section and gospel-tinged backing vocals. Think: Leon Bridges x Lianne La Havas x the horn-arrangement warmth of a Sharon Jones record, produced with the room left in.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 90 BPM, 4/4
- Key: A flat major (rich, horn-friendly, sits low and warm for the lead)
- No modulation. The final chorus lifts by adding the full horn section, the full choir and a handclap layer, and by rewriting its middle two lines to look thirty years forward.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, close and amused in the verses, quiet and careful in the funeral verse, full and open in the choruses, almost spoken on the last line of the record.
- Backing vocals: gospel-tinged answering vocals from the first chorus, a full choir stack in the last two choruses and the post-chorus chant. The second verse has none at all.

**Instrumentation:**
- Rhodes electric piano: alone in the intro and outro, answering every second line, carrying the bridge
- Live horn section — trumpet, tenor and baritone saxophone, trombone — defining every chorus and taking the break
- Fat round electric bass and a soft, deep-pocketed drum kit
- Muted rhythm guitar chopping on the offbeat through the verses
- Hammond-style organ pad lifting the pre-choruses and widening the choruses
- Tambourine from the first pre-chorus; handclaps in the final chorus and post-chorus
- One sustained low string under the bridge
- Textures: a wide-open room mic with chair creak and breath left in, and a muffled laugh through a wall at the very start and again under the last two lines

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the sound through a wall, before any face
- Verse 1: the catalog of the laugh, comic and specific
- Pre-chorus: what she is willing to do for it
- Chorus: the thesis, full horns, joyful
- Verse 2: February, the funeral, the story about the boat and the dog
- Instrumental: horn arrangement and a baritone sax solo
- Bridge: everyone else fell for a face
- Final chorus: thirty years ahead, biggest arrangement
- Outro: the fourteen seconds, and what she wants done with them

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – Rhodes alone, room mic wide open]

```
I heard you before I saw you, through a wall in someone's house,
Some joke I never got the start of, and a sound came out.
Big and dumb and honest and completely out of key,
And I thought, whoever that is, I want to know what's funny.
```

- Scene: A house party from the wrong side of a wall.
- Shot: A hallway, a closed door with warm light under it, Mahima standing outside it with a drink, not moving.
- Cut to: Her head turning toward the door; her hand on the door frame; the door opening onto a room of people, all out of focus.
- Her reaction / Emotion: Curiosity, then something more specific than curiosity.
- Lighting: Cool hallway, warm room beyond, the contrast doing all the work.

### [verse 1a]

```
You laugh like a door coming off its hinge,
Like a kettle, like a seagull, like a bad idea.
You put your whole hand over your mouth like you're embarrassed,
Then it comes out through your fingers anyway.
```

- Scene: The laugh, cataloged. Four literal comic cutaways, played straight.
- Shots: A door swinging badly on one hinge; a kettle reaching the whistle; a seagull on a railing; then Kai's whole hand clamped over his own mouth and the sound escaping through his fingers anyway, close-up, entirely delighted.
- Emotion: Affectionate cataloging. The cutaways are literal and should be shot beautifully, not as jokes.
- Lighting: Warm interiors and one bright exterior for the seagull.

### [verse 1b]

```
It scared the birds up off the wire at the bus stop,
It made a stranger on the crosstown bus turn around.
You crack before the punchline every single time,
And I have never wanted anything more in my life.
```

- Scene: The laugh in public.
- Shots: A line of birds on a wire going up all at once; a stranger on a bus turning around two rows ahead, faceless and unbothered; Kai losing it halfway through telling a story and never getting to the end of it; Mahima watching him do it with an expression that gives her away completely.
- Emotion: Public embarrassment on his side, none at all on hers.
- Lighting: Flat afternoon daylight; the bus interior cool and the two of them the warmest thing in it.

### [pre-chorus 1]

```
So I say the stupid thing, I do the stupid voice,
I'd fall down a whole flight of stairs on purpose.
I have got no pride, no shame and no material,
And I'll take a bad review just to hear it once.
```

- Scene: Her working for it.
- Shots: Mahima doing an extremely bad impression at a kitchen table; her mouthing a stupid voice with total commitment; a very short shot of her deliberately falling into a sofa; and a moment where the joke lands nowhere and she takes it well.
- Emotion: Willing to be the least dignified person in the room.
- Lighting: Warm kitchen, practical lamps, the room mic feel of the record made visual.

### [chorus 1 – full horns]

```
You're pretty when you laugh,
So I'll never stop trying.
Say something back to me, I'll set you up all night,
I'll be the worst comedian in town and call it dying.
Give me the wheeze, give me the eyes going gone,
Give me the two seconds of silence before it comes.
You're pretty when you laugh,
So I'll never stop trying.
```

- Scene: The chorus is a portrait. Kai laughing, filmed like a love letter, intercut with the room around him.
- Shots: Slow-motion of him actually laughing, eyes gone, head back; the two seconds of silence before it starts, held on his face; a table of friends going up around him; her, watching, not laughing at the joke at all, laughing at him.
- Emotion: Joy, unembarrassed, at full volume.
- Lighting: Warm gold, horn-section energy, the widest and brightest interiors in the film.

### [verse 2a]

```
February, and your uncle's funeral, and the coats,
And your mother saying nobody's eaten anything all day.
Then somebody told a story about a boat and a dog,
And you went first, and everybody followed you.
```

- Scene: The video's serious sequence, and the one that earns the whole song.
- Shots: A hallway hung with too many black coats; a table of untouched food; a room of people standing in small silent groups; an older man beginning a story with his hands; Kai's face breaking first; then the room going one by one, a laugh moving through it like weather.
- Emotion: Grief and relief in the same shot.
- Lighting: Gray February light through net curtains, everything desaturated except faces.

### [verse 2b]

```
I watched a room of people put their shoulders down at once,
And I understood what you are actually for.
That night you said, was that alright, and I said, that was the best of you,
And you laughed at that as well, quietly, in the dark.
```

- Scene: The realization, and then a bedroom at the end of that day.
- Shots: A wide of the whole room, every set of shoulders visibly dropping at once, shot from a doorway; Mahima's face understanding something; then night, a dark bedroom, the two of them lying awake, only the shape of them visible; his quiet laugh in the dark with no picture on it at all — hold on black for a beat.
- Emotion: Love arriving as comprehension rather than as feeling.
- Lighting: The wide is the coldest frame in the video; the bedroom is almost entirely dark.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
So I keep a voice note in a folder on my phone,
Fourteen seconds of you losing it at nothing.
On the days I can't reach you and the days I can't reach me,
I put it on, and the whole place starts to move.
```

- Scene: The voice note.
- Shots: Extreme close-up of a phone, a folder, one audio file, a waveform (UI composited); her thumb pressing play; her in a stairwell at work with one earbud in, starting to smile against her will; a bad morning on a bus where it does not fix anything and she plays it anyway.
- Emotion: A private object, treated as precious.
- Lighting: Fluorescent stairwell, gray bus window, and the smile the only warm thing in either.

### [chorus 2]

Repeat of chorus 1 — bigger, horns answering line for line, the portrait continued.

- Scene: More of the same portrait, in more places: a car, a kitchen, a pub table, a supermarket aisle.
- Shots: Him laughing in each, always caught, never performed; her in the edge of frame in every one.
- Emotion: Accumulation. Lighting: warm everywhere, deliberately unglamorous locations.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: The horn arrangement, and a montage with no dialogue.
- Shots: A baritone sax player in a room with the band, actually playing; then a run of very short clips of Kai laughing across what is clearly several years, hair different, seasons different; then Mahima's face at the end of the run, still watching.
- Lighting: Band room warm and low; the clip run varies wildly on purpose, like real footage.

### [bridge – Rhodes and voice, one low string]

```
People say it's the eyes, people say it's the smile,
People marry a jawline and wonder what went wrong.
But a face is just a face on a cold gray morning in November,
And a laugh is a whole climate, and you brought yours along.
So keep the eyes, keep the smile, keep whatever they were selling,
I fell for the sound, and I would fall for it again.
```

- Scene: Direct address. One location, almost no cuts.
- Shots: Mahima at the kitchen table, alone, straight to camera for the only time in the film; two two-second inserts — a magazine-perfect face on a poster, deliberately lifeless; a cold November street with nothing happening on it; then back to her for the last two lines with no cut at all.
- Emotion: Certainty. Not a speech, an explanation.
- Lighting: One window, side light, the room otherwise dim.

### [final chorus – whole horn section, full choir, claps]

```
You're pretty when you laugh,
So I'll never stop trying.
Thirty years from now I'll still be working on the timing,
Still be dying at the table, still be trying.
Give me the wheeze, give me the eyes going gone,
Give me the two seconds of silence before it comes.
You're pretty when you laugh,
So I'll never stop trying.
```

- Scene: Thirty years ahead, without changing the actors — done entirely with staging.
- Shots: The same kitchen table, now with more chairs and more people around it, children among them; Mahima telling a joke to a full table and getting it slightly wrong; Kai going anyway; the whole table going; a handclap layer visible in the frame; the two of them at the end of the table, the oldest thing in the room being the joke.
- Emotion: Full, loud, celebratory.
- Lighting: The warmest and busiest frames in the video.

### [post-chorus – call and response over claps]

```
I'll never stop trying, no I'll never stop trying,
Tell me it's not funny, I'll go get another one.
I'll never stop trying, no I'll never stop trying,
You're pretty when you laugh and I am never going to stop.
```

- Scene: Four call-and-response images on the claps — the table clapping, a child copying the laugh, Kai's hand over his mouth again, her walking out of the room already thinking of the next one.
- Lighting: Warm, high energy.

### [outro – Rhodes alone, room mic wide]

```
I heard you before I saw you, through a wall in someone's house,
And I've heard it every day since and I still turn around.
If you go before me, leave the fourteen seconds on,
And I'll play it in an empty room and swear you're still in it.
```

- Scene: The bookend, and the ending nobody expects from a funny song.
- Shots: The same hallway and closed door from the intro, matched exactly, with the laugh audible through it; Mahima turning her head toward it the same way she did at the start; then a quiet room, a phone on a table, the voice note playing to nobody; final shot on the empty room with the sound in it, holding through the fade.
- Emotion: Tender, and only sad if you want it to be.
- Lighting: Cool hallway, then one warm lamp in an otherwise empty room.

## Viral moments

- **The hook.** *"You're pretty when you laugh, so I'll never stop trying"* — a caption, a card, a wedding-speech line.
- **The line for captions.** *"A face is just a face on a cold gray morning in November, and a laugh is a whole climate."*
- **The knife line.** *"If you go before me, leave the fourteen seconds on."*
- **The funeral verse.** *"You went first, and everybody followed you"* — the most-quoted four lines in the song and the reason it is not just charming.
- **The challenge.** Post the voice note: fourteen seconds of somebody you love laughing at nothing, over the chorus.
- **The portrait.** The chorus is a slow-motion portrait of a man laughing, filmed like a love letter — a format anyone can copy with a phone.
