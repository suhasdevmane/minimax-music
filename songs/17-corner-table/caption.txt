Global Metadata
Basic Attributes: bpm is 110. key is C major, and scale is major. Understated Indie-Pop with a warm live-band feel, a quiet café love story told across four seasons.
Global Emotional Progression: The track opens small and domestic, a warm clean electric guitar picking a two-bar figure over the hush of a café, cups and a milk steamer buried low in the mix, the lead voice close and half-smiling as she describes the chair, the window and the book she never reads. The first verse keeps it intimate, an upright piano joining the guitar with a soft kick and a brushed rim click, September rain shaken off a jacket, two people pretending not to look. The pre-chorus tightens, the bass entering with a walking pulse and the guitar strumming instead of picking, the rhythm of showing up at ten every week, and it lifts into an open, mid-tempo chorus with the full kit, layered guitars and soft stacked harmonies, warm and sure rather than loud, the hook landing on the word down each time. The second verse advances the story, November and the order he already knew, then the street lights going up and the two of them sharing one table, then January and a door that keeps opening on the wrong person, the arrangement thinning back under those lines to piano and a single guitar. The second pre-chorus is quieter and more stubborn, the foam going flat, and the second chorus is fuller than the first with a low string pad beneath it. The instrumental break is the guitar figure over piano and brushed drums, a gentle wordless vocal line drifting over the top, the sound of a café in winter, ending on a held chord and the small ring of a shop bell. The bridge is spring, near-spoken at first over piano only, the window open, the bell above the door, his explanation and her laugh, and it builds line by line as the kit and bass return until she says sit down. The final chorus is the biggest moment of the song, restrained strings entering for the first time, violins and a cello line under the layered harmonies, the napkin and the number, the hook repeated twice, then a post-chorus that pulls back to guitar, piano and voice for the barista and the two leaves in the foam. The outro returns to the intro figure alone, warm and settled, two coffees instead of one, the last line repeated softly and left hanging on the guitar.

Modern pop-R&B polish: controlled low end with weighty sub-bass and 808s, scooped low-mids for vocal clarity, crisp airy top end. Dynamics are contained in the verses and open dramatically in the choruses. Generous plate and hall reverb on atmospheric elements, with the lead vocal kept close and dry for intimacy.
