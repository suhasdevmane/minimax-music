# Cry in the Uber — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
The ride home after it ends. She holds herself together for the length of a goodbye, gets into a stranger's car in the rain, and gives herself exactly one journey to fall apart in — the driver's small unasked-for kindnesses, the city smeared across wet glass, and a decision, somewhere over the river, to be alright by the time she reaches her own front door.

**Mood:**
Quiet, wet, contained. British restraint cracking in a back seat. Devastating without ever raising its voice.

**Message:**
"I'm not fine, but I'm fine enough to open my own door."

**Genre:**
Sad pop and cinematic downtempo, piano-led, deep sub, real rain. Think: Adele x Sampha x RAYE — London specificity, a felt piano, and strings that swell instead of drop.

**Tempo & Key:**
90 BPM, 4/4, F minor throughout. No modulation; the final chorus lifts by arrangement alone — full kit, second string layer, widest harmonies — because the song's whole argument is that nothing was solved, only survived.

**Vocal setup:**
Lead: young adult female, tight and held back in the intro, conversational and cracked in the verses, high in the chest voice and staying there through the choruses. Backing: stacked harmonies from the second chorus, widest on the last; wordless echoes through the outro.

**Instrumentation:**
- Felt piano (the spine)
- Deep sub-bass, running like an engine under everything
- Wide cinematic strings; a second layer from chorus two
- Solo cello from verse two, answering the piano in the break
- Warm choir pad
- Soft heartbeat kick and brushed hats at wiper speed; full kit only in the final chorus
- Ambient: rain on glass, wet-road hiss, indicator ticks, a distant siren, passing traffic, a car door, a key, a kettle

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the pavement, the rain, the small blue dot
- Verse one: getting in, and the driver noticing without saying so
- Pre-chorus: the eleven minutes she held her face together
- Chorus: permission, granted to herself, for one journey only
- Verse two: the tissues at the lights, and the quiet sentence that ended it
- Bridge: the crying stops on its own, near the bridge, and the city carries on
- Final chorus: past tense, and she means it
- Outro: the hallway, the shoes still on, the hall light on

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro — rain and felt piano, no drums]

```
On the pavement in the rain outside your door,
Watching a small blue dot come crawling round the block,
Three minutes, then two, then none,
And I hold it in until the door clicks shut.
```

- Scene: A wet residential street in south London at night. A front door behind her with the hall light on inside it.
- Shot: Her from behind on the pavement in the rain, phone lit in her hand, coat not done up, nothing dramatic in her posture.
- Cut to: A phone screen, composited, a small blue dot crawling round a block. Then a car arriving, headlights sweeping her legs, the door opening and closing.
- Emotion: Total control, held at enormous cost.
- Lighting: Sodium orange, rain in the beams, the door's warm interior light going out as it shuts.

### [verse 1 — kick and sub enter]

```
He says you alright, love, is it Peckham, is that right,
I say yes, and my voice comes out in bits.
A bottle of water in the door, a charger on the seat,
A cardboard pine tree turning where the mirror sits.
He turns the radio down before I have to ask,
Puts the heating on my side and doesn't say a word.
The wipers keep a rhythm that my ribs are trying to match,
And the whole of south London goes by, blurred.
```

- Scene: Inside the car, and inside it for most of the video.
- Shot: The driver from behind and in fragments only — a hand on the wheel, an ear, a shoulder, eyes in the rear-view strip. He never gets a full face.
- Cut to: The bottle of water in the door pocket; the cardboard pine tree turning; his hand reaching for the volume dial without looking.
- Emotion: Being cared for by someone who owes her nothing, which is what breaks her.
- Lighting: Dashboard green, streetlights strobing across her face at intervals, everything else black.

### [pre-chorus 1 — kick drops out]

```
Not on your stairs, not in your street,
Not while your kitchen light was still on.
I kept my face together for eleven minutes,
And now the door is shut and you're gone.
```

- Scene: A rapid rewind to the eleven minutes she is talking about.
- Shot: Her in his kitchen, coat already on, standing very straight. His hands on a counter; his face never in frame.
- Cut to: Her on the stairs going down, holding the rail, absolutely composed; a kitchen light going off behind a window.
- Emotion: The cost of composure, itemised.
- Lighting: Warm domestic light behind her, cold blue in front.

### [chorus 1 — strings swell, no drop]

```
Let me cry in the Uber, I'll be fine by my door,
Half an hour, that's all, I won't ask for more.
Let the streetlights run like water down the glass,
Let a kind man with the heating on let it pass.
Let me cry in the Uber all the way through town,
I'll be fine by my door, I just can't be fine now.
```

- Scene: The crying, finally, filmed with no shame in it.
- Shot: Her face against the window in profile, streetlights running down the glass across her skin, held for a very long time.
- Cut to: The city through the wet window — brake lights, a bus, a chicken shop, a bridge — all abstract and smeared.
- Emotion: Relief that hurts. Permission granted to herself.
- Lighting: Every colour in the video happens on this glass.

### [verse 2 — cello enters]

```
At the lights he passes back a packet of tissues,
Doesn't look in the mirror, keeps his eyes on the road ahead.
Says the traffic's proper bad round Elephant and Castle,
Which is the kindest thing that anyone has said.
And I think about your kitchen and how you said it,
Like a thing that you'd rehearsed on the way back from the shop.
No shout, no slammed door, no reason I can hold,
Just a quiet, I think that we should stop.
```

- Scene: The single kindest gesture in the video, and the memory it unlocks.
- Shot: At a red light, his arm coming back between the seats holding a packet of tissues, his eyes staying forward. Her taking them.
- Cut to: The kitchen memory again, but closer — his mouth saying a short sentence, no shouting, a carrier bag still on the counter with the shopping in it.
- Emotion: Being handled gently by a stranger and abruptly by someone who loved her.
- Lighting: Red light filling the car, then the kitchen's flat honest bulb.

### [pre-chorus 2 — slow rising filter]

```
Not in your hallway, not on your stairs,
Not with your neighbours coming in from the rain.
I held it through the goodbye and the going,
And now the New Cross Road can take the strain.
```

- Scene: The last of the rewind, then back to the road.
- Shot: A communal hallway, neighbours shaking out umbrellas, her walking past them with her face perfect.
- Cut to: The car on a long wet arterial road at speed, filmed from outside, tail lights and spray.
- Emotion: Handing the weight to the road.
- Lighting: Wet tarmac reflecting everything; the car is the only warm thing in frame.

### [chorus 2 — second string layer]

```
Let me cry in the Uber, I'll be fine by my door,
Half an hour, that's all, I won't ask for more.
Let the streetlights run like water down the glass,
Let a kind man with the heating on let it pass.
Let me cry in the Uber all the way through town,
I'll be fine by my door, I just can't be fine now.
```

- Scene: The same journey, deeper into it, the crying at its worst.
- Shot: A very slow push from the front passenger footwell up to her face, the whole city passing behind her.
- Cut to: Her hands in her lap holding the tissue packet without opening it again; the water bottle untouched.
- Emotion: The bottom of it.
- Lighting: One long stretch with no streetlights at all, her face almost gone in the dark.

### [instrumental — piano, cello, traffic, then rain alone]

- Scene: The river crossing, and the only beautiful sequence in the video.
- Shot: The car going over a bridge, filmed from outside and above, the water black and the lights on it in a long gold line.
- Cut to: Her face turning to look at it properly for the first time; the driver's eyes in the mirror strip noticing that she has stopped.
- Final beat: A bar where only rain is audible, on a static shot of the windscreen with the wipers stopping mid-sweep at a red light.
- Lighting: Gold on black. The first warm exterior light in the piece.

### [bridge — close voice, one held sub note, then rebuild]

```
Near the bridge the crying stops of its own accord,
The way rain does, without deciding to.
The lights come off the water in a long gold line,
And the city carries on without you.
He says, that's you there, love, mind how you go,
And I tip him more than I have and tell him so.
The rain has come to nothing by the time I reach my floor,
And I'm not fine, but I'm fine enough to open my door.
```

- Scene: Arrival. The rain stopping without ceremony.
- Shot: Her face, dry now, not repaired, just finished; the passing lights slowing as the car pulls in.
- Cut to: The phone screen and a tip being added (composited); her saying thank you properly; the door opening onto a street where the rain has stopped and everything is dripping.
- Emotion: The turn — not healing, just the end of the crying.
- Lighting: Streetlight through a wet windscreen, then clean night air.

### [final chorus — full kit, widest harmonies]

```
Let me cry in the Uber, I'll be fine by my door,
Half an hour, that's all, I won't ask for more.
Let the streetlights run like water down the glass,
Let a kind man with the heating on let it pass.
I didn't text you, I didn't ring, I didn't say a thing,
I cried in the Uber all the way through town,
And I'm fine by my door, I'm fine, I'm fine now.
```

- Scene: The walk from the kerb to her own front door, which takes the whole chorus.
- Shot: Tracking behind her up a wet path, keys already out, the car pulling away behind her and its tail lights leaving frame.
- Cut to: Her at the door, key in the lock, one deep breath, the door opening onto her own hall light.
- Emotion: Arrival as an achievement.
- Lighting: The warmest of the video — her own hallway, seen from outside in the rain-washed dark.

### [post-chorus — piano and pad, soft tag]

```
Five stars for the man who let me be a mess,
Five stars for the rain on the glass,
Five stars for the key and the hall light on,
And a girl who got herself home at last.
```

- Scene: The rating screen, and the things being thanked.
- Shot: A phone on a hall table, a rating being given (composited), then the phone going face-down.
- Cut to: Three quick reprises, warmer than they were — the glass, the pine tree, the key.
- Emotion: Gratitude, small and real.
- Lighting: Hall light only, everything else off.

### [outro — piano alone, then a door and a kettle]

```
Kettle on, coat on the floor, phone face down,
Tomorrow I'll be someone who cried in a car and lived.
Tonight I'm a woman in a hallway with her shoes still on,
And the hall light's on, and the worst of it has gone.
```

- Scene: The hallway, and nothing further.
- Shot: Her sitting on the bottom stair with her coat off and her shoes still on, a kettle boiling out of frame.
- Cut to: The wet coat on the floor; the phone face-down on the hall table.
- Final frame: Her from behind on the stair with the hall light above her, held until the piano stops and the kettle clicks off. No text.
- Emotion: Not repaired. Home.
