# Forever Looks Like Tuesday — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
A duet about the version of forever nobody photographs. Not the wedding, not the proposal, not the anniversary dinner — the Tuesday afternoon with nothing on the calendar, when he is halfway inside a kitchen cupboard with a wrench and she is on the sofa losing an argument with a tax form. Both of them mean it when they say they would not trade it, and the second verse proves why: they have already survived a much worse Tuesday.

**Mood:**
Warm, plain-spoken, funny in the small details and quietly devastating in the middle. Country-pop with no irony in it anywhere.

**Message:**
"Forever isn't the party. It's getting through the week, over and over, with the same person."

**Genre:**
Country-pop ballad with close male-female harmony. Think: Kacey Musgraves x The Civil Wars x the plain-language storytelling of a Chris Stapleton verse, produced modern with a soft pop low end.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 84 BPM, 4/4
- Key: D major (open, guitar-friendly, warm)
- No modulation. The lift is the whole band returning on a single crash after a drumless bridge.

**Vocal setup:**
- Singer A, young adult female: lead on the intro, verse one and the outro; top line of every harmony.
- Singer B, male baritone: lead on verse two; a third below her in every chorus; trades a line at a time with her through the bridge.
- The chorus is always both voices. The bridge is the only place they alternate.

**Instrumentation:**
- Fingerpicked acoustic guitar, opening and closing the record and running underneath every section
- Pedal-steel-style electric guitar as the arrangement's second voice, answering every second line and taking the solo
- Warm upright-feeling bass; brushed snare with a soft kick on the two and four
- Clean Telecaster-style electric in the choruses; mandolin-style tremolo in the pre-choruses
- Fiddle-style line entering late in the instrumental and staying to the end
- Low string bed under the second and final choruses; tambourine in the post-chorus
- Textures: kitchen room tone, a very low dripping faucet that stops for good after the bridge, a radio playing in another room

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the house, its small unfinished jobs
- Verse 1: her Tuesday, in objects
- Pre-chorus: what she was told forever would look like
- Chorus: both voices, the thesis, warm and wide
- Verse 2: his turn, and the hospital parking lot
- Instrumental: pedal steel solo, the house in daylight
- Bridge: no drums, a line each, everything they will take
- Final chorus: full band, the faucet fixed
- Outro: the list one line shorter

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – fingerpicked guitar, one steel note, no drums]

```
The faucet in the kitchen has been dripping since the spring,
You've been meaning to look at it since May.
There's a light bulb out above the stairs we walk around,
And a list up on the fridge that never gets shorter.
```

- Scene: A lived-in house at eleven in the morning. Nobody in frame yet.
- Shot: Macro on a kitchen faucet releasing one slow drop into a steel sink.
- Cut to: A dark stairwell with one dead bulb; a fridge door covered in a list, a takeout menu and a photo.
- Her reaction / Emotion: Affection, not complaint. The house is the third character.
- Lighting: Flat honest daylight through a net curtain, no grade.

### [verse 1a]

```
It's a Tuesday, and there's nothing on the calendar at all,
Just the trash, and the bank, and a form I have to sign.
You've got the cupboard open and your shoulder in the pipe,
I've got the laptop on my knees and I am losing at the taxes.
```

- Scene: The two of them, twelve feet apart, doing entirely different jobs.
- Shots: A wall calendar with an empty Tuesday square; a trash can wheeled out to the curb; the male lead flat on the kitchen floor with his head and one shoulder inside the sink cupboard, only his legs and a flashlight visible; the female lead cross-legged on the sofa with a laptop and a paper form, glasses on, entirely defeated.
- Emotion: Companionable, ordinary, funny.
- Lighting: Daylight from two windows, warm on her, cooler on the kitchen floor.

### [verse 1b]

```
There's a shirt of yours drying on the radiator wrong,
And the good scissors are somewhere neither of us can name.
Nobody's taking pictures, nobody got dressed up,
And I wouldn't trade this afternoon for anything.
```

- Scene: The house's small evidence.
- Shots: A shirt dried into a bad shape over a radiator; a drawer opened, closed, and another drawer opened, in search of scissors that never appear; a wide of the whole room with neither of them looking at each other and both of them completely at ease; her looking up from the laptop at his legs sticking out of the cupboard and smiling to herself.
- Emotion: The thesis, delivered by a look.
- Lighting: Midday, plain, real.

### [pre-chorus 1]

```
They told me it would look like a church and a dress,
Rice in my hair and your hand holding mine.
And it did, for a day. But mostly it looks like this,
Two people and a Tuesday and a job half done.
```

- Scene: The one wedding sequence, and it is deliberately brief.
- Shots: Three fast warm flashes — a church door, rice in the air, two hands — each under a second, shot beautifully and given no time at all; then a hard cut back to the kitchen floor and the wrench.
- Emotion: The wedding was real and lovely and it is not the point.
- Lighting: The flashes are overexposed and golden; the return is plain daylight.

### [chorus 1 – both voices, full band]

```
Forever looks like Tuesday,
And Tuesday looks like you.
Wet hands, a wrench, a radio on low,
And a coffee going cold and a whole afternoon to go.
Nobody writes it down, nobody makes a fuss,
But the ordinary hours are the best of us.
Forever looks like Tuesday,
And Tuesday looks like you.
```

- Scene: The house in motion, the two of them moving around each other.
- Shots: Wet hands under a running faucet; the wrench turning; a radio on a windowsill; a mug of coffee going cold beside the laptop with a skin on it; laundry being carried through a doorway; the two of them passing in the hall and each moving aside for the other without looking up.
- Emotion: Warm, wide, entirely unspectacular and completely full.
- Lighting: Sun moving across the room over the course of the chorus.

### [verse 2a – male lead]

```
I thought forever came with fireworks and a plane,
Some big loud proof that you could point to on a wall.
Then the winter that your father was in the hospital,
And we ate out in the parking lot and you slept against my coat.
```

- Scene: His turn, and the video's only serious sequence.
- Shots: A framed photo on a shelf, deliberately not shown clearly; then a hard cut to a hospital parking lot at night in winter, a car with the engine off, two takeout containers on a dashboard, breath on the inside of the windshield; her asleep against his shoulder in the passenger seat, his coat over her.
- Emotion: The weight underneath the whole song.
- Lighting: Sodium parking-lot orange through a fogged windshield; the only night sequence.

### [verse 2b]

```
That was a Tuesday too. Nobody sang about it.
You held the paper cup with both your hands and you were fine.
And I knew right then, whatever this thing is,
It isn't in the party, it's in getting through the week.
```

- Scene: Still in the parking lot.
- Shots: Macro on a paper cup held in two hands; her face lit by a vending machine, tired and steady; his face watching her and understanding something; the two of them walking back toward a lit hospital door, small in a wide.
- Emotion: Not sad. Certain.
- Lighting: Vending-machine white, then the hospital doors.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
They told us it would look like a stage and a speech,
Somebody's mother crying in the second row.
And it did, for an hour. But mostly it looks like this,
Two people and a Tuesday and a job half done.
```

- Scene: Back to daylight, and a second brief wedding flash.
- Shots: A microphone on a stand at a reception; a woman crying happily in a second row; a hand raising a glass — all under a second each; then the kitchen floor again, the flashlight, the wrench.
- Emotion: The same argument, made again, gently.
- Lighting: Warm flashes, plain return.

### [chorus 2]

Repeat of chorus 1 — later in the day, longer shadows.

- Scene: The same house an hour on. Shots: the same six actions in the same order, all with the light lower and warmer; the coffee mug now empty; the cupboard now half emptied onto the floor around him.
- Emotion: Bigger, with the hospital sitting underneath it now.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: The house alone, then them.
- Shots: Dust in a sunbeam over the sofa; the list on the fridge with one item now crossed out; the pile of shoes by the front door; the pedal steel's long note over a slow tracking shot down the hall; both of them at the kitchen table with the faucet parts spread out between them, laughing at something that has clearly gone wrong.
- Lighting: Late afternoon, gold coming in low.

### [bridge – no drums, a line each]

```
I'll take the faucet that drips, I'll take the burnt-out bulb,
I'll take the taxes and the form you never signed.
I'll take your terrible singing coming through the wall,
I'll take the way you fall asleep in the middle of the film.
I'll take the trash night and the shoes piled by the door,
I'll take the way you always buy too many eggs.
And if forever's just a lot of Tuesdays in a row,
Then I'll take Tuesday, and I'll take it slow.
```

- Scene: The two of them on the kitchen floor with their backs against the cupboards, side by side, trading lines.
- Shots: One long two-shot held for the first four lines with no cut at all; then single close-ups alternating on each line; the last two lines back on the two-shot, both of them looking straight ahead rather than at each other.
- Emotion: A vow, delivered while sitting on lino.
- Lighting: One warm kitchen light on and the daylight going; the softest lighting in the film.

### [final chorus – full band, single crash]

```
Forever looks like Tuesday,
And Tuesday looks like you.
Wet hands, a wrench, a radio on low,
And the faucet has stopped its dripping and there's still a night to go.
Nobody writes it down, nobody makes a fuss,
But the ordinary hours are the best of us.
Forever looks like Tuesday,
And Tuesday looks like you.
```

- Scene: The faucet works. The bulb goes in. Evening.
- Shots: The faucet turned on and off cleanly, no drip, macro; a hand at the top of a stepladder fitting a bulb and the stairwell filling with light; the two of them eating standing up at the counter; the radio turned up; a genuinely bad slow dance across the hall that neither of them commits to.
- Emotion: Full, warm, celebratory in the smallest possible way.
- Lighting: Warm interior lights on for the first time in the video.

### [post-chorus]

```
Tuesday, Tuesday, and the week rolling on,
Tuesday, Tuesday, and I want every one.
Tuesday, Tuesday, and the light turning gold,
Forever looks like Tuesday, and I'll take it till we're old.
```

- Scene: Four warm images on the tambourine.
- Shots: The calendar page turning to the next week; the trash cans going back in; the shoes by the door; the two of them on the sofa, one asleep already.
- Lighting: Last gold of the day through the window.

### [outro – fingerpicked guitar, both voices soft]

```
The faucet in the kitchen isn't dripping anymore,
There's a bulb above the stairs and we can see the floor.
The list up on the fridge is one line shorter than it was,
And that is what a good life looks like, I suppose.
```

- Scene: The house at the end of the day, matching the intro shot for shot.
- Shots: The same macro on the faucet, now dry; the same stairwell, now lit; the same fridge list with one line crossed through; final locked-off wide of the kitchen with both of them just out of frame and the light going, holding through the fade.
- Emotion: Quiet, complete.
- Lighting: The intro's flat daylight replaced by one warm lamp.

## Viral moments

- **The hook.** *"Forever looks like Tuesday, and Tuesday looks like you"* — an anniversary caption, a wedding-video title card, a first-dance line.
- **The bridge list.** Six things they will take, one line each, trading — the most quotable and most imitable section in the song.
- **The line for captions.** *"Nobody writes it down, nobody makes a fuss, but the ordinary hours are the best of us."*
- **The turn.** *"That was a Tuesday too. Nobody sang about it."*
- **The challenge.** Post your own Tuesday: the unglamorous frame, the job half done, the person you would not trade it with. Three seconds, no filter.
- **The bookend.** The dripping faucet in frame one and the dry one in the last shot, cut back to back.
