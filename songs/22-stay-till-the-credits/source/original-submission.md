# Stay Till the Credits — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
A woman who has only ever been loved through the exciting part. Every relationship she has had peaked at the big scene and left before the fade. Then she meets a man who sits through the credits — every name, the caterers, the drivers, the second unit crew — because the people who built the thing deserve a little while. She realises that is the only kind of love she wants: not the montage, not the kiss before the black, but whoever is still in the seat when there is nothing left on the screen.

**Mood:**
Quiet, adult, unglamorous, warm. A love song about the boring part, sung by someone who has finally worked out that the boring part is the whole point.

**Message:**
"Stay till the credits, stay till the lights come on."

**Genre:**
Cinematic ballad with a modern low end. Think: Birdy's restraint x Hozier's weight x the piano-and-cello writing of a Max Richter cue, produced like a current pop record rather than a film score. Piano, solo cello, sub-bass, a slow heavy backbeat.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 86 BPM, 4/4
- Key: C minor (adult, unsentimental)
- No modulation. The final chorus widens instead of lifting — the song is about staying in the same place, and a key change would contradict it. One line changes instead.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, conversational and low in the verses, open and steady in the choruses, near-spoken in the bridge and the last two lines of the outro.
- Backing vocals: stacked female harmonies in the choruses, a hushed stack holding the post-chorus, nothing in the outro.

**Instrumentation:**
- Close-mic'd grand piano (main spine, a slow descending figure)
- Solo cello (the second lead voice; enters in verse one, carries the instrumental)
- Deep sub-bass under the long notes
- Warm string section, violins and violas (chorus one on)
- Low brass swell at the end of the instrumental
- Slow heavy backbeat, soft kick and brushed rimshot from the first pre-chorus
- Ambient textures: seats folding up, footsteps on a sloped floor, a distant projector, a room emptying

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the room emptying, one man still reading the names
- Verse 1: the loves that left at the ending, then the man who stays
- Pre-chorus: anyone can love her in the good light
- Chorus: the hook, wide and steady, not desperate
- Verse 2: three months in, and the film is over — dishes, traffic, a bad Tuesday
- Bridge: the usher, the popcorn, the screen reduced to a rectangle of light, and a new definition of love
- Final chorus: the widest, and she puts herself in the seat instead of asking him to stay in his
- Post-chorus: hushed, the lights coming on, both of them still there
- Outro: the empty room, the question, the plain answer

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – piano, room tone, almost conversational]

```
Everybody's leaving while the music's still playing,
Coats on, phones out, halfway up the aisle.
But you're still sitting there reading every name,
Like the people who built it deserve a little while.
```

- Scene: A cinema at the end of the last show. House lights half up. The credits rolling on a screen we only ever see obliquely.
- Shot: A slow wide from the front of the auditorium looking back up the rows: silhouettes standing, coats going on, phone screens lighting faces, a slow drift up the aisle.
- Cut to: Two people still seated in the back row. One of them is reading.
- Her reaction: She is half out of her seat, coat already on her knees, and she sits back down.
- Lighting: The screen's grey credit light on everything, house lights coming up warm and ugly at the edges.

### [verse 1]

```
I've had the kind of love that leaves at the ending,
Kisses at the big scene, gone before the fade.
Loud in the trailer, quiet when it mattered,
Every promise in the dark got quietly unmade.
```

- Scene: The exes, all of them faceless, all of them shot like film moments.
- Shots: A hand pulling away in a taxi window. A kiss lit like a poster, cut off mid-frame. A phone face-down going dark on a restaurant table. Every one of them staged and beautiful and hollow — deliberately over-lit compared with the rest of the video.
- Emotion: Wry, not bitter. She has been the audience for other people's endings.
- Lighting: Over-saturated, cinematic, obviously artificial.

```
But you stay in your seat for the names nobody knows,
The caterers, the drivers, the second unit crew.
And I'm watching you watch it with my coat still on my knees,
Thinking, that's the kind of staying that I want from you.
```

- Scene: Back to the real back row, the two of them.
- Shots: His profile, lit only by the credit roll, eyes moving down the screen. Her face turned toward him, not the screen. Her coat still folded across her knees, the sleeve trailing on the floor.
- Emotion: The whole thesis of the song lands here and neither of them says anything.
- Lighting: Grey screen light on both faces, everything else black.

### [pre-chorus]

```
Because anyone can love me in the good light,
Anyone can hold me when the strings swell high.
I don't need the hero, I need the one
Who's still in his seat when the story's done.
```

- Scene: A short return to the artificial world of the exes, then out of it.
- Shot: One of the poster-lit kisses again, then the light draining out of the frame until only the cinema remains.
- Emotion: Clarity. She knows exactly what she is asking for.
- Lighting: The transition is the point: saturated to plain in one move.

### [chorus – wide, steady, strings in]

```
Stay till the credits, stay till the lights come on,
Stay through the boring part after the ending's gone.
When the crowd walks out and the screen goes grey,
Be the one in the back row who doesn't walk away.
Stay till the credits, stay till the end of the song,
Stay till the lights come on.
```

- Scene: The auditorium emptying completely around the two of them, in a single held perspective.
- Shots: A locked-off wide from the back row over their shoulders: the rows in front emptying one by one across the length of the chorus. The house lights coming up in stages. The screen going from credits to grey to blank.
- Emotion: Steady. This is a request, not a plea.
- Lighting: The widest and brightest the video gets — and it is ugly fluorescent house light, deliberately not flattering.

### [verse 2]

```
Three months in, and it's not a movie anymore,
It's the dishes and the traffic and my mother on the phone.
It's me at my worst on a Tuesday in the kitchen,
It's the version of me that the trailer never showed.
```

- Scene: Out of the cinema entirely. Real life, shot plainly.
- Shots: A sink full of dishes. Brake lights in rain through a windscreen. Her on the phone to her mother with her hand over her eyes. Her crying in a kitchen in a hoodie at four in the afternoon with the overhead light on.
- Emotion: Unglamorous and unashamed. No slow motion, no score-swell in the picture.
- Lighting: Flat domestic light, overhead bulbs, nothing warm.

```
And you're still here, reading all of my small print,
The flaws in the footnotes, the parts I'd cut if I could.
You don't need the montage, you don't need the ending,
You just want to sit here, and I never understood.
```

- Scene: The same flat rooms, with him in them.
- Shots: Him drying the dishes she left. Him in the passenger seat in the traffic, not talking. Him sitting on the kitchen floor with his back against a cupboard, opposite her, saying nothing.
- Emotion: The credit-roll behaviour, transplanted into a kitchen.
- Lighting: Identical to the shots above — the point is that nothing about the light improves.

### [pre-chorus]

```
Because anyone can love me in the good light,
Anyone can hold me when the strings swell high.
I don't need the hero, I need the one
Who's still in his seat when the story's done.
```

- Scene: The kitchen floor, held.
- Shot: A slow push in on the two of them sitting opposite each other on the floor, the room dark except the extractor light.
- Emotion: Recognition.
- Lighting: One small light, the rest of the flat dark.

### [chorus]

```
Stay till the credits, stay till the lights come on,
Stay through the boring part after the ending's gone.
When the crowd walks out and the screen goes grey,
Be the one in the back row who doesn't walk away.
Stay till the credits, stay till the end of the song,
Stay till the lights come on.
```

- Scene: The chorus world again, now intercut with the flat: cinema row and kitchen floor, matched shot for shot.
- Emotion: The two places becoming the same place.
- Lighting: Both plain now.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: No lyrics. The cinema, nearly empty.
- Shots: An usher moving down a row with a bin bag. A single unclaimed scarf on a seat. The projector beam full of dust, seen from below. Popcorn on the sloped floor, kicked and rolling. The two of them, small, in the back row of a wide empty frame. A slow tilt up to the screen: grey, blank, still lit.
- Lighting: Half house lights, dust in the beam, everything grey-warm.

### [bridge – bare, piano and voice, then building]

```
The usher's got his flashlight out, the popcorn's getting swept,
The screen's just a rectangle of light.
I used to think that love was the part with the music,
The kiss before the black, the walk into the night.
```

- Scene: The last minutes before the room is closed.
- Shots: A flashlight beam sweeping along a row of seats and passing over their feet. A broom. The screen with nothing on it. Then two seconds of the over-lit poster kiss from verse one, cut hard against the flat grey.
- Emotion: The old idea of love, seen for what it was.
- Lighting: The flashlight is the only moving light in the frame.

```
Now I think it's who's still there when there's nothing left to watch,
Who's holding my hand when the story lets go.
So if this is the after, if this is the rest of it,
Then I don't want the movie, I want the after show.
```

- Scene: The two of them in the empty room.
- Shots: Their hands on the armrest between the seats, hers going over his. Both of them looking at a blank screen. Her turning to look at him and staying turned.
- Emotion: The turn. The definition changes, and she is not sad about it.
- Lighting: The warmest frame in the video, and it is only house light.

### [final chorus – widest, one line changed]

```
Stay till the credits, stay till the lights come on,
Stay through the boring part, that's where I belong.
When the crowd walks out and the screen goes grey,
I'll be the one in the back row who doesn't walk away.
Stay till the credits, stay till the end of the song,
Stay till the lights come on.
```

- Scene: The request becomes a promise. She stops asking and starts staying.
- Shots: The locked-off wide from chorus one, but now the camera is beside them rather than behind. Both of them still seated as the last person leaves. Her settling back into the seat, not him.
- Emotion: Commitment, delivered flatly and completely.
- Lighting: Full house lights, the widest frame, nothing hidden.

### [post-chorus – hushed, slow pulse]

```
And the lights come on, and the lights come on,
And we don't get up, we don't get up.
And the lights come on, and the lights come on,
And we don't get up, we don't get up.
```

- Scene: The house lights coming up to full in stages across the four lines.
- Shots: Four cuts, each one a stop brighter, all on the same two people in the same two seats.
- Emotion: Stillness. Nothing happens and that is the event.
- Lighting: Rising to full, plain, unflattering, kind.

### [outro – piano and one voice, the empty room]

```
Everybody's gone now, the music's still playing,
The last name rolls up and the screen goes white.
You turn to me in the empty room and ask me what I thought,
And I say, I think I'll stay.
I think I'll stay.
```

- Scene: The room with nobody in it but them.
- Shots: The last name clearing the top of the frame. The screen going white and then dark. Him turning to her and speaking, unheard. Her face. Final shot: the two of them from the front of the auditorium, tiny in a wide empty room, the house lights full, neither of them moving. Hold.
- Emotion: Peace, and a small joke — she is answering a question about the film and both of them know she isn't.
- Lighting: Full house light, the screen dead, everything plain and clear.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the locked-off wide up the emptying rows with *"Stay till the credits, stay till the lights come on"* on screen.
- **The challenge:** film the credits at your next cinema trip and cut it to the chorus, tagging whoever stayed with you.
- **The caption line:** *"Anyone can love me in the good light."*
- **The turn:** *"Then I don't want the movie, I want the after show."*
- **The quote:** *"I don't need the hero, I need the one who's still in his seat when the story's done."*
- **The last frame:** two people, full house lights, an empty room, nobody moving.
