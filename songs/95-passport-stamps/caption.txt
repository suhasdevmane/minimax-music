Global Metadata
Basic Attributes: bpm is 116. key is G major, and scale is major. Global Pop with world percussion and bright synths, sunlit and forward-moving, a travel record built on real hand drums rather than programmed loops.
Global Emotional Progression: The track opens on an airport before sunrise, a low concourse hum, a distant departure chime and the hard mechanical thud of a stamp, with a bright nylon-string guitar figure and a single shaker, the lead voice close and unhurried over the top. The first verse brings in a hand-drum bed of djembe and congas under the guitar with a round electric bass, each city given its own small percussion detail, warm and conversational, the delivery listing people rather than places. The pre-chorus lifts, a bright plucked synth arpeggio and stadium claps entering, her mother asking when she will settle down, and it opens into a wide sunlit chorus with the full percussion ensemble, a bright synth lead doubling the vocal, layered harmonies and a four-on-the-floor kick underneath the hand drums. The second verse tightens the groove and pulls the synths out, a bass line and a shaker carrying the Berlin basement and the two stamps side by side in Istanbul, warmer and more personal, the strings entering under the last two lines as one person leaves. The second pre-chorus is disoriented and half-awake, and the second chorus is fuller with a countermelody in the guitar and a wordless vocal on top. The instrumental break is a percussion feature, djembe and congas trading with a tambourine and a talking drum, over a repeating guitar figure and a filtered chopped vocal of the hook, then a drop to guitar alone. The bridge is quiet and grounded, guitar and a low string pad, a shoebox under a bed and an old passport kept, and it turns on the definition of home as the drums return line by line. The final chorus is the brightest and widest of the track with every layer in, the city names in the hook changed to the ones from the second verse, and a chanted post-chorus rides the groove with claps and a stamp sound on every downbeat. The outro returns to the airport and the guitar figure, one voice, the same stranger at the desk, and a settled ending on a single stamp.

Dynamics are contained in the verses and open dramatically in the choruses. Generous plate and hall reverb on atmospheric elements, with the lead vocal kept close and dry for intimacy.
