# Grandma's Kitchen — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
A grandmother's kitchen as the safest place on earth. Spices in the hall, a radio that was always on, hands that measured by feel, a chair pulled up beside the stove for anyone with a broken heart or a scraped knee. She's gone now; the granddaughter stands in her own small kitchen with the same radio, the same apron and a recipe nobody ever wrote down, and discovers that the recipe was written into her.

**Mood:**
Warm, unhurried, grateful. Grief that has settled into tenderness. Golden-hour nostalgia, not sadness.

**Message:**
"Everything I know about love, I learned in grandma's kitchen — and now I'm giving it away."

**Genre:**
Soul ballad with a gospel lean. Think: Adele "Hometown Glory" x Leon Bridges x Lizzy McAlpine's warmth. Piano, Hammond organ, strings, brushed drums, a choir-style final chorus.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 78 BPM, 4/4
- Key: E♭ major (warm, hymn-like)
- Final chorus lifts a half step brighter (E major) for the "giving it away" turn.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, close and almost spoken in the verses, big and open in the choruses with a raspy edge at the peaks.
- Backing vocals: stacked female harmonies in the second chorus, full choir-style stack (male and female) on the final chorus, a soft wordless vocal in the instrumental.

**Instrumentation:**
- Warm upright piano (main spine, slow gospel figure)
- Hammond organ (swells under pre-choruses and choruses)
- Round electric bass
- Brushed drum kit, soft rimshots, full backbeat in the choruses, tambourine from chorus two
- String section: violins, viola, cello (chorus one on, melody in the instrumental)
- Choir-style stacked backing vocals
- Ambient textures: kitchen room tone, a faint AM radio, a kettle rising to a whistle, the clink of a plate

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the smell of the hall, the radio, the flour in the light
- Verse 1: her hands, her chair by the stove, everything she fixed
- Pre-chorus: the things she used to say, delivered as rules for living
- Chorus: big, glowing, joyful — the hook
- Verse 2: her own apartment now, the burnt onions, the photo, the radio on the shelf
- Bridge: the apron goes on, the friend at the door, her grandmother's voice comes out of her own mouth
- Final chorus: brighter, fuller, "I'm giving it away"
- Outro: the opening figure, the radio, the unlocked door

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – hushed, piano, radio and kettle]

```
Cardamom and cinnamon still hanging in the hall,
The radio was always on and never turned up at all.
Flour on the counter, morning sun across the floor,
I could walk that kitchen blindfolded, a thousand times before.
```

- Scene: An old family kitchen at golden hour. Spice jars on a shelf, a small radio on the windowsill, flour dust hanging in a shaft of sun.
- Shot: Slow push down the hall toward the kitchen doorway, the light growing.
- Cut to: Present day — the granddaughter stands in that doorway, older, alone, a set of keys in her hand.
- Her reaction: She closes her eyes and breathes in. A small smile.
- Lighting: Warm amber, dusty, slightly overexposed at the window.

### [verse 1]

```
She never used a measuring cup, she measured with her hands,
A pinch of this, a little more, she said the dough understands.
She'd hum along to something old while the kettle took the high part,
Pull a chair up by the stove and say, sit, baby, tell me your heart.
```

- Scene: Memory, warm filter. An older woman's hands (face never fully shown, or shown softly from behind) kneading dough, a pinch of salt from an open dish, a wooden spoon.
- Shots: Close-ups of the hands. The kettle starting to sing. A kitchen chair being pulled across the tile toward the stove. A young girl (the granddaughter, aged eight) sitting on it, feet not reaching the floor.
- Cut to: The grandmother's shoulder and the back of her head as she hums, sun behind her.
- Lighting: Warm, soft, kitchen-window gold.

```
I brought her every broken thing, a scraped knee, a bad grade,
She fixed them all with the same two hands and the same thing she made.
```

- Scene: Three quick memories. A plaster going on a knee. A report card being smoothed flat on the counter and a plate slid over it. A teenage girl crying at the table, a plate of something warm arriving without a word.
- Shot: Always the same framing: the counter, the hands, the plate.
- Emotion: Safety. The kitchen fixes things.
- Lighting: Warm, consistent, the same lamp over the table every time.

### [pre-chorus]

```
She'd say, you don't rush the rising, honey, and you don't skip the salt,
And when somebody leaves the table, that's not always your fault.
She said, you learn it by the feel, girl, you learn it by the smell,
And I'm still learning, standing here, trying to do it half as well.
```

- Scene: Memory — dough under a cloth, rising on the windowsill. A chair pushed back and left empty at a full table, the grandmother's hand landing on the girl's shoulder.
- Shot: The girl watching her grandmother's face for how it's done, then present day — the granddaughter's hands over a bowl, hesitating.
- Emotion: The rules for living, delivered as cooking advice.
- Lighting: Memory warm; present slightly cooler but still golden.

### [chorus – wide, glowing, joyful]

```
Everything I know about love, I learned in grandma's kitchen,
Where the door was never locked and nobody went missing.
How to feed a crowd, how to hold a hand, how to let the bread rise slow,
How to leave a light on so the lost ones find their way home.
She never wrote the recipe, she just lived it every day,
Everything I know about love, she taught me anyway.
```

- Scene: Montage of the kitchen at its fullest. A back door with no lock, people coming in unannounced. A long table with too many chairs. A hand held across the table. Bread coming out of the oven. A kitchen light left on in a window at night, seen from the street.
- Shots: Wide, generous, the room full of people and steam. The grandmother always at the centre, seen softly, never in a hard close-up.
- Emotion: Joy. This is the safest place on earth.
- Lighting: Golden, warm, the widest and brightest the video gets so far.

### [verse 2]

```
Now it's my apartment, my counter, and the sun still hits the floor,
But I burn the onions every time, and there's no one to ask anymore.
A photo on the fridge, her in that apron, laughing at the lens,
I talk to her while the water boils like the conversation never ends.
```

- Scene: Present day. A small city apartment kitchen, clean and a little too tidy. Onions smoking in a pan; she pulls it off the heat, swears silently.
- Shot: A photo held to the fridge with a magnet: the grandmother in a floral apron, laughing. The granddaughter talking to it while a pot boils, half smiling, half not.
- Emotion: Loss admitted quietly; she's not crying, she's talking.
- Lighting: Natural afternoon light, cooler than the memories, still warm at the window.

```
Her radio sits on my shelf, still tuned to her old station,
It plays the songs she hummed to teach a hurried girl some patience.
```

- Scene: The same small radio from the intro, now on a shelf in the apartment, next to her spice jars.
- Shot: Close-up of her turning the dial to the old station. Something faint and old comes through. She stops what she's doing and listens.
- Emotion: The bridge between the two kitchens is the radio.
- Lighting: A single lamp, the radio dial glowing.

### [pre-chorus]

```
She'd say, you don't rush the rising, honey, and you don't skip the salt,
When the whole thing falls apart, you start again, it's not your fault.
My mother says I've got her hands, the way I hold the spoon,
And I've got her stubborn heart, and I know just what to do.
```

- Scene: She scrapes the burnt pan into the bin and starts again. Her mother on speakerphone, laughing: "You hold the spoon like she did."
- Shot: Close-up on her hands holding a wooden spoon — the same grip as the grandmother's hands in verse one, matched frame.
- Emotion: Resolve. She knows what to do now.
- Lighting: Lamp light, warmer as the section builds.

### [chorus]

```
Everything I know about love, I learned in grandma's kitchen,
Where the door was never locked and nobody went missing.
How to feed a crowd, how to hold a hand, how to let the bread rise slow,
How to leave a light on so the lost ones find their way home.
She never wrote the recipe, she just lived it every day,
Everything I know about love, she taught me anyway.
```

- Scene: The chorus montage again, now intercut with the present: memory kitchen and apartment kitchen, matched shot for shot. The grandmother's hands / her hands. The old table / her small table. The light in the old window / the light in her apartment window.
- Emotion: The two kitchens becoming one.
- Lighting: Both worlds warm now.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: No lyrics. Slow, wordless. Dough being kneaded in the apartment, flour on her forearms. The bread going into the oven. Her sitting on the floor with her back to the oven door, waiting, the radio playing. A slow drift over the old kitchen, empty and clean, the sun moving across the floor. The grandmother's apron hanging on a hook.
- Lighting: Golden, dusty, the empty old kitchen slightly desaturated.

### [bridge – bare, then building]

```
So I put on her apron and I set the table for two,
Even though it's only me tonight, that's what she would do.
```

- Scene: She takes the floral apron from the hook and ties it on. She sets two places at her small table.
- Shot: Close-up of the apron strings being tied at the back. The second plate going down.
- Emotion: Ritual, not sadness.
- Lighting: Evening, lamp light, warm.

```
When my best friend called me crying, I said, come over, don't explain,
I pulled a chair up by the stove and said, sit, baby, tell me your pain.
```

- Scene: The phone rings. Her friend's voice, tearful. She doesn't ask what happened. The friend arrives at the door, mascara run. She pulls a chair across the floor to the stove — exactly the grandmother's move from verse one.
- Shot: Matched frame with the chair being pulled in verse one. The friend sits. A plate arrives.
- Emotion: The kitchen fixing things, one generation on.
- Lighting: Warm lamp over the stove, the rest of the apartment dark.

```
And I heard her voice come out of me, the same words, the same key,
Turns out she wrote it down after all, she wrote it into me.
```

- Scene: She hears herself say it. She stops with the spoon in her hand. A slow half-laugh.
- Shot: Extreme close-up on her face, realising. Then her hands — the grandmother's grip.
- Emotion: The turn. The recipe was never lost.
- Lighting: The warmest frame in the video.

### [final chorus – brighter, choir stack, strings]

```
Everything I know about love, I learned in grandma's kitchen,
Where the door was never locked and nobody went missing.
How to feed a crowd, how to hold a hand, how to let the bread rise slow,
How to leave a light on so the lost ones find their way home.
She never wrote the recipe, she just lived it every day,
And everything I know about love, I'm giving it away.
```

- Scene: Her apartment filling up: the friend, then more friends, a neighbour, someone's kid. Bread out of the oven. Too many chairs at a small table. Her at the centre, seen the way the grandmother was seen in chorus one.
- Shots: Wide, generous, steam and laughter, the framing echoing chorus one deliberately.
- Emotion: She is the kitchen now.
- Lighting: Golden, the brightest and widest the video gets.

### [outro – piano, radio, one voice]

```
Cardamom and cinnamon still hanging in the hall,
The radio is always on, I never turn it up at all.
Everything I know about love, I learned it in her kitchen,
And I'll leave the door unlocked in case you need it.
```

- Scene: Late. Everyone gone. She turns the radio down but not off. Puts the photo back straight on the fridge. Walks to the front door — and leaves it unlocked.
- Shot: Final frame: her apartment door from the hallway outside, a strip of warm kitchen light under it. Hold.
- Emotion: Peace. An open ending.
- Lighting: Hallway dark, the light under the door warm.
