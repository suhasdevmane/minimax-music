# Dancing on the Table — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
Two strangers seated together at a family wedding, at the hour when the
caterers have gone and only the people who really love the couple are left.
They end up dancing on the cleared trestle table while three generations of
somebody else's family clap them up there. It is not a love song yet — it is
the song of the moment before one.

**Mood:**
Joyful, communal, slightly out of breath. Big brass, bare feet, sunrise.
Celebration without a single sad line in it.

**Message:**
"The best part of the wedding is the part nobody planned, and the best way
to join a family is to get up on their table."

**Genre:**
Modern Latin pop with a live horn section and gang-vocal choruses. Think:
Marc Anthony x Camila Cabello x Bruno Mars horn writing — party records that
are still properly sung.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 128 BPM, 4/4
- Key: D minor (bright and driving rather than sad, the way Latin pop minor
  works)
- The final chorus adds a second brass layer and a full gang vocal rather
  than modulating; the lift is arrangement, not key.

**Vocal setup:**
- Singer A (female lead): verse one, pre-choruses, the top line of every
  chorus.
- Singer B (male): verse two alone, the answering line in the intro,
  harmony a third below in the choruses, alternating lines in the bridge.
- Gang vocals: the whole party on the last two lines of each chorus and on
  the chant.

**Instrumentation:**
- Live horn section: two trumpets and a trombone (the signature of the song)
- Nylon-string and muted electric guitar montuno
- Round upright-style electric bass, walking Latin pattern
- Congas, bongos, timbales, hand-struck cowbell
- Handclaps, finger snaps, a whistle, real crowd shouts
- Bright piano montuno from the second chorus
- Shaker and tambourine for the bridge
- Backyard ambience: laughter, cutlery, a plate stack, a radio two doors
  down, birdsong and early traffic at the end

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the party's leftovers, cowbell and guitar, two people noticing each other
- Verse 1: her side of the table, dry and funny, the night winding down
- Pre-chorus: the countdown, one shoe off, the family sensing what's coming
- Chorus: full horns, everyone up, the table becomes the stage
- Post-chorus: chant, claps, pure lift
- Verse 2: his side, the excuse he had ready and abandoned
- Instrumental: brass call and response over a timbale break, real claps
- Bridge: the drums drop away, the street is audible, the two of them quiet
- Final chorus: the biggest arrangement, two more generations up on the wood
- Outro: sunrise, a borrowed suit jacket, the hook sung almost spoken

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – cowbell, guitar, backyard ambience]

```
Caterers gone, and the cake is a crime scene,
String lights still swinging where the big tent had been.
Somebody's cousin took over the sound,
And nobody left in this yard is sitting down.
Forks in the sink and the chairs pushed aside,
And you at the far end, catching my eye.
```

- Scene: A suburban backyard at two in the morning after a wedding. The
  marquee is half down, string lights are still up, and a long trestle table
  runs the length of the lawn.
- Shot: Slow drift across the wreckage of a good night — a collapsed cake,
  a tipped bottle, a folded veil over a chair back.
- Cut to: Mahima at one end of the table in her wedding-guest dress, shoes
  already in her hand.
- Emotion: Amused, awake, not remotely ready to go home.
- Lighting: Warm string lights, deep blue sky behind, one kitchen window
  glowing at the back of the house.

### [verse 1 – her side of the table]

```
They sat us with the singles where the music didn't go,
Two strangers and a candle that was burning very slow.
You said your mother's cousin married my best friend,
I said we will be family by the time this weekend ends.
```

- Scene: The far end of the table, the "singles" seating, half in shadow.
- Shot: Two-shot across a guttering candle, Mahima and Kai both angled away
  from the empty dance floor.
- Cut to: The seating card being flicked over between her fingers.
- Emotion: Dry, sizing each other up, enjoying the joke of it.
- Lighting: Candle plus one string-light run; the rest of the yard darker.

```
I took the last empanada, you took half of mine,
You poured me something red and swore that it was fine.
The band packed up their trumpets at a quarter after one,
And that is the exact minute this party had begun.
The long wood was all empty and the string lights hung low,
And the whole yard was waiting for somebody to go.
```

- Scene: The end of catering, the beginning of the actual party.
- Shots: Her hand and his reaching the same plate; a bottle poured into a
  plastic cup; the band zipping a trumpet case; a phone being plugged into a
  speaker by a teenage cousin.
- Cut to: A wide of the cleared table, nobody on it, everybody looking at it.
- Emotion: Anticipation, the shared understanding that something is about to
  happen.
- Lighting: Warm, low, the marquee light off, string lights doing all the work.

### [pre-chorus]

```
One shoe on and one shoe gone,
Cowbell hitting like a countdown song.
Grandmother clapping, the aunties know,
There is only one place left to go.
```

- Scene: The countdown before the jump.
- Shots: One sandal dropping into the grass; a grandmother in a folding
  chair starting a slow clap on the two and the four; three aunts turning to
  each other; a cousin's hand raising the volume.
- Emotion: Grinning nerve.
- Lighting: Claps lit from behind by the kitchen window; faces warm.

### [chorus – full horns, gang vocals]

```
We're dancing on the table, let the whole street hear,
Plates in the kitchen and the brass in the air.
Hold on to my hand and don't look down,
We are the last two standing in this town.
We're dancing on the table, let the whole street hear,
Two strangers and a hundred years of family here.
Play it again, play it loud, play it till we fall,
We're dancing on the table, or we're not dancing at all.
```

- Scene: The table as a stage.
- Shots: Her stepping onto a chair, onto the wood; his hand taking hers; the
  whole family standing up around them; a low angle straight up the length of
  the table with string lights overhead; the grandmother clapping harder.
- Emotion: Release. Nothing bittersweet in it.
- Lighting: The brightest the video ever gets on a practical — every bulb,
  the kitchen door open, a phone torch or two.

### [post-chorus – chant]

```
Up, up, higher than the lights,
Up, up, this is how we say goodnight.
Up, up, one more time around,
Nobody in this family sits down.
```

- Scene: Chant, claps, four fast cuts.
- Shots: Feet on wood; a cowbell being struck by an uncle; hands in the air
  in a ring around the table; the bride herself climbing up in her dress.
- Emotion: Communal, loud.
- Lighting: Same, with a lens flare off a phone torch.

### [verse 2 – his side, stripped groove]

```
I had my keys in my pocket and a lie about the time,
A meeting in the morning that was never really mine.
Then the lights came up gold over everybody's heads,
And you kicked off your sandals and you climbed on up instead.
```

- Scene: Flashback, twenty minutes earlier, from Kai's point of view.
- Shots: His thumb on a car key in his pocket; his eyes on the gate; the
  string lights coming on in a run down the garden; her sandals hitting the
  grass, seen from his seat.
- Emotion: A decision being changed in real time.
- Lighting: Cooler for his side of the table, warming as the lights come up.

```
You asked me, are you coming, like it wasn't a request,
Like the wood would hold us both and the night would do the rest.
My father's on his feet now with a napkin in the air,
Your grandmother is filming with the phone up in her hair.
I forgot about the morning, I forgot the whole plan,
There is red wine on my collar and I am a happy man.
```

- Scene: Back on the table, both of them now.
- Shots: Her hand down to him; his father spinning a napkin overhead; a
  grandmother filming vertically with the phone held level with her hairline;
  a red splash on a white collar in close-up.
- Emotion: Delighted, undignified, entirely committed.
- Lighting: Full warm; motion blur welcome.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
Two shoes gone and three songs deep,
Cowbell running like a heartbeat.
The aunties clapping, the cousins know,
There is only one place left to go.
```

- Scene: Deeper into the night, the crowd around the table doubled.
- Shots: Both pairs of shoes now in the grass; the cowbell in a close-up;
  cousins pulling more people out of chairs.
- Emotion: Momentum.
- Lighting: Warm, busier frame.

### [chorus 2]

- Scene: The chorus again, but the yard is full and the table is crowded.
- Shots: Wider versions of the chorus-one frames; a plate finally sliding off
  the end and nobody caring; the couple who got married watching from the
  porch steps, laughing.
- Emotion: Peak communal joy.
- Lighting: Same practicals, more bodies, more movement.

### [instrumental – brass call and response, timbale break]

- Scene: The party at full tilt, no lyrics.
- Shots: A trumpet and trombone traded four bars each, played by two
  musicians who came back out of the van; the timbale break as a percussion
  duel between an uncle and a twelve-year-old on an upturned bucket; a ring of
  clapping hands filmed from inside the ring; both leads catching their breath
  at the far end of the table, laughing, not talking.
- Lighting: Warm, hazy, a little smoke from the kitchen door.

### [bridge – drums drop, the street audible]

```
When the brass goes quiet you can hear the whole street,
A radio two doors down and a hundred tired feet.
You said you will remember this in twenty years or so,
I said then help me down and ask me where I go.
There's a scratch in the varnish that the two of us just made,
And every wedding needs two strangers who forgot to be afraid.
```

- Scene: The quiet inside the loud night.
- Shots: The two of them sitting on the table edge, feet swinging; a neighbour's
  lit window across the fence; his hand offered to help her down; a macro of a
  fresh scratch in the table varnish.
- Emotion: The first real tenderness in the song, understated.
- Lighting: Half the string lights switched off by someone; blue pre-dawn
  entering the frame for the first time.

### [final chorus – biggest arrangement]

- Scene: Everyone up, three generations on and around the table.
- Shots: A mother and a father climbing up; the grandmother finally getting
  helped up by two cousins; a crane-style wide of the whole lit yard; both
  leads in the middle of it not looking at anyone else.
- Emotion: Full release, family adopted.
- Lighting: Widest and warmest frame of the video.

### [post-chorus 2]

- Scene: The chant one last time as the sky greys.
- Shots: Feet, hands, cowbell, the bride's veil being swung like a flag.
- Lighting: Warm practicals against a lightening sky.

### [outro – sunrise, spoken-soft]

```
Sun coming up on the lights and the chairs,
Your suit jacket over me on the back porch stairs.
Table still standing and the yard is a mess,
We are going to be family, I guess.
We're dancing on the table, let the whole street hear.
```

- Scene: Dawn on the back porch.
- Shots: String lights pale against daylight; his suit jacket over her
  shoulders; the two of them on the steps with plastic cups; the empty table
  standing in a field of paper napkins; the last frame a locked-off wide of
  the yard with the lights still on in the morning.
- Emotion: Tired, warm, quietly certain.
- Lighting: First real sun, long shadows, string lights redundant and still on.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** both of them stepping onto the table as the horns hit,
  filmed low from the far end with the string lights overhead.
- **The challenge:** the table climb — invite people to post the moment
  somebody at their family party got up on the furniture, with the chant
  under it.
- **The caption line:** *"Two strangers and a hundred years of family here."*
- **The grandmother shot:** her filming vertically, phone held level with her
  hairline, then being pulled up onto the table herself in the last chorus.
- **The quote:** *"Every wedding needs two strangers who forgot to be
  afraid."*
- **The sound:** the cowbell countdown into the first chorus is a two-second
  audio hook for edits.
