# Main Character — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
A Monday morning commute shot like the opening of a film she is the star of. Not a glow-up after a breakup, not a revenge story — just the ordinary day a woman stops playing the supporting role in her own life and starts walking through her city like the camera is on her.

**Mood:**
Bright, propulsive, cinematic. Confidence without aggression. The feeling of a good soundtrack starting exactly as you step outside.

**Message:**
"Nobody is going to cast you in your own life. Walk out the door like the scene already started."

**Genre:**
Bright cinematic pop with widescreen synth-pop lift. Think: Dua Lipa x Carly Rae Jepsen production gloss with the storytelling detail of a Lorde verse — title-sequence energy, punchy live-feel drums, one big plucked synth motif.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 124 BPM, 4/4
- Key: F major (bright, open, singable in chest voice)
- The final chorus does not modulate; instead the harmonies lift a third and the arrangement widens, so the lift is textural rather than a key change.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, dry and conversational in the verses, belted and wide open in the choruses.
- Backing vocals: stacked female harmonies on both ends of the title line; a chanted crowd layer on the post-chorus; a filtered vocal answer under the walking groove.

**Instrumentation:**
- Bright plucked synth arpeggio (the signature motif); round bouncing electric bass
- Punchy live-feel drum kit with a fat clap layer
- Clean electric guitar, eighth-note picking and an octave chorus hook; muted Rhodes and tambourine in the second verse
- Strings sliding up under the pre-choruses and wide in the final chorus; brass stabs in the second and final choruses
- Piano and one sustained pad (bridge)
- Ambience: room tone, distant street, a soft alarm pulse, footstep claps, a crosswalk chime, wing flutter, traffic

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: half-awake, blue light, the score starting
- Verses: ten years of background and the elevator glass, then the same day indoors and the sentence she finally says out loud
- Pre-chorus: no audition, no permission, the light turns green
- Chorus: the city as a film set, the biggest and most singable moment; the post-chorus is a chanted walking groove
- Instrumental: the commute as pure movement, no words
- Bridge: the generous turn, everybody else is a lead too
- Final chorus: evening, neon, the same words in a different light, then the outro at the next morning, same cup, different woman

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – half-awake, arpeggio only]

```
Alarm at six forty, blue in the room,
Steam on the window, an unmade bed.
Coffee in a paper cup, my name spelled wrong,
Headphones in and the score comes on.
Roll it.
```

- Scene: A small apartment before sunrise, everything blue.
- Shot: Extreme close-up of a phone face-down on a nightstand, vibrating; her hand landing on it.
- Cut to: Her sitting on the edge of the bed, hair everywhere, feet on cold floor; the window fogged.
- Emotion: Nothing heroic yet. Ordinary, tired, human.
- Lighting: Pre-dawn blue, one warm sliver from the hallway.

### [verse 1]

```
Ten years of standing just outside the frame,
Holding a coat, remembering a name.
I was so good at background, so good at "fine,"
Second row, third choice, back of the line.
Then the elevator caught me in the glass,
And the girl looking back did not look past.
Something in my shoulders finally set,
And the lobby doors opened like a silhouette.
Wind off the avenue, coat coming loose,
Pigeons hit the sky like they were cued to move.
Somebody's playlist matched my walking speed,
And the whole street turned into an opening scene.
```

- Scene: A memory montage of being the supporting character, then the elevator, then the street. This is where the film starts.
- Shots: Her holding two coats at a party while other people laugh; her clapping at someone else's announcement; her name misspelled on a badge; her stepping aside in a doorway again and again; mirrored elevator walls with her reflection multiplied; her shoulders dropping and squaring; the doors sliding open into hard white morning; a low ankle-height tracking shot as she steps out; her coat catching the wind; pigeons lifting off the plaza in one wave.
- Cut to: The avenue opening out ahead of her as the camera rises from ankle to chest height.
- Emotion: Recognition rather than anger. The reflection holds her eye and does not blink. Not a smile yet, a decision.
- Lighting: Memories flat and slightly grey; the elevator hard and clean; the doorway blown-out white; the avenue clean gold.

### [pre-chorus]

```
No audition, no note, no callback list,
Nobody's casting this, so I insist.
Light turns green and the crowd divides,
Cue the strings, hit my mark, I rise.
Action.
```

- Scene: A crosswalk, packed with commuters, countdown ticking.
- Shots: Close on the countdown numbers; her face in the crowd, chin lifting; the crowd starting to move and parting slightly around her.
- Emotion: Anticipation. A held breath.
- Lighting: Sun low and directly down the avenue, everyone in silhouette.

### [chorus 1 – the city as a set]

```
Main character, and the city's my set,
Best scene of my life and I haven't shot it yet.
Sun through the scaffolding, that is my key light,
Puddles under taxis holding all that bright.
I'm not the friend in the doorway with the one good line,
Not the girl who waits on somebody else's sign.
Nobody yelled "cut," and I'm done with the regret,
Main character, and the city's my set.
```

- Scene: The full commute montage, shot like a title sequence.
- Shots: Tracking with her down the avenue at chest height; sun strobing through scaffolding poles; a taxi rolling through a puddle in slow motion; her reflection travelling across shop windows; a hard cut of her stepping onto a subway platform as the train arrives on the beat.
- Emotion: Joy that she is not performing for anyone.
- Lighting: Golden morning, high contrast, lens flare through the scaffold gaps.

### [post-chorus – the walking groove]

```
Wide shot, chin up, coat in the wind,
Wide shot, this is where the good part begins.
Steam and the sirens and the crosswalk chime,
Everything out here sounds like a soundtrack of mine.
Doors on the boulevard swing when I pass,
Main character, main character at last.
```

- Scene: Pure walk. The camera pulls back for the first true wide shot.
- Shots: Drone or long-lens wide of one figure in a crowd, the only one in a coloured coat; steam off a grate behind her; a revolving door catching light as she passes.
- Emotion: Ease. Rhythm. The body doing the talking.
- Lighting: Long morning shadows, warm on one side of the street.

### [verse 2]

```
Nineteenth floor, my badge swings on the strap,
Beige on beige and a coffee ring map.
I used to fold my sentences inside a notebook,
Rehearse them in the stairwell where nobody looked.
Today I said the one I had been saving all year,
Table went quiet and I let it stay clear.
Somebody nodded and the meeting moved my way,
That's not a plot twist, that's just a Monday.
Sent the email I rewrote about twelve times,
Cut the word "sorry" and it read just fine.
Lunch on the fire escape, sun on the brick,
One slice, no company, and I did not feel sick.
Walked home at six with the gold on the glass,
And I never once checked who was watching me pass.
```

- Scene: The office, deliberately the least cinematic place in the video, shot cinematically anyway, then the walk home.
- Shots: Badge swinging on a lanyard in slow motion; a beige corridor; a notebook page dense with crossed-out sentences; her in a fire-escape stairwell mouthing words to nobody; a meeting room, eight people, her speaking, the room going still, then a single nod; a cursor deleting the word "sorry" from an email (composited) and the send button; her on the fire escape with a slice and her shoes off.
- Cut to: The office door at six, the coat back on, the low sun turning every window gold.
- Emotion: Fear, then the specific relief of having said it, then quiet unbragging competence. Comfortable alone.
- Lighting: Flat fluorescent with one shaft of window light on her at the table, then warm brick, then full golden hour on glass.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
No stand-in, no stunt double, no cue,
Nobody's writing this but me and the avenue.
Light turns green and the crowd divides,
Cue the strings, hit my mark, I rise.
Action.
```

- Scene: The same crosswalk as the first pre-chorus, twelve hours later.
- Shots: Identical framing to the earlier crosswalk shot, now at dusk; the countdown again; her face lit by headlights instead of sun.
- Emotion: The same decision, made on purpose this time.
- Lighting: Blue hour, headlight white, the first shop neon coming on.

### [chorus 2 – repeat, the evening version]

- Scene and shots: The same tracking shot down the same avenue in a different light; scaffolding lit from below; wet asphalt; her reflection now confident in the same shop window as chorus one.
- Emotion: Bigger, less careful, more physical — she actually dances two steps.
- Lighting: Dusk into first neon, saturated.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: The commute as pure movement. No lyrics, no story beats, just motion.
- Shots: Feet on wet pavement in rhythm with the claps; a subway car window with her reflection strobing past pillars; escalator handrail from a low angle; a revolving door spinning; a crowd crossing in fast motion while she stays at normal speed.
- Lighting: Mixed — tunnel sodium, train fluorescent, street neon.

### [bridge – piano, near-spoken]

```
For years I gave the good lines away for free,
Made myself small so the room could breathe.
Somebody else got the close-up, I held the light,
And I called it being humble, and I called it right.
Now look at the woman across the aisle in blue,
Reading her book like the ending is overdue.
She is the lead of a film I will never see,
So let me be the lead of the one in front of me.
Everybody gets a set. This one is mine.
```

- Scene: A late subway car, half empty, the camera still for the first time in the video.
- Shots: Her sitting, coat across her knees; a wide of the carriage; a stranger in a blue coat opposite, reading, entirely unaware she is being watched; a slow push-in on the stranger, then back to her; on the last line she looks straight down the lens for one beat.
- Emotion: Generosity. The turn is that being the lead is not a competition.
- Lighting: Cold carriage fluorescent with warm tunnel lights strobing past the windows.

### [final chorus – evening, widest]

```
Main character, and the city's my set,
Rain on a windshield is the best shot I'll get.
Sun gone soft on the scaffolding, still my key light,
Neon in the puddles holding all that bright.
I'm not the friend in the doorway with the one good line,
Not the girl who waits on somebody else's sign.
Nobody yelled "cut," and I'm done with the regret,
Main character, and the city's my set.
```

- Scene: Night city, light rain starting. The full-size version of everything.
- Shots: Rain on a bus windshield with the wipers on the beat; the scaffolding lit amber; neon bleeding into puddles; her walking through the middle of the frame while the crowd moves around her; a crane or drone pulling up and back over the block.
- Emotion: Full release, arms loose, actual laughing.
- Lighting: Wet neon, deep saturation, practical light only.

### [post-chorus 2 – repeat, at crowd scale]

- Scene: The same walking groove, now at night and at crowd scale: wide of the boulevard, revolving doors, steam, her coat in the wind, and strangers who happen to be walking in step with her.
- Emotion: Shared. She is not the only main character in the frame any more.
- Lighting: Neon and headlights, rain on the lens.

### [outro – arpeggio and one voice]

```
Alarm at six forty, blue in the room,
Same cup, same name spelled wrong.
The door swings different when you know it's your scene,
Same old city, brand new song.
Main character, and the city's my set.
```

- Scene: The next morning. The intro's exact framings, replayed by a different woman.
- Shots: The same nightstand and the same hand on the same phone; the same paper cup with the same wrong name, held differently; the same lobby doors; the final shot is the low ankle-height tracking shot from verse one, held, as she steps out into the light and keeps walking out of frame.
- Emotion: Calm ownership. No triumph face — just going.
- Lighting: Pre-dawn blue resolving into clean white morning on the last frame.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the low ankle-height tracking shot as the lobby doors open and she steps into the light, with *"main character, and the city's my set"* landing on the step.
- **The challenge:** the wide-shot walk. Post your own commute at chin-up walking pace with the post-chorus over it — the same coat, the same crosswalk, thirty seconds.
- **The caption line:** *"Nobody yelled cut, and I'm done with the regret."*
- **The quote:** *"Everybody gets a set. This one is mine."*
- **The relatable knife:** *"Cut the word sorry and it read just fine."* The clapperboard hit on *"roll it"* is the natural transition sound for edits.
