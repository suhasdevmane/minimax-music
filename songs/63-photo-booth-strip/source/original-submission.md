# Photo Booth Strip — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
Four frames in a mall photo booth on a closing-time Tuesday: the smile, the
kiss, the laugh, and a blur where he turned to say something the flash never
caught. The whole relationship fits inside four inches of paper, and the frame
that came out wrong is the one she kept.

**Mood:**
Bright, quick, bittersweet. Jangly and cheerful on the surface with the ache
underneath, the way the best indie-pop breakup songs work.

**Message:**
"I'd rather have a blur than a night I never spent."

**Genre:**
Jangly indie pop. Think: The 1975 x Alvvays guitars with a Maggie Rogers
narrative and a Vampire Weekend tambourine.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 108 BPM, 4/4. Key: E major (open, ringing, guitar-friendly)
- No key change; the lift is a twelve-string layer, a high countermelody
  guitar and a doubled tambourine on the final chorus.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead: young adult female, half-talking and fast in the verses, open and
  belted on the hook, almost whispered in the bridge.
- Backing: octave doubles and stacked harmonies on the chorus, a gang
  harmony on the last title line, a chanted post-chorus.

**Instrumentation:**
- Two interlocking clean electric guitars, one chorus-pedal arpeggio and one
  palm-muted; a third for the countermelody; twelve-string from chorus one
- Melodic eighth-note electric bass, bright kit, tambourine, floor tom, claps
- Warm electric piano in the bridge only; light organ pad on the last chorus
- No strings anywhere on the record
- Textures: mall room tone, escalator motor, closing-time chime, a
  camera-flash noise stab, the mechanical clunk of a booth cycling

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the red curtain, the quarters, a mall shutting down around them
- Verse one: frames one and two, the smile and the kiss
- Pre-choruses: the countdown, and the fact that you cannot do it again
- Chorus: the hook, four inches of paper, the strip nobody can divide
- Verse two: frames three and four, the laugh and the blur, then the tear
- Instrumental: the mall closing, the booth left glowing
- Bridge: a coat pocket in November, and why the blur is the true one
- Final chorus and post-chorus: she keeps it, out of sight, on purpose
- Outro: the mall is gone, the strip is not

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro — mall room tone, one guitar, no drums]

```
Curtain the color of a cheap red wine,
Two dollars in quarters and a boy with a plan.
The mall is closing, the escalator's stopped,
And we're the only ones still standing where we stand.
```

- Scene: A dying suburban shopping mall fifteen minutes before close.
- Shot: The red booth curtain in macro, velvet nap, one hand pulling it back an inch and letting it fall. Cut to: A wide of the empty concourse, shutters half down, an escalator standing still, two small figures at the far end by a lit booth.
- Emotion: Conspiracy. Two people who have found the last open thing in the building.
- Lighting: Cold overhead fluorescent everywhere except the booth, which is warm and slightly too bright.

### [verse 1 — tambourine shuffle, bass in]

```
Frame one, I'm looking at the lens like I'm supposed to,
Hair behind my ear and my hands in my lap.
You're half a second late and your eyes are on me,
So the first of the four is already a trap.
Frame two, you turn my face with two fingers,
And the flash catches the second I say okay.
Somebody's mom is waiting on the bench outside,
And I forget my own name in the easiest way.
```

- Scene: Inside the booth. The camera lives where the lens is for half of this.
- Shot: Lens-POV: her seated square to camera, hair tucked, hands in her lap, a polite photo face; Kai arriving into frame a beat late, already looking at her instead of the lens. Cut to: Profile two-shot from outside the curtain gap: two fingers turning her chin, the flash blowing the frame white for four frames of video, her mouth on the word okay.
- Emotion: Frame one is performance, frame two is the truth arriving faster than either of them expected.
- Lighting: Hard on-axis flash punctuating a warm tungsten booth; outside the curtain, cold blue.

### [pre-chorus 1 — hats and tambourine only]

```
The machine counts down in a voice like a school bell,
Four little squares and no way to choose.
No take it again, no do it better,
Just whatever we were, and then the proof.
```

- Scene: The countdown between frames, stretched.
- Shot: Macro on the booth's countdown lamp, a mechanical clunk, the shutter iris (all numerals composited). Cut to: Both of them frozen mid-adjustment, waiting, breathing, not talking, shot tight from the side.
- Emotion: The specific panic of being about to be photographed honestly.
- Lighting: Booth tungsten with the flash lamp cycling brighter between beats.

### [chorus 1 — full jangle, twelve-string]

```
Four frames, one night, photo booth strip of my life,
A smile, a kiss, a laugh and a blur on the right.
Two dollars, a curtain, and a flash in the dark,
Cut it down the middle, you still can't tell us apart.
Everybody gets a wall, a shoebox, a shelf,
I got four inches of paper that I keep to myself.
In a wallet, in a coat pocket, out of the light,
Four frames, one night, photo booth strip of my life.
```

- Scene: The strip itself as the hero object, and the four frames played out full-size.
- Shot: The wet strip sliding into the metal tray, four squares in a vertical column, lit from above. Cut to: Each frame expanding to fill the screen in turn and becoming live action for one bar, then snapping back to paper.
- Emotion: The song's big, simple, singable centre. Play it as pride, not as grief.
- Lighting: The paper lit like a museum object; the live frames matched exactly to the booth tungsten.

### [verse 2 — floor tom, second guitar answering]

```
Frame three, you say the thing about the sandwich,
And I'm laughing with my whole face, eyes gone.
Frame four is a blur, you turn to tell me something,
And the flash gets the motion and none of the sound.
I still don't know the end of that sentence,
The mall guy kills the lights and rolls the gate.
We tear it down the middle on the escalator,
And you get the frames where I look great.
```

- Scene: The last two frames, then the mall throwing them out.
- Shot: Lens-POV again: her head back, eyes screwed shut, laughing so hard the frame shakes; Kai mid-sentence turning toward her, and the flash catching pure motion smear. Cut to: A security guard's flashlight beam across the concourse, shutters coming down, the escalator handrail, the strip torn in half against a thumb at the top of the steps.
- Emotion: Peak happiness in the first half of the stanza, and a completely undramatic ending in the second.
- Lighting: The blur frame is the only overexposed image in the video; then the mall goes to emergency lighting, green and low.

### [pre-chorus 2 — same frame, colder]

```
The machine counts down in a voice like a school bell,
Four little squares and no way to choose.
Nobody tells you when you're in the last one,
You find that out later, and it lands like news.
```

- Scene: The same countdown shots as the first pre-chorus, played against an empty booth.
- Shot: The countdown lamp cycling with nobody on the stool, the curtain moving slightly. Cut to: The seat, worn in the middle, a quarter on the floor beneath it.
- Emotion: The retrospective chill. She is narrating from years later for the first time.
- Lighting: Booth tungsten alone in a dark concourse, flash lamp cycling to nothing.

### [chorus 2 — countermelody guitar, shaker]

```
Four frames, one night, photo booth strip of my life,
A smile, a kiss, a laugh and a blur on the right.
Two dollars, a curtain, and a flash in the dark,
Cut it down the middle, you still can't tell us apart.
Everybody gets a wall, a shoebox, a shelf,
I got four inches of paper that I keep to myself.
In a wallet, in a coat pocket, out of the light,
Four frames, one night, photo booth strip of my life.
```

- Scene: The strip travelling: every place it has lived since that night.
- Shot: The half-strip in a wallet behind a bus pass; taped inside a locker door; between the pages of a paperback; face-down in a drawer under batteries. Cut to: The four live frames again, but each one held two frames longer and slightly slower.
- Emotion: Bigger, sadder, and still not self-pitying.
- Lighting: Each location has its own light; the strip stays the same warm tungsten in every one of them.

### [instrumental] — scene block only

- Scene: The mall after everyone has gone.
- Shot: The concourse in emergency lighting; the still escalator from above; the booth still lit and glowing, the only warm thing in the building. Cut to: The curtain moving with no wind reason; the empty tray; a single quarter spinning to rest on the tiles.
- Emotion: Absence, played straight, no melancholy filter.
- Lighting: Green emergency low light everywhere, one warm rectangle.

### [bridge — electric piano, half feel]

```
Found it in a coat in November with a receipt,
Half a strip, three good frames and one that came out wrong.
Everybody wants the kiss, everybody wants the laugh,
But the wrong one is the one I've kept this long.
We were both already moving when the last flash went,
And I'd rather have a blur than a night I never spent.
```

- Scene: Present day. A hallway, a coat, a hand going into a pocket.
- Shot: Winter light through a window, a wool coat on a hook, her hand finding paper and a folded receipt together, close on the hands.
- Cut to: The half-strip held up against the window, all four squares backlit, then her thumb settling over the blur.
- Emotion: The turn. Quiet, adult, resolved, with one breath she does not quite control.
- Lighting: Flat blue-grey daylight, no warmth at all, so the tungsten inside the paper glows by contrast.

### [final chorus — gang harmony, widest arrangement]

```
Four frames, one night, photo booth strip of my life,
A smile, a kiss, a laugh and a blur on the right.
Two dollars, a curtain, and a flash in the dark,
Cut it down the middle, you still can't tell us apart.
Everybody gets a wall, a shoebox, a shelf,
I got four inches of paper that I keep to myself.
Not on the fridge, not in a frame, just out of sight,
Four frames, one night, photo booth strip of my life.
```

- Scene: She decides where it lives now, on purpose, in front of us.
- Shot: A fridge door covered in other people's photos, her hand hovering with the strip and then not doing it; a picture frame, empty, closed and put back in the drawer.
- Cut to: The strip going into an inside coat pocket, the coat going onto the hook, and her walking out of frame without looking back at it.
- Emotion: Choosing to keep something privately is the happy ending here.
- Lighting: Warm domestic light for the first time in the present-day scenes.

### [post-chorus — hats and claps]

```
A smile, a kiss, a laugh, a blur,
A smile, a kiss, a laugh, a blur.
Four frames, one night,
A smile, a kiss, a laugh, a blur.
```

- Scene: The four frames, chanted, cut on the word.
- Shot: Each live frame for exactly one word, in order, four times through, getting faster.
- Cut to: The blur frame held alone at the end of each pass, one beat longer each time.
- Emotion: A ritual. She has clearly done this in her head a thousand times.
- Lighting: Booth tungsten, hard flash punctuation, black between cuts.

### [outro — one guitar, empty parking structure]

```
The mall came down the summer after,
There's a parking structure where the booth used to stand.
I've still got four inches of a Tuesday,
And a laugh with my eyes gone and your hand.
```

- Scene: The site, now. Nothing left.
- Shot: A slow drift across a concrete parking structure deck, numbered bays, strip lights, absolutely nobody.
- Cut to: Her standing roughly where the booth was, hands in coat pockets, working out the geometry of it.
- Emotion: Not sad. Interested, and a little amused that the building went first.
- Lighting: Grey daylight through open concrete sides, one strip light on in the middle of the day.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** four wet squares sliding into the metal tray and each one expanding into live action for one bar.
- **The blur:** the only overexposed image in the video, held alone at the end of every post-chorus pass.
- **The challenge:** post your own photo booth strip, four frames, and caption the fourth one — the bad one — with what was actually happening.
- **The caption line:** *"Cut it down the middle, you still can't tell us apart."*
- **The quote:** *"I'd rather have a blur than a night I never spent."*
