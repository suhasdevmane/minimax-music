# Grow Old Loud — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
A commitment song that is not about staying together — that part is assumed — but about *how*. Two people in their early twenties promise each other they will not become the quiet version of themselves: the beige, careful, sit-at-the-back couple who say "we used to" and never "we will". It is a vow against shrinking, delivered as a pop-rock shout-along.

**Mood:**
Funny, defiant, and then, for eight lines in the bridge, completely serious. Humour is the delivery system for a real fear: not of dying, of getting boring first.

**Message:**
"Do not let me die of being sensible."

**Genre:**
Anthemic pop-rock with brass and gang vocals. Think: The Killers' stadium sweep x Bleachers' horn-driven joy, with the plain-spoken duet writing of a Zach Bryan record.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 118 BPM, 4/4. Key: E major, the guitar-friendliest key there is, open strings ringing through every chorus
- Final chorus modulates up a whole step to F♯ with two extra gang-vocal lines

**Vocal setup:**
- Singer A (female): intro, verse 1, pre-choruses, second half of the bridge, hook throughout. Singer B (male): verse 2, first half of the bridge, octave doubling on every hook, the thermostat line alone.
- Backing: a full gang-vocal wall on every chorus, a crowd chant on the post-chorus, one shouted unison line to close.

**Instrumentation:**
- Two electric guitars: one palm-muted and dry, one wide open and chiming
- Live drum kit with a big room snare, driving electric bass, four-piece brass section
- Warm organ pad (pre-choruses, and alone under the bridge)
- Stomps, handclaps, cowbell, tambourine, and a raw single-coil guitar solo in the second half of the instrumental
- Ambience: room noise, a chair scrape, distant laughter

**Mood arc:**
- Intro and verse 1: a grandmother telling them to keep it down, then the quiet version of them, described in enough detail to be frightening
- Pre-chorus and chorus: the swearing-in, then the promise at full volume with horns
- Verse 2 and bridge: his picture of eighty, ridiculous and specific, then his father, who went out politely, and her answer
- Final chorus and outro: modulated, nobody leaving, back to the grandmother who was the last one standing

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – stomps, claps, one dry guitar]

```
Somebody's grandmother told us to keep it down,
We were twenty-two and wrong about everything but this.
So here's a promise with my whole chest in it,
We are going to be the loudest old people in this town.
```

- Scene: A crowded family kitchen at the end of a party, present day, the couple in their early twenties.
- Shot: An older woman in a doorway, hand up, mock-stern, saying something we cannot hear over the room.
- Cut to: The two of them at the counter, caught mid-laugh, absolutely unrepentant; then straight to camera for the last line.
- Her reaction / Emotion: Delighted to be told off. This is the only shot in the video where either of them addresses the lens directly until the very end.
- Lighting: Warm overheads, a hard practical over the sink, everything a little too bright and a little too full.

### [verse 1]

```
There's a version of us that goes quiet at forty,
Beige and careful with a lawn and a schedule.
Sits at the back at the wedding with a plate on their knees,
Says we used to, we used to, and never says we will.
I have met them, I have sat across from them at dinner,
They ordered the same thing they have ordered for a decade.
And I love a routine, I do, I will take the Tuesday,
But do not let me die of being sensible.
```

- Scene: The ghost couple. The same two actors, aged up with makeup and costume into a beige, careful forties, playing an alternative timeline.
- Shots: A perfect empty lawn; a wall calendar with everything filled in; the pair of them at the back of a wedding with plates balanced on their knees, watching other people dance.
- Cut to: A restaurant, the same order arriving in front of them, neither of them looking at the menu; then a hard cut back to the real, young couple at a kitchen table looking slightly sick.
- Her reaction / Emotion: Recognition, then horror. The ghost couple are not villains, which is what makes them frightening.
- Lighting: The ghost timeline is flat, even, shadowless and slightly desaturated — a catalogue photograph. The real timeline is warm and messy.

### [pre-chorus]

```
So swear it on the driveway, swear it on the porch,
Swear it on whatever we still own at ninety.
No inside voice, no sitting one out,
No wondering what the neighbors think.
```

- Scene: A driveway at night, the party still going behind them.
- Shots: The two of them facing each other on cracked concrete, hands on each other's forearms like a real oath; a porch light with moths.
- Cut to: A neighbour's curtain twitching across the street, and the couple noticing and getting louder.
- Her reaction / Emotion: Ceremonial and ridiculous at the same time. They mean it completely.
- Lighting: One porch light, one street lamp, headlights sweeping past on the last line.

### [chorus – full band, brass, gang vocals]

```
Let's grow old loud, baby, never grow old quiet,
Turn it up in the kitchen till the pictures leave the wall.
Let's be the ones they talk about at every family thing,
The two at the front of the dance floor who should not be there at all.
Gray hair, bad knees, brand new speakers,
Same fight about the thermostat for sixty years.
Let's grow old loud, baby, never grow old quiet,
I'll be the one still shouting when you can't hear.

Loud, loud, never grow old quiet,
Loud, loud, we are not going to try it.
Loud, loud, put the windows down,
We're the loudest old people in this town.
```

- Scene: The first big age jump — the same two actors aged up to their seventies in the same kitchen — then the car, windows down, at every age at once.
- Shots: Two people in their seventies dancing hard in a kitchen with the volume visibly too high; a framed picture walking itself off a nail and hitting the floor; then three quick match cuts of the same two people in the same car with the same windows down at twenty-two, fifty and eighty, the car getting older and the volume never dropping.
- Cut to: A wedding dance floor with the two of them front and centre among people fifty years younger; a brand new speaker box with the sticker still on it next to a walking frame; then a wide of a suburban street with one car in it and the whole street's lights coming on.
- Emotion: Joy with no self-consciousness anywhere in it, then pure chant energy. The car cut is the section people will film themselves to.
- Lighting: Warm, bright, practicals everywhere, the most saturated frames in the video; day, dusk and night across the three matched car cuts.

### [verse 2 – male lead]

```
I looked at you today across a sink of dishes,
And I saw the whole thing, eighty and still ridiculous.
Matching jackets that nobody on earth asked us to wear,
Front row at a show for a band that we outlived.
You singing the wrong words with total confidence,
Me swearing that the wrong words are the right ones.
A camper van, a bad map and an argument at lunch,
And a bench with our names on it that we're never sitting on.
```

- Scene: His imagination, played straight, and it is the funniest and warmest part of the film.
- Shots: The present-day pair at the sink, him watching her; then the aged-up pair in identical loud jackets that nobody would choose; the two of them in the front row of a rock show, the only people over sixty in frame, both delighted.
- Cut to: A camper van pulled over on a verge with a paper map spread on the hood and a full argument in progress; a park bench with a small engraved plaque (composited) and the two of them walking straight past it.
- Emotion: Affection that has decided to be funny about itself.
- Lighting: Kitchen daylight for the present; concert lighting, road daylight and flat park light for the imagined future.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
So swear it in the driveway, swear it in the car,
Swear it on the day a doctor says take it easy.
No inside voice, no sitting one out,
No dying with a good song left unplayed.
```

- Scene: The oath again, decades later.
- Shots: The same cracked driveway, the same two people, sixty years on, same grip on each other's forearms, the porch light replaced with a newer one.
- Cut to: A consulting room, an unheard sentence from a doctor off-frame, both of them nodding politely; then the two of them in the car park outside turning the stereo up.
- Emotion: Defiance that has learned what it is defying.
- Lighting: Clinical white in the consulting room, then warm evening outside — the sharpest lighting contrast in the video.

### [chorus 2]

```
Let's grow old loud, baby, never grow old quiet,
Turn it up in the kitchen till the pictures leave the wall.
Let's be the ones they talk about at every family thing,
The two at the front of the dance floor who should not be there at all.
Gray hair, bad knees, brand new speakers,
Same fight about the thermostat for sixty years.
Let's grow old loud, baby, never grow old quiet,
I'll be the one still shouting when you can't hear.
```

- Scene: The family thing they get talked about at.
- Shots: A large family gathering across three generations, the oldest two people in the room clearly the loudest; grandchildren filming them on phones; an aunt covering her face and laughing.
- Cut to: The thermostat, a hand adjusting it, a second hand adjusting it straight back, twice, on the beat.
- Emotion: Beloved and mildly embarrassing, which is the goal stated in the lyric.
- Lighting: Warm room, phone screens as extra practicals, night through the windows.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: Twenty-four bars. The ageing montage proper, and the video's only quiet trick.
- Shots: A brass shout chorus over a fast decade montage in one fixed kitchen frame — the same doorway, the same couple, the wallpaper changing four times and the pair getting older each time they cross it.
- Cut to: The false ending — everything drops to a bare clap track and one held frame of the empty kitchen with nobody in it, two seconds, genuinely still; then the tom rebuild brings them back in mid-dance.
- Emotion: The one moment the video lets you feel the years passing rather than laugh at them.
- Lighting: Each decade graded differently — warm tungsten, cool eighties fluorescent, flat nineties daylight, modern warm LED.

### [bridge – organ and voice, half-time]

```
My father went out whispering, he was polite about it,
Held the door for everybody right up to the end.
And I loved him and I am not doing that,
I want the neighbors calling and the ceiling coming down.
So if we get the forty years, let's spend them like they're stolen,
Let's be embarrassing in every photograph.
And when they turn the volume on the world down for us,
Let's find the dial and break it off.
```

- Scene: The only serious scene in the film, and it is not sad — it is a decision.
- Shots: An old man in a chair by a window, seen from behind, holding a door open for someone off-frame; a hand on a doorframe; a room with the television on mute.
- Cut to: The aged-up couple sitting on the edge of a bed at night, not performing for anyone, talking quietly; her hand finding his on the last two lines; then a close-up of a volume dial being turned all the way up.
- Emotion: Grief that turns into a plan inside eight lines.
- Lighting: One lamp, deep shadows, the least saturated frames in the video — everything the choruses are not.

### [final chorus – modulated, two extra lines]

```
Let's grow old loud, baby, never grow old quiet,
Turn it up in the kitchen till the pictures leave the wall.
Let's be the ones they talk about at every family thing,
The two at the front of the dance floor who should not be there at all.
Gray hair, bad knees, brand new speakers,
Same fight about the thermostat for sixty years.
No last dance, no lights up, no thank you for coming,
We are staying till they stack the chairs and sweep the floor.
Let's grow old loud, baby, never grow old quiet,
I'll be the one still shouting when you can't hear.
```

- Scene: The end of a wedding reception, and two people who will not leave it.
- Shots: House lights coming up on an emptying function room; staff stacking chairs; a caterer sweeping around the feet of two people in their eighties still dancing to a band that has stopped playing.
- Cut to: The band packing up and one of them plugging back in for them; a full wide of the room with two figures in the middle of it and everything else cleared.
- Emotion: The song's whole argument, staged in one room.
- Lighting: Ugly house lights up full, which is the point — they do not care what light they are in.

### [post-chorus 2 – crowd chant]

```
Loud, loud, never grow old quiet,
Loud, loud, we are not going to try it.
Loud, loud, put the windows down,
We're the loudest old people in this town.
```

- Scene: Outside the reception, the car again.
- Shots: The same three-age match cut from the first post-chorus, now with a fourth cut added at the end: eighty, windows down, same street.
- Cut to: Neighbours' lights coming on down the street, and a hand out of the car window waving at them.
- Emotion: Unrepentant, exactly as promised in the intro.
- Lighting: Night, street lamps, headlights.

### [outro – stomps and one guitar]

```
Somebody's grandmother told us to keep it down,
And she was the last one standing at eleven.
So here's to the noise and the knees and the years,
Let's grow old loud and let them hear.
```

- Scene: Back to the opening kitchen, and the reveal.
- Shots: The grandmother from the intro, in a flashback to the same night, dancing on her own in the emptied kitchen at eleven with the radio on.
- Cut to: The final shot — the aged-up couple in that same kitchen doorway, decades later, one of them reaching for the volume, both looking straight down the lens for the first time since the intro, and holding it, locked-off, through the fade.
- Emotion: A whole life landed in four lines.
- Lighting: Warm, single overhead, the same practical over the sink as shot one.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the framed picture walking itself off the nail and hitting the kitchen floor, cut to *"turn it up in the kitchen till the pictures leave the wall."*
- **The caption line:** *"Do not let me die of being sensible."*
- **The challenge:** the three-age car match cut — one framing, windows down, different decades. Invite couples and families to post their own version with a parent or grandparent in the passenger seat.
- **The quote card:** *"When they turn the volume on the world down for us, let's find the dial and break it off."*
