# Scrapbook of Us — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
An eleventh-anniversary present, made by hand. She has been quietly keeping
every scrap of paper their relationship produced — a bar napkin, a diner
receipt, a hospital wristband, a lease, a paint swatch — and tonight she hands
him the book. The last page is deliberately blank.

**Mood:**
Warm, close, domestic. A love song sung across a kitchen table rather than
from a stage. Grateful without being sentimental, and honest about the year
they nearly did not make.

**Message:**
"A box you never empty is a love song."

**Genre:**
Cinematic piano pop. Think: Adele x Sam Smith piano weight with a Birdy
intimacy and a Ludovico-style solo restatement in the middle.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 86 BPM, 4/4. Key: A major (open, warm, sits low and speaks easily)
- No key change; the lift comes from the string section moving up an octave
  and the low piano octave arriving in the final chorus.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead: young adult female, almost spoken in the verses, open and full on
  the title line, near-whispered in the bridge.
- Backing: stacked harmonies on the title, a layered final hook, wordless
  echoes over the outro.

**Instrumentation:**
- Felt upright piano, close-mic'd with the hammers and pedal audible (the
  whole spine of the record)
- Warm upright bass, brushed kit, light shaker, tambourine from chorus two
- Full string section: cello from the first pre-chorus, violas from chorus
  one, everything in the final chorus
- No guitars and no synthesisers anywhere
- Textures: room tone, a chair on a wooden floor, paper turning, a pen cap,
  piano pedal noise left in the mix

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: glue, a shoebox, a table set for two, a secret about to be handed over
- Verse one: page one and page two, the napkin and the receipt
- Pre-choruses: eleven years of quiet keeping, never once explained
- Chorus: the hook, and the box that was never emptied
- Verse two: the hospital, the folded note, the year that is mostly blank
- Instrumental: the piano restatement, and his hands on the pages
- Bridge: the last page is empty on purpose
- Final chorus: eleven years in the palm of one hand, the box half full
- Outro: the pen goes to him

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)
### [intro — felt piano, room tone, no drums]
```
Glue on my fingers, a shoebox on the floor,
Nine tea lights and a table set for two.
I have been hiding this since February,
And tonight I'm handing it to you.
```
- Scene: A kitchen at night, the table cleared for the first time in years.
- Shot: Macro on fingertips tacky with dried glue, pressing together and coming apart. Cut to: A wide of the table from the doorway, nine tea lights, two plates, a shoebox on the floor beside a chair.
- Emotion: Nerves. She is about to be more sincere than she is usually comfortable being.
- Lighting: Nine candle flames as the only source, one warm bulb over the counter behind, everything else falling to black.

### [verse 1 — soft kick and brushes in]
```
Page one is a napkin from a bar on Delancey,
Your number in a pen that hardly worked at all.
You wrote it upside down so I'd have to turn it,
And I've turned it over ever since that fall.
Page two is a receipt from a diner at three,
Two coffees and a plate of fries we didn't share.
I circled the total and wrote the date beside it,
Because I knew already I'd want the proof somewhere.
```
- Scene: Two pages, each becoming the live moment it came from.
- Shot: The book opened on his knee, page one: a napkin glued flat, ink bled into the fibre. The frame pushes into the napkin until the paper texture becomes a bar counter. Cut to: The bar, eleven years ago, a pen tested three times against a beer mat, the napkin slid across upside down; then a diner at three in the morning, two coffees, one plate of fries, her hand circling a total with a borrowed pen.
- Emotion: Delight, and a little disbelief that she kept a napkin.
- Lighting: Candlelight for the present; warm sodium and neon spill for the bar; hard diner fluorescent for the receipt.

### [pre-chorus 1 — first cello]
```
You never saw me keep them, you were watching me,
A ticket in my pocket, a stub up my sleeve.
Eleven years of small and careful stealing,
From every single room that we would leave.
```
- Scene: The thefts, played as a montage of small sleights of hand.
- Shot: Four one-bar inserts across the years: a ticket stub palmed off a cinema seat, a coaster into a coat, a luggage tag into a bag, a receipt folded into a back pocket, each time with Kai in the background looking somewhere else.
- Cut to: His face in the present, looking up from the book at her, catching on.
- Emotion: Caught out, affectionately. He had no idea.
- Lighting: Each theft keeps the real light of its own year; the present stays candlelit.

### [chorus 1 — full strings, stacked harmonies]
```
Every page in the scrapbook of us, I'd live again,
Soft ones, sharp ones, and the ones that left a stain.
A napkin, a wristband, a flower pressed to dust,
A ticket to a movie we walked out of, and us.
I never called it saving, I just never threw it out,
And a box you never empty is a love song.
Take your time, there's a page for every when,
Every page in the scrapbook of us, I'd live again.
```
- Scene: The book as an object, and the pages turning under his hands.
- Shot: Overhead, locked-off, the whole book in frame, hands entering from the bottom, one page turn per line, the paper catching candlelight as it lifts.
- Cut to: Four one-bar live inserts, one per object in the list, each matched exactly to the page it comes from.
- Emotion: The chorus is not a swell. It is somebody explaining something plainly and meaning it.
- Lighting: Candlelight only, so every page turn changes the exposure slightly and nobody corrects it.

### [verse 2 — piano and voice, no drums for two lines]
```
Page nine is a wristband from a hospital in April,
Your name typed wrong, and I never got it changed.
Page ten is only a folded piece of paper,
With the thing I would have said if you had stayed.
Page eleven is a lease with both our names,
Page twelve is mostly empty, and we know why.
A swatch of the wall we painted twice,
And a note that I have read a hundred times.
```
- Scene: The hard pages. The smallest arrangement in the song gets the biggest content.
- Shot: A plastic hospital wristband glued to a page, the name misspelled (composited), his thumb going over it and stopping. Cut to: A folded square of paper glued at one edge so it can still be opened, and neither of them opening it; then a lease with two names, a paint swatch in two nearly identical greens, and a page that is almost entirely blank with one small note in the corner.
- Emotion: This is the only stretch where neither of them speaks or smiles. Play it completely straight.
- Lighting: The candles have burned down; the frame is measurably darker than the first verse and nobody has relit anything.

### [pre-chorus 2 — low viola]
```
I didn't keep the bad ones to make a point,
I kept them because they belong to us the same.
Eleven years of getting half of it right,
And I wouldn't give a single Tuesday away.
```
- Scene: Her explaining the editorial decision, out loud, badly.
- Shot: A locked-off two-shot across the table, her talking with her hands, him listening with the book still open on his knee.
- Cut to: Macro on the blank-ish page from verse two, and her hand covering it and then deliberately uncovering it again.
- Emotion: The bravest thirty seconds she has had in a while.
- Lighting: Two candles left, both close to the book; her face is half in shadow for the whole stanza.

### [chorus 2 — strings an octave up, tambourine]
```
Every page in the scrapbook of us, I'd live again,
Soft ones, sharp ones, and the ones that left a stain.
A napkin, a wristband, a flower pressed to dust,
A ticket to a movie we walked out of, and us.
I never called it saving, I just never threw it out,
And a box you never empty is a love song.
Take your time, there's a page for every when,
Every page in the scrapbook of us, I'd live again.
```
- Scene: The same overhead book shot, now with the hard pages included in the sequence.
- Shot: Repeat the locked-off overhead exactly, one page turn per line, but the turns are slower and one of them stops halfway.
- Cut to: Four more live inserts, this time drawn from the second verse, cut against the first verse's inserts so the good and bad years alternate.
- Emotion: Fuller, heavier, and warmer than chorus one because of what is now in it.
- Lighting: The relit candles: he gets up mid-chorus and lights three more without breaking the shot.

### [instrumental] — scene block only
- Scene: Nobody speaks. He reads and she watches him read.
- Shot: His hands on the pages in close-up for a long time: a thumb under a corner, a page held up to a candle to see through it, a laugh with no sound.
- Cut to: Her face across the table, watching, chin on her hand; then the shoebox on the floor with the lid off and still half full of unused paper.
- Emotion: The most comfortable silence in the catalogue.
- Lighting: Candles, one warm counter bulb, and a piano-shaped pool of light from the hallway.

### [bridge — half-time, piano and voice]
```
The last page is empty and I left it that way,
A line of dried glue and a space for a date.
There's no question in this, there's nothing to say,
I'm telling you I'm nowhere near done, and I'll wait.
Keep your thumb in the gap and hold our place,
And we'll fill it the slow way, at the pace we make.
```
- Scene: The last page.
- Shot: The book turned to the final spread: one clean page with a single line of dried glue across it and nothing else.
- Cut to: His face taking a second longer than expected to understand it; then his thumb going into the gap between the last two pages and staying there.
- Emotion: The turn. No proposal, no big gesture, just a stated intention to keep going.
- Lighting: The darkest frame of the video, lit by two candles and the page itself reflecting them.

### [final chorus — full section, low piano octave]
```
Every page in the scrapbook of us, I'd live again,
Soft ones, sharp ones, and the ones that left a stain.
A napkin, a wristband, a flower pressed to dust,
A ticket to a movie we walked out of, and us.
You're holding eleven years in the palm of one hand,
And the box is only half full, which was more or less the plan.
Take your time and turn them, then hand me back the pen,
Every page in the scrapbook of us, I'd live again.
```
- Scene: The book closed and weighed, and the room seen properly for the first time.
- Shot: The whole book resting on one open palm, actual size, actual weight, candlelight raking across the cover.
- Cut to: A wide of the kitchen with every light on: the table, the glue, the scissors, the shoebox, both of them standing, the room finally legible.
- Emotion: Peak. Settled rather than euphoric. Nobody cries.
- Lighting: The only fully lit frame in the video, and it arrives on the fifth line.

### [post-chorus — brushes only]
```
I'd live it again, I'd live it again,
Every wrong turn, every long way round.
I'd live it again, I'd live it again,
Turn the page, I'm not putting it down.
```
- Scene: A fast reprise of every live insert in the video, in order, one per beat.
- Shot: Sixteen half-second cuts: the bar, the diner, the cinema, the airport, the hospital corridor, the lease signing, the two greens on the wall, and back to the table.
- Cut to: The book open on the table with nobody holding it, pages moving slightly in the draught.
- Emotion: Momentum. The video's only fast passage.
- Lighting: Every insert keeps its own year's light; the table shot stays warm.

### [outro — felt piano, room tone]
```
Glue on your fingers now, the shoebox on the floor,
The candles are down to nothing, the tea's gone cold.
You've got one page left and a pen in your hand,
And I'm not saying anything. Go on. Write.
```
- Scene: The handover.
- Shot: Macro on his fingertips, now tacky with the same glue, matched exactly to shot 1.
- Cut to: Two candle stubs guttering, two cold mugs, and a pen going into his hand; then a wide of the table from the doorway, matched to shot 4, with the book open at the blank page and him leaning over it.
- Emotion: Handing over authorship. That is the whole ending.
- Lighting: Candles almost gone, first hint of hallway light, the blank page the brightest thing in frame.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the locked-off overhead of the book with one page turn per lyric line, candlelight changing the exposure on every lift.
- **The blank page:** a single line of dried glue and nothing else, held long enough to be uncomfortable, then a thumb going into the gap.
- **The challenge:** show the oldest piece of paper you kept from someone and say what page it would be. Ticket, receipt, wristband, napkin.
- **The caption line:** *"A box you never empty is a love song."*
- **The quote:** *"I'm telling you I'm nowhere near done, and I'll wait."*
