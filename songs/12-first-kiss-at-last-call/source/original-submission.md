# First Kiss at Last Call — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
One night in a cheap bar that is nothing but near-misses. Every time they almost get there, something moves — a friend calls, a stranger cuts between them, the song ends. Then the bar closes, the houselights slam on, and in the worst light either of them has ever stood in, they finally kiss. It is not romantic in any way she planned. It is better.

**Mood:**
Fun, loud, a little reckless, warm underneath. Not a party song about a party — a party song about the two minutes after the party, when everything is being swept up and something real happens anyway.

**Message:**
"You don't get to choose the light you're standing in when it finally happens. Take it anyway."

**Genre:**
Anthemic pop-rock with gang vocals. Think: The Killers x Paramore x Lorde's rhythmic phrasing, guitars big enough for a festival tent, sung like a story told at a table.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 120 BPM, 4/4
- Key: A major (bright, guitar-friendly, singable)
- The final chorus adds a harmonized twin guitar lead and doubled gang vocals rather than a key change; the lift is arrangement, not modulation.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, dry and amused in the verses, full-chested and slightly raspy in the choruses.
- Backing vocals: stacked female harmonies in the choruses; a shouted gang vocal (mixed male and female, room mics) on the title line and through the post-chorus chant.

**Instrumentation:**
- Electric guitars: palm-muted in the verses, wide distorted power chords in the choruses, a harmonized twin lead in the final chorus, a clean arpeggio for verse two and the bridge
- Melodic electric bass, round and forward; live drums with a big room snare and rim clicks in the verses
- Tambourine on every chorus backbeat, doubled in the last; handclaps and a stomp through the instrumental and the post-chorus
- Warm organ pad under the pre-choruses and the last chorus
- Textures: bar-room ambience, glassware, a distant jukebox, a heavy door closing at the very end

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the bar at full volume, the night still undecided
- Verses: a comedy of near-misses, then the colder side door and the choice not to take the easy dark
- Pre-choruses: the room being packed up around them, the music dying, everything narrowing
- Chorus: the houselights, the kiss, the release
- Instrumental: the whole bar emptying, a stomp-clap breakdown
- Bridge: the honest bit — nobody plans this, and it still beat the plan
- Final chorus: biggest, twin guitars, whole-room gang vocals, then the chant
- Outro: the sign goes off, the street, one guitar

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – bar at full volume, guitars muted, hi-hat counting in]

```
Nine o'clock, the door swings, the neon buzzing pink,
You had one job tonight and that job was buying me a drink.
Somebody's racking pool, somebody's playing sad,
And I'm already counting up the almosts that we had.
```

- Scene: A dive bar at nine in the evening, full, loud, cheap pink neon over the back bar.
- Shot: The front door swinging open, her walking in past a line of shoulders, camera following at head height.
- Cut to: The pool table, a rack being lifted; the jukebox lit up in a corner; a man alone at the end of the bar.
- Her reaction / Emotion: Amused, already scanning the room for him, already braced for the night to be a comedy.
- Lighting: Pink neon, warm tungsten over the pool table, everything else in shadow.

### [verse 1a]

```
Sticky floor, cheap draft, forty bucks in the jar,
A dollar taped to the ceiling with a stranger's name.
There's a dartboard nobody has hit since the summer,
You leaned across the jukebox and you played the worst song there.
```

- Scene: Bar-texture montage, fast, on the beat — a beer glass leaving a wet ring, a tip jar of folded notes, a dollar taped to the ceiling, a dartboard with one dart lodged near the edge, his hands on the jukebox glass.
- Emotion: Affectionate cataloguing — she loves this awful room. Lighting: warm orange, hard shadows, practical light only.

### [verse 1b]

```
I said that is a crime, you said dance to it anyway,
So we did it badly, out of time and out of tune.
Your friend kept calling and my friend kept a look on you,
Every time you got close somebody moved the room.
Ten thirty you almost, eleven I almost, and the night was almost through.
```

- Scene: The two of them dancing badly in the two feet of space beside the jukebox.
- Shots: Her laughing with her head back; his hand landing on her waist and then a stranger squeezing past and breaking it; his phone lighting on the bar with a name on it; her friend across the room raising an eyebrow over a glass.
- Cut to: A wall clock at ten thirty, then the same clock at eleven, the same two people a metre apart both times.
- Emotion: Frustration made funny. The joke is on both of them.
- Lighting: Jukebox glow on their faces, moving bodies cutting the light.

### [pre-chorus 1]

```
The bartender's counting tips, the stools are going up,
There's a mop bucket sighing and a last song running out.
I thought we'd lost it somewhere between the pool cue and the door,
Then the ceiling opened up and I could not hide anymore.
```

- Scene: Closing time beginning around them while they stand still.
- Shots: The bartender counting notes; stools going upside down onto the bar; a mop bucket wheeled across frame; the last customers leaving in a blur behind them.
- Emotion: The room is being taken apart and neither of them is moving.
- Lighting: Still warm, but the room emptying out into cold edges.

### [chorus 1 – houselights, the kiss]

```
First kiss at last call,
Lights up and I don't care at all.
No candle, no slow song, no dark to hide in,
Every ugly bulb in the building coming on.
You looked at me like I was the one thing worth the wait,
And I never looked better than I did at half past late.
First kiss at last call,
Lights up and I don't care at all.
```

- Scene: THE moment. Fluorescent tubes bang on across the ceiling in one hit.
- Shots: Ceiling tubes flickering to life in sequence; the room suddenly ugly and green and honest; both of them squinting, laughing; then the kiss, held, while a mop passes behind them.
- Emotion: Relief, delight, complete lack of embarrassment.
- Lighting: Flat, green-white, no flattery whatsoever — and shot to look beautiful anyway.

### [post-chorus 1 – chant]

```
Lights up, lights up, and the whole town saw,
Lights up, lights up, kiss me in the ugly light.
Lights up, lights up, and I don't care at all,
Kiss me in the ugly light, kiss me at last call.
```

- Scene: Four hard impact shots on the chant — a hand on the switch panel, the tubes, her face lit flat and grinning, the bar staff pointedly not looking.
- Emotion: Celebration. Lighting: the new hard white.

### [verse 2a]

```
Nine bucks in quarters and a game that we both lost,
You chalked the cue like it mattered and you scratched it on the break.
The beer sign in the window's missing half of its letters,
So it turned your face red and then it turned your face blue.
```

- Scene: Rewind — earlier in the night, told after the kiss so the audience already knows where it lands.
- Shots: Quarters going into the pool table slot; blue chalk on a cue tip; the white ball dropping into a pocket on the break; a neon beer sign in the window with dead letters cycling red then blue across his face.
- Emotion: Warm, teasing.
- Lighting: The sign color-shifting on skin; the rest of the room dark.

### [verse 2b]

```
Somebody's cousin got engaged and the corner booth went up,
And we clapped like we knew her, and you looked at me too long.
Then the side door, and the cold air coming down on my arms,
You gave me your jacket and you kept your hands in yours.
I could have kissed you out there in the dark and got away,
But the dark is easy, and I wanted you to stay.
```

- Scene: A stranger's engagement erupting in the corner; then the two of them stepping out the side door into an alley.
- Shots: A booth of strangers on their feet cheering, a ring held up; the two of them clapping and glancing at each other; the side door pushing open on a cold blue alley; his jacket going over her shoulders; his hands going straight back into his own pockets; her looking at him for one beat too long and then looking away.
- Emotion: The near-miss she chose. Not shyness — a decision.
- Lighting: Corner booth warm and chaotic; the alley cold blue with one security lamp.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
The speakers cut to nothing, somebody yells last call,
The chairs go up like a curtain coming down on us all.
I figured we would wave goodbye and let it die out on the street,
Then you said my name like a question I already knew.
```

- Scene: Back inside for the actual close.
- Shots: A hand pulling a fader down and the music dying mid-song; a bartender's shout; chairs going up on tables in a fast row; the two of them at the door about to go opposite ways.
- Emotion: Resignation flipping to hope in one line.
- Lighting: Warm dying, cold coming up.

### [chorus 2]

Repeat of chorus 1 — bigger, the kiss shot from a second angle, more of the room visible.

- Scene: The same moment from the other side of the bar, wider — the whole room in frame, chairs up, mop, staff, two people kissing in the middle of it, a slow push-in.
- Emotion: The world carrying on around a thing that is enormous to exactly two people. Lighting: flat fluorescent, wide.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: The bar emptying out, a stomp-clap breakdown.
- Shots: Bar staff stacking glasses in rhythm; a broom sweeping bottle caps across the floor; the two of them still standing in the middle not moving; the jukebox going dark; the pool table lamp switching off.
- Lighting: Fluorescent white going down one bank at a time.

### [bridge – one clean guitar, no drums]

```
Nobody plans it under the bad light,
Everybody's holding out for candles and a perfect night.
But you got me under the buzz and the beer and the broom,
Cold air in the doorway and a bar going back to a room.
It beat every version I rehearsed of how it should have gone,
It beat every slow song I was ever saving for someone.
```

- Scene: Her alone in frame, direct, honest. Shots: her sitting on a barstool that has already been put up, boots on the rail; brief two-second flashes of imagined versions — a candlelit table, a rooftop, a slow song — all shot deliberately flat and lifeless; hard cut back to the real bar.
- Emotion: The turn. She is not settling for this; she prefers it.
- Lighting: One warm practical over the bar, everything else out.

### [final chorus – twin guitars, full gang vocals]

```
First kiss at last call,
Lights up and I don't care at all.
No filter, no shadow, no soft place to land,
Just a bouncer holding the door and your hand in my hand.
You looked at me like I was the one thing worth the wait,
And I never looked better than I did at half past late.
First kiss at last call,
Lights up and I don't care at all.
```

- Scene: The two of them leaving through the front, the bouncer holding the door.
- Shots: Bar staff singing the line back without stopping work; a bouncer holding the door with one arm; the two of them going out under the doorframe hand in hand; the street outside wet and empty.
- Emotion: Full release, gang-vocal energy.
- Lighting: Fluorescent inside, sodium orange outside, a hard cut between the two.

### [post-chorus 2 – chant]

Repeat of the chant, on the street this time.

- Scene: The sidewalk outside, the two of them walking backwards, still talking. Four hard cuts: the sign, her shoes on wet sidewalk, his laugh, their hands.
- Lighting: Sodium orange, wet reflections.

### [outro – one guitar and a shaker]

```
The bar sign flickers dead behind us on the street,
Your jacket on my shoulders and nowhere left to be.
Every good light since has come up a little short,
Worst light in the city and the best night of my life.
```

- Scene: The bar sign switching off behind them as they walk away.
- Shots: The sign dying in two stages; the two of them getting smaller down the street, his jacket still on her shoulders; final locked-off wide of an empty wet street with one working streetlight.
- Emotion: Quiet, satisfied, a little in love. Lighting: one sodium lamp, everything else gone.

## Viral moments

- **The houselight hit.** The single frame where the fluorescent tubes bang on and the whole bar turns ugly — the most recognizable image in the video and the natural loop point.
- **The chant.** *"Kiss me in the ugly light"* is a four-word caption and a shoutable gang-vocal hook.
- **The caption line.** *"I never looked better than I did at half past late."*
- **The quote.** *"The dark is easy, and I wanted you to stay."*
- **The challenge.** Film your own closing-time moment: phone flash on, no filter, houselights energy, cut on the tube flicker.
- **The relatable joke.** Ten thirty you almost, eleven I almost — everyone has had that night.
