# High Heels on Concrete — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:** A woman walks home across a London night and hears her own
footsteps as a drumline. The sound she used to make when she was frightened
is the same sound she now makes when she is untouchable. Nothing has changed
about the street; everything has changed about her.

**Mood:** Cold, controlled, glamorous. Menace without aggression. Confidence
that arrives quietly and then fills the whole road.

**Message:** "The city hears me before it sees me, and I stopped apologising
for the noise."

**Genre:** Drill-pop with a widescreen cinematic chorus. Think: Central Cee x
Raye x Little Simz — UK street cadence under a chorus built for a stadium,
not a freestyle.

**Tempo & Key:** 140 BPM, 4/4, D minor. The final chorus keeps the key and
lifts with a choir layer and an octave vocal on the hook.

**Vocal setup:** Female lead. **Both verses are rapped** in a UK drill
cadence, low and clipped, slightly behind the beat; pre-choruses move from
rap into melody across four bars; choruses and post-choruses fully sung; the
bridge near-spoken. Stacked female harmonies on the chorus, a wordless
counter-line above the hook, tunnel-length reverb throws on the ad-libs. No
male feature — this one is hers alone, on purpose.

**Instrumentation:**
- Sliding 808 bass (glissando, not static) — the spine — and dark minor keys on a two-note cathedral motif
- Triplet hi-hat rolls, sparse rimshot, open snare fills, stadium claps on the chorus backbeat
- A hard, dry **heel-strike sample** used as the signature percussion of the record
- Cinematic strings from the first pre-chorus, muted brass in the break, choir on the final chorus only
- Street ambience: distant traffic, a train passing over the arches

**Mood arc:** Intro, the street and the count-in. Verse 1, the years of
keeping quiet and the end of them. Pre-chorus, the shoulders squaring and rap
turning into melody. Chorus, the drumline and the whole road in time.
Post-chorus, the chant stripped to heel, clap and bass. Verse 2, the tunnel,
the cab, the doubter and the long way home. Bridge, the truth under it — she
used to walk this fast because she was scared. Final chorus, the crowd, the
lane, the choir. Outro, half three, the road empty, one last strike.

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – street ambience, heel-strike count-in]

```
Half past midnight and the high road's humming,
You can hear me on the pavement before you see me coming.
Steel on wet stone, that's the only sound,
London, hold your breath, I am coming round.
```

- Scene: A wide London high road at half past midnight, shutters down, wet tarmac.
- Shot: Extreme low angle, six inches off the ground — a single black heel entering frame and striking concrete, sodium light behind it.
- Cut to: The empty road ahead of her, one bus disappearing, then her silhouette from behind, coat, shoulders.
- Her reaction / Emotion: Nothing performed. Complete calm. She does not look at the camera.
- Lighting: Sodium orange from above, cold blue fill from a shop sign, everything wet.

### [verse 1 – rapped, UK drill cadence]

```
Off the last Overground with my collar to my chin,
Two years keeping quiet was a habit that I binned.
They said "stay small, keep the volume low,"
Now the whole road turns when I take it slow.
Kick, snare, kick — that's the pavement talking,
Every crack in the concrete keeping time while I am walking.
No entourage, no ring light, no crew,
Just a coat, a key and a couple of things to prove.
I was bare polite the whole time they took the mick,
Learned to keep receipts and let the silence stick.
Now the same lot slide in, saying "hope you're doing well,"
I read it on the escalator, kept the story to myself.
```

- Scene: The last Overground train pulling out, her the only person left on it; then the walk down into the street, and the contrast with a group filming under a ring light in a shop doorway.
- Shot: Her coming down the station steps in one continuous tracking move, collar up, the barrier beeping behind her; then a rack focus off the ring light onto her passing in the dark half of the frame.
- Cut to: Three hard cuts on the beat — a heel, a drain cover, a painted road marking sliding under her; her hand in her pocket closing around one door key; an escalator, her face lit by a phone she barely looks at, pocketed without a reply.
- Emotion: Locked in. She is working, not walking. One flicker of dry amusement at the ring light, then gone.
- Lighting: Station strip light, then the drop into sodium. Ring-light white on the group, sodium on her — she is the darker, better-looking half of the frame.

### [pre-chorus 1 – rap turning to melody]

```
Feel that in the tarmac — that's a shoulder squaring up,
Hear that in the doorway — that's a woman had enough.
Give me one dark mile with a bassline underneath,
And I'll turn two leather soles into a full percussion piece.
```

- Scene: A long straight road with the lamps receding to a point.
- Shot: Camera pushing in on her shoulders from behind as they square; her stride lengthening.
- Cut to: Macro on the heel as it lands, water jumping off the pavement in slow motion.
- Emotion: The gear change. She stops carrying herself and starts driving.
- Lighting: Cold blue creeping in over the sodium as the strings enter.

### [chorus 1 – wide, cinematic]

```
High heels on concrete — that's my drumbeat,
Every step a snare going down an empty street.
Lamp light, long shadow, nothing in my lane,
The city hears me coming and it learns to say my name.
Click, clack — hear it ricochet and rise,
No apology, no permission, no disguise.
High heels on concrete — that's my drumbeat,
And the whole of London moves in time with my feet.
```

- Scene: The drumline sequence. Her walk becomes the percussion of the edit.
- Shots: A crane rising behind her as she walks the centre line of an empty road. Her shadow stretching thirty feet ahead under a lamp and shortening as she passes it. Four street objects hit on the beat — a bollard, a railing, a grate, a bin lid — each ringing as her heel lands. A shop window reflecting her whole body in one clean tracking pass.
- Emotion: Not smiling, not scowling. Certain.
- Lighting: Sodium and cold blue alternating lamp by lamp, so she strobes between warm and cold as she walks.

### [post-chorus 1 – chant]

```
Listen to the concrete, listen how it rings,
Leather, steel and pavement — that's the only drum I bring.
Listen to the concrete, hear it under me,
I don't need a stage tonight, I've got a whole street.
```

- Scene: Four half-second impact shots, all heel-level.
- Shots: Heel on a drain cover. Heel on a painted white line. Heel on a wet cobble. Heel on a metal utility plate, which rings.
- Emotion: None — this is pure rhythm.
- Lighting: Hard, single-source, high contrast, almost black around each strike.

### [verse 2 – rapped, the tunnel and the river]

```
Tunnel under the arches and the echo does the mixing,
Bassline off the brickwork and the ceiling does the lifting.
Black cab slows beside me — says, "love, where you going?"
"Nah mate, I'm still working," and the working's in the walking.
There's a lad on the corner who once told me I was dreaming,
Head down now, phone out, pretending he's not seeing.
I don't want the last word, I would rather have the sound,
Every door I got shut out of, I just laid it in the ground.
Puddle catching neon and it doubles up the light,
Two of me now walking and the both of us alright.
Nobody left a lane for me, so I poured one out of nothing,
Took the long way past the river just to hear the bridges drumming.
```

- Scene: A brick railway tunnel — the sonic centrepiece of the video — then out onto the river walk.
- Shot: Her entering the tunnel, the reverb visibly changing the space, a train passing overhead shaking dust down through the caged bulbs; later, her walking beside her own reflection in a wall of dark glass, two of her in step.
- Cut to: A black cab rolling alongside at walking pace, window down, only the driver's hand visible — she waves it on without breaking stride; a **faceless** young man on the corner, hood up, glancing up then dropping his head to his phone; her heel breaking a neon puddle and the image reassembling behind her; a wide from across the water, one small figure on a bridge.
- Emotion: She does not gloat and she does not slow down — that is the point. The first hint of enjoyment arrives on the river.
- Lighting: Tunnel: one caged bulb every ten metres, so she flashes in and out of darkness with each step. River: neon pinks and blues off the water, sodium behind, the water black.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
Feel that in the railings — that's the iron keeping score,
Hear that in the stairwell — that's a nobody no more.
Give me one wet mile with a chorus in my chest,
And I'll turn a bad address into a name you won't forget.
```

- Scene: A riverside stairwell and a run of iron railings.
- Shot: Her hand dragging along the railings as she walks, each bar ringing on the beat.
- Cut to: Her taking a concrete stairwell two steps at a time, the sound stacking in the enclosed space.
- Emotion: Building. This is the last controlled breath before the big one.
- Lighting: Green stairwell strip light, then out into open sodium at the top.

### [chorus 2 – heavier]

```
(as chorus 1)
```

- Scene: The same drumline language, but the street is no longer empty.
- Shots: People appearing — a night bus queue, two chefs on a break, a courier — all turning as she passes. A wider crane than chorus 1, more depth, more traffic. Her shadow now crossed by other shadows.
- Emotion: She notices them noticing and does not adjust for it.
- Lighting: Brighter, more saturated, more sources — the city has woken up around her.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: No lyrics. The pure sound-of-the-city sequence.
- Shots: A locked-off low angle of the empty tunnel with only the heel sound arriving before she does. A slow-motion overhead of the puddle, undisturbed. Her stopping dead on the bridge for the beat drop, the whole city still behind her. The low end slamming back as she starts walking again straight at camera.
- Lighting: The drop is the darkest frame in the video — everything but the skyline goes out for two seconds.

### [bridge – half-time, near-spoken]

```
There was a year I walked this fast because I was frightened,
Keys between my fingers and my shoulders always tightened.
Same street, same shoes, same length of stride,
I just stopped asking the dark for a place to hide.
So if you hear me late and you wonder what it is —
that's not running, that's a rhythm, that's a business.
```

- Scene: A flashback, ungraded and handheld, cut against the present.
- Shots: The same street, a year earlier: her walking fast, keys laced between her fingers, checking behind her, shoulders up. Present: her hand opening, the keys going back in the pocket, her shoulders dropping. The two versions of the walk in a split second match cut, same frame, same stride length.
- Emotion: This is the only vulnerable moment in the song, and it lasts six lines.
- Lighting: Flashback colder, greener, noisier. Present warmer and steadier by contrast.

### [final chorus – widest, choir]

```
High heels on concrete — that's my drumbeat,
Every step a snare and now there's hundreds on the street.
Lamp light, long shadow, and they're falling into line,
The city hears me coming and the city knows it's mine.
(remaining four lines as chorus 1)
```

- Scene: The crowd. Not fans — night people, all walking the same direction.
- Shots: A wide of a busy night street where the crowd opens ahead of her without anybody being asked. Twenty pairs of feet joining the rhythm, all different shoes. A crane pulling straight up until she is one lit point in a whole moving city.
- Emotion: Shared. The confidence stops being about her.
- Lighting: The fullest, warmest, most saturated frame in the film.

### [post-chorus 2 – chant]

```
(as post-chorus 1)
```

- Scene: The four impact shots again, now with other feet joining each one.
- Shots: Her heel, then a trainer, then a work boot, then a heel again, all striking on the same beat.
- Lighting: Hard single-source, as before, but warmer.

### [outro – the street empties]

```
Half past three and the high road's gone quiet,
Nothing left but me and the noise I made of it.
High heels on concrete — that's my drumbeat,
Let it ring out down the middle of the street.
```

- Scene: Half past three. The crowd is gone. The road is hers again.
- Shots: The high road from the opening, empty, wet, lit. Her walking away from camera down the centre line. Final frame: the road held after she has left it, the last heel-strike still ringing in the reverb tail.
- Emotion: Quiet. Satisfied. Not finished.
- Lighting: Sodium going cool as the sky begins to lift at the very top of the frame.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the six-inch low angle of a single heel striking wet concrete under a sodium lamp, cut on the beat — a three-second loop that works with no context.
- **The tunnel:** the reverb change as she enters the arches, with the line *"Tunnel under the arches and the echo does the mixing"* — audio-visual, and instantly copyable in any underpass.
- **The challenge:** the **walk-out** — film your own heel-strike drumline on your own street and cut it to the post-chorus chant. Any shoes count; that is the joke and the point.
- **The caption line:** *"No apology, no permission, no disguise."*
- **The quote:** *"That's not running, that's a rhythm, that's a business."*
- **The bridge match cut:** keys-between-the-fingers to hand-open. One second long, and the whole song is in it.
