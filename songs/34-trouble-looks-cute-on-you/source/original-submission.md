# Trouble Looks Cute on You — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
A slow-burn flirtation on a salsa-bar dance floor with a man everybody in
the room has already warned her about. She hears every warning, checks every
one of them herself, and decides to stay anyway — not because she is naive,
but because she is the one choosing. The night ends on a roof where the red
light can't do the work any more and he tells her one true thing.

**Mood:**
Warm, sweaty, amused, in control. Flirtation as a negotiation she is winning.
Never desperate, never reckless — knowing.

**Message:**
"I read the warning label. I'm still buying."

**Genre:**
Latin-pop with a live salsa-bar band feel. Think: Rosalía x Camila Cabello,
with the horn-and-conga confidence of a Gloria Estefan record and the dry
modern lyric of a Dua Lipa verse.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 105 BPM, 4/4
- Key: G minor (hot, minor-key Latin colour, easy for brass)
- The final chorus sits a register higher with the widest brass of the track;
  no modulation needed, the lift comes from arrangement.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, close and conversational in the verses,
  belted and playful in the choruses, near-spoken on the rooftop bridge.
- Backing vocals: stacked female harmonies on the choruses; a call-and-
  response chant on the post-chorus.

**Instrumentation:**
- Nylon-string acoustic guitar (the signature voice, first bar to last)
- Congas and bongos
- Timbales, clave, shaker, cowbell
- Brass section: trumpet and trombone
- Montuno piano
- Muted upright bass, walking in the choruses
- Sustained strings and a lone muted trumpet for the bridge
- Room tone: a crowded bar, glasses, distant conversation

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the band already playing, a name said like a weather warning
- Verses: taking inventory of him, then his own confession answered by her choice
- Pre-chorus: the warnings stack up, the red flags wave, and she decides anyway
- Chorus: the hook, brass wide open, the decision made out loud and then bigger
- Instrumental: guitar solo trading with brass over a percussion breakdown
- Bridge: the roof, no red light, one honest sentence, half-time
- Final chorus: the same decision made in daylight, and it holds
- Outro: the chant, then the bar shut and his jacket on her shoulders

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – congas already moving, nylon guitar, room tone]

```
Red bulb over the bar and the brass coming down,
Somebody says your name like a warning to me.
The congas keep going, nobody's sitting,
And you cross the whole room way too easy.
```

- Scene: A small salsa bar at one in the morning, red bulb over the bar, packed and humid.
- Shot: Slow push through dancers to her at the end of the bar, glass in hand, watching the band.
- Cut to: A friend at her ear saying a name; her eyebrows lifting, not alarmed, a small private smile as she turns back to the floor.
- Lighting: Deep red key from the bar bulb, warm amber spill from the stage, everything else black.

### [verse 1]

```
Your friends won't meet my eyes, that's the first thing I clock,
Second, the barman pours before you talk.
Third, you don't lie about a single part,
You just say it plain and let me choose my heart.
```

- Scene: The bar itself, the two of them a metre apart, not touching.
- Shots: Three quick beats on the lyric — his friends looking away; the barman setting a glass down before he speaks; his shrug, palms up, an honest man admitting he is not a safe one.
- Emotion: Assessment, not infatuation. She is doing maths, chin up.
- Lighting: Red and amber, hard shadow under the brow.

```
There's a nick on your knuckle from a bad last spring,
And a chain at your collar holding somebody's ring.
I should be saying goodnight, I should be gone,
But the nylon guitar keeps talking me on.
```

- Scene: Detail shots at the bar rail.
- Shots: Macro on the small white scar across his knuckle as he lifts a glass. Macro on a plain silver ring hanging on a chain at his open collar. Her hand finding her coat check ticket in her pocket, turning it over, putting it back.
- Emotion and lighting: The exit is right there and she leaves it there; red rim light on his neck and the chain, her face in shadow.

### [pre-chorus]

```
Every warning that they gave me had your name inside,
Every one of them was right, and I decide.
Keep your hand right where it is, don't lose the beat,
I'm not looking for the door, I'm looking at your feet.
```

- Scene: The floor, the band tightening, the timbales entering.
- Shots: Two friends at a table mouthing something urgent at her; she nods, yes, I heard you. She sets her glass down. His hand at her waist, held exactly where it is. Her eyes going down to their feet finding the same beat.
- Emotion and lighting: A choice being made in public, the stage lights sweeping across them both.

### [chorus – brass wide open]

```
Trouble looks cute on you, so I'll take my chances,
Red light, wrong man, right song, two dances.
I know how this ends, I've been told twice tonight,
And I'd still pick the floor over doing it right.

Trouble looks cute on you, you wear it like linen,
Loose at the shoulder and the whole room is in it.
Call it a mistake, I'll call it mine,
Trouble looks cute on you, and I've got time.
```

- Scene: The dance floor at full tilt.
- Shots: The two of them dancing properly, not choreographed to perfection — real, close, laughing. The horn section standing up on the small stage. A spin, a catch, a hand at the small of her back. The rest of the room a red blur behind them.
- Emotion and lighting: Joy with its eyes open; red key, amber back light, sweat catching the highlights.

### [verse 2]

```
Two in the morning and the band takes a break,
You give me the version your mother would hate.
A job that you walked out of, a bridge that you burned,
A girl at the end of the bar who still turns.
```

- Scene: The band on their break, the room suddenly quieter, the two of them in a corner booth.
- Shots: The stage empty, instruments on stands. Him talking, hands moving, telling it straight. Cut on each item: an office lobby he never went back to (two-second flash, cold light); a phone screen face-down; a woman at the far end of the bar turning her head at the sound of his laugh, seen over his shoulder, her face soft-focused and never held.
- Emotion: He is not selling himself. That is the dangerous part.
- Lighting: House lights half up between sets, unflattering and honest.

```
I'm nodding along like I'm reading a menu,
Choosing the one thing I know will undo me.
You say, you can still leave, the door is right there,
And I say, I've counted every step, I don't care.
```

- Scene: Same booth, closer.
- Shots: Her chin on her hand, entirely calm, listening the way you read a menu. A wide shot with the bar's front door visible behind her, propped open to the street. She does not look at it. She looks at him and says the line.
- Emotion and lighting: The power sits with her and both know it; the doorway a cold blue rectangle in a warm red room.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
Every red flag in this room is doing a slow wave,
Every single one is right, and I stay.
Keep your hand right where it is, don't lose the beat,
I'm not looking for the door, I'm looking at your feet.
```

- Scene: The band coming back on, the room refilling around them.
- Shots: Her friends collecting their coats and giving her the look; she waves them off, warm, no drama. Him offering a hand; her taking it. The timbales starting again.
- Emotion and lighting: The room clears of everyone who was going to stop this; stage lights up again, the red returning.

### [chorus]

(repeat)

- Scene: Second dance, more confident, more of the room watching.
- Shots: The floor giving them space. A trumpet player leaning into the solo. Her head back, laughing. His hand and hers, fingers laced, held up on the beat.
- Emotion: Bigger, freer, still in control.
- Lighting: Fuller, more saturated red and gold.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: The band takes over. No lyrics.
- Shots: The guitarist's hands on nylon strings, macro. The brass section trading phrases, bells up. A percussion breakdown: congas, bongos, timbales, a cowbell, cut fast on the pattern. The two of them dancing in silhouette against the stage lights. The bar clock reading past three.
- Lighting: Stage-only, everything else silhouette.

### [bridge – half-time, out on the roof]

```
Up on the roof there's no red light to hide in,
Just the city on its four in the morning hum.
You go quiet, and you tell me one true thing,
Not the dangerous kind, the kind that's hard to say.
And trouble looks different when it's cold and it's honest,
It looks like a man who might actually stay.
```

- Scene: The fire-escape roof above the bar, four in the morning, cold.
- Shots: The door pushing open onto grey-blue air, the music dropping behind it. The two of them sitting on the ledge, a metre of space between them. Him looking at the city, not at her, saying something we do not hear. Her face changing — the amusement going out of it and something steadier arriving.
- Emotion: The flirtation stops being a game for eight bars.
- Lighting: Pre-dawn blue-grey, city glow from below, no red anywhere in the frame.

### [final chorus – widest brass, daylight coming]

```
Trouble looks cute on you, so I took my chances,
Dawn light, same man, one more of those dances.
I knew how this ends, they told me twice that night,
And I'd pick this roof again and call it right.

Trouble looks cute on you, you wear it like linen,
Loose at the shoulder with the sun coming in it.
Call it a mistake, I'll call it mine,
Trouble looks cute on you, and I've got time.
```

- Scene: The roof at first light, the two of them dancing to the music leaking up through the floor.
- Shots: Her shoes off on the gravel. A slow spin with the skyline behind. The sun edging over the buildings and catching the chain at his collar. Both of them laughing at how ridiculous it is.
- Emotion: The same decision, made in daylight, and it holds.
- Lighting: First gold light, long shadows, no artificial colour.

### [post-chorus – chant]

```
So I'll take my chances, so I'll take my chances,
Red light, wrong man, right song, two dances.
So I'll take my chances, so I'll take my chances,
Trouble looks cute on you.
```

- Scene: Fast cuts back through the whole night on the chant.
- Shots: Four ultra-fast beats — the red bulb, his hand at her waist, the trumpet bell, her shoes in her hand — then back to the roof.
- Emotion and lighting: Pure release, alternating red and gold on the beat.

### [outro – nylon guitar and a lone trumpet]

```
Bar is shut, the brass is packed away,
Your jacket on my shoulders and the street is grey.
Somebody's going to say your name like a warning,
And I'll smile and I'll say, I know, and I'll stay.
```

- Scene: Street level, morning, the bar shuttered.
- Shots: The horn player carrying a case to a car. His jacket around her shoulders, sleeves too long. The two of them walking, not holding hands, close. Final shot: she glances back at the dark bar door once and keeps walking.
- Emotion: Calm, warm, unafraid.
- Lighting: Flat grey early morning, one warm shop light.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the spin on *"Trouble looks cute on you, so I'll take my chances"* with the horn section standing up behind them.
- **The challenge:** the two-dance edit — post the chorus cut and invite people to film the moment they knew a night was going to be a story.
- **The caption line:** *"I read every warning. I stayed for the second dance."*
- **The quote:** *"Trouble looks different when it's cold and it's honest."*
- **The still:** her shoes in one hand on a gravel roof at sunrise.
