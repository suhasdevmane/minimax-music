# Vespa Through the Old Town — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:** A one-week holiday romance in a Mediterranean old town, told
almost entirely from the back of a borrowed scooter. She is a bad tourist
with a folded map and no sense of direction; he is a local who knows a guy
with a key. Nothing about it is permanent and nobody pretends otherwise,
which is exactly why it works.

**Mood:** Sunlit, dry-witted, unhurried. Joy without soppiness. The sadness
arrives once, in the bridge, and is dealt with in six lines like adults.

**Message:** "It doesn't have to last to have counted."

**Genre:** Italian-flavoured Mediterranean pop — modern acoustic pop
production carrying mandolin tremolo, a light accordion, nylon guitar and
handclaps. Think: Jack Savoretti x HAIM x the sunnier half of Maggie Rogers.

**Tempo & Key:** 112 BPM, 4/4, C major. Choruses climb toward the dominant;
the final chorus brightens with trumpet and tambourine rather than a key
change.

**Vocal setup:** Female lead, solo. Verses told rather than sung, with a dry
amused half-smile in the tone. Choruses full-voiced and open. The bridge
near-spoken, the outro barely above a breath. Stacked female harmonies on
the hook.

**Instrumentation:**
- Nylon-string acoustic guitar (the spine), fingerpicked then strummed
- Warm walking bass, brushed kit, handclaps on two and four
- Mandolin-style tremolo plucks answering the vocal and carrying the chorus
  countermelody
- Light accordion swelling into every pre-chorus, solo in the break
- Bright piano on offbeat chords in the choruses; trumpet and tambourine on
  the last one
- Ambience: cup-and-saucer clatter, a church bell, a scooter catching,
  cobblestone rumble, a square band heard from across a piazza

**Mood arc:** Intro, a coffee drunk standing up and a badly folded map. Verse
one, the borrowed bike and the road tipping downhill. Pre-chorus, the streets
narrowing. Chorus, the ride, full sun. Verse two, evening: the fountain, the
lemon ice, the band that only knows seven songs. Pre-chorus, the harbour.
Bridge, the plane on Sunday, and the decision not to make a promise. Final
chorus, the widest and happiest. Outro, the bike chained up, the flight at
seven, and one open door left in June.

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – nylon guitar, one mandolin tremolo, no drums]

```
Two euros for a coffee at a bar with no chairs,
You drank yours standing up like you were born here.
I had a map that I folded wrong,
A terrible sense of north, and a week.
```

- Scene: A small square at nine in the morning. A bar with a zinc counter and no seating at all.
- Shot: Two small white cups on the counter, one hand each; a wide of the square through the open doorway behind them.
- Cut to: Her wrestling a paper map that will not fold back the way it came, entirely defeated by it.
- Emotion: Amusement, both directions. Nobody is trying to impress anybody yet.
- Lighting: Hard early sun coming through the doorway in a single bar across the counter; the interior cool and dark.

### [verse 1 – brushed kit, walking bass, claps]

```
You said no bus comes back here until Thursday,
But you knew a guy with a key and a bike.
It was mint green and older than both of us,
And the mirror was taped back on twice.
Bag on my back and my hands on your jacket,
And the engine sounded like a wasp in a jar.
Then the road tipped down and the town opened,
And I forgot to ask how far.
```

- Scene: A garage courtyard behind a house. An old mint-green scooter under a tarpaulin.
- Shot: The tarpaulin coming off in one movement; a slow pan down the length of the bike, ending on a wing mirror wrapped twice in silver tape.
- Cut to: The kick-start, the engine catching badly then holding; her hand hovering, then closing on his jacket.
- Cut to: The road tipping downhill and the whole town opening out below — rooftops, the campanile, the sea beyond.
- Emotion: Nerves turning into delight inside four seconds.
- Lighting: Deep shade in the courtyard, then a hard cut into full late-morning sun as they clear the archway.

### [pre-chorus 1 – accordion swell]

```
Left at the church, right at the laundry lines,
Down a street too narrow for a car.
Somebody's grandmother waved from a window,
And I waved back like I lived here.
```

- Scene: The old town proper. Streets a car could not physically enter.
- Shot: A mounted camera on the handlebars, the walls sliding past a hand's width from each mirror.
- Cut to: Laundry lines strung overhead, white sheets flaring as they pass under.
- Cut to: An old woman at a first-floor window raising one hand; Mahima waving back with both, over-committed and delighted.
- Emotion: The exact moment a tourist stops feeling like one.
- Lighting: Alternating hard sun and deep shade every few metres — the street strobing.

### [chorus 1 – full band, mandolin countermelody]

```
On a Vespa through the old town, holding on to you,
Cobblestones like a heartbeat coming through my shoes.
Lemon trees and laundry and a bell I cannot see,
And a boy from a town I can't spell in front of me.
On a Vespa through the old town, nothing left to prove,
Half a tank of nowhere and a summer to use.
There's no part of this week that I'd undo,
On a Vespa through the old town, holding on to you.
```

- Scene: The ride, at full sun. The centrepiece of the film.
- Shots: A long tracking car alongside the scooter on a coast road, both of them laughing, sea on the far side. Her feet on the footboard, vibrating with the cobbles. A lemon tree branch hanging low enough that she reaches up and touches it going past. A bell tower heard and never shown, only the sky where it should be. A drone lifting off them as they come out of a tunnel into light.
- Emotion: Uncomplicated happiness. There is no plot in this chorus and there does not need to be.
- Lighting: Full midday, hot and slightly overexposed, apricot walls throwing warm bounce onto both faces.

### [verse 2 – rim pattern, nylon guitar, accordion]

```
We split a lemon ice on the rim of a fountain
Where the water's been running since before either name.
You said everyone here has kissed somebody on it,
And you said it to the water, and I said the same.
The band in the square only knows seven numbers,
So they played the slow one twice and took their time.
You put your jacket round my shoulders near eleven,
And I decided the night could be mine.
```

- Scene: Evening in a piazza. A stone fountain, a paper cup of lemon ice, one spoon between two people.
- Shot: The two of them sitting on the fountain rim from behind, the water lit from beneath, the square busy behind them.
- Cut to: Neither of them looking at the other; both talking to the water. Two separate close-ups, never a two-shot, for the whole exchange.
- Cut to: A five-piece square band on a low platform playing the same slow number for the second time; couples of every age dancing badly.
- Cut to: His jacket going round her shoulders; her not commenting on it.
- Emotion: The tilt from holiday to something with weight in it.
- Lighting: Blue hour going to warm string-light gold, the fountain the brightest thing in frame.

### [pre-chorus 2 – the harbour]

```
Left at the bakery, right at the harbour,
Down where the boats are all faded blue.
Somebody's radio was playing our nothing,
And I sang it wrong on purpose so you would too.
```

- Scene: Night. A working harbour, not a marina — nets, crates, faded blue hulls.
- Shot: The handlebar mount again, but slower, the streets empty now.
- Cut to: A radio on a crate beside a man mending net; a song neither of them knows.
- Cut to: Her singing it wrong on purpose; him joining in worse.
- Emotion: The private joke that makes it theirs.
- Lighting: Harbour sodium and one working floodlight; the water black and moving.

### [chorus 2 – fuller, trumpet doubling]

```
(as chorus 1)
```

- Scene: The ride again, now at night, the same roads completely transformed.
- Shots: The coast road with no other traffic, the single headlight throwing forward. Her arms out sideways for two seconds, then back. The town from above, all lit. The same lemon tree in the dark, unreachable now.
- Emotion: Bigger, looser, later.
- Lighting: One headlight, sodium, and a moon on the water.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: No lyrics. Accordion, then mandolin and guitar trading, then the clap-and-stomp break.
- Shots: A slow-motion pass of white sheets on a laundry line, filling and emptying. The square band packing up, instruments into cases. A dawn swim: the two of them going into flat grey sea fully awake and shouting. Old men playing cards outside a bar at seven in the morning, entirely unimpressed by them. The scooter parked against a wall with the mirror tape catching first light.
- Lighting: The dawn swim is the only cool-graded sequence in the film.

### [bridge – voice and nylon guitar, near-spoken]

```
Sunday there's a plane with my name on a seat,
And a job and a coat and a grey little street.
I won't ask you to wait, you won't ask me to stay,
We're better than a promise we'd break anyway.
So give me the long way back to the harbour tonight,
And a taped-up mirror holding all of that light.
```

- Scene: A stone wall above the harbour, the last night. The scooter parked behind them.
- Shot: A locked-off wide, both sitting on the wall with a gap between them, the harbour lights below.
- Cut to: Her phone face-down on the wall with a boarding pass on it, unlooked at (composited).
- Cut to: Macro of the taped mirror with the harbour lights in it — the shot the whole video has been building toward.
- Emotion: Clear-eyed, not tragic. This is two adults choosing not to lie to each other.
- Lighting: The darkest grade in the film, warm sodium from below, nothing from above.

### [final chorus – widest, trumpet and tambourine]

```
On a Vespa through the old town, holding on to you,
Cobblestones like a heartbeat coming through my shoes.
Lemon trees and laundry and a bell I finally see,
And a boy who says my name like it belongs to the sea.
(remaining four lines as chorus 1, with "nothing left to lose")
```

- Scene: The long way back to the harbour. Every location from the film, in one continuous-feeling ride.
- Shots: The archway, the laundry street, the grandmother's window with the shutter now closed, the fountain empty, the piazza empty. The bell tower finally shown, on *"a bell I finally see."* A drone pulling up and back until the whole old town and the sea are in one frame.
- Emotion: Everything at once, and none of it sad.
- Lighting: Deep night into the very first blue of morning by the last shot.

### [post-chorus – clap and stomp]

```
Take the corner wide, let the old town blur,
Every shutter open, every awning in the sun.
Take the corner wide, I'm holding on for good,
And the road keeps going, the summer's not done.
```

- Scene: Four fast daylight returns, cut hard on the stomp.
- Shots: A corner taken wide, the whole street smearing. Shutters opening along one wall. An awning cranked out over a doorway. The road ahead with nothing on it.
- Lighting: Full sun, saturated, hard cuts, no fades.

### [outro – one guitar, the bell, room tone]

```
The mint green bike is chained by the church again,
My flight goes at seven, and the sea doesn't care.
I'll come back in June when the lemons are heavy,
On a Vespa through the old town, if you're still there.
```

- Scene: Morning. The bike back where it started, chained to a railing by the church.
- Shots: The chain going round the frame; a hand on the taped mirror for one second, then gone. Her walking away with the bag on her back, not looking back. The sea from the road, completely indifferent. Final frame: the mint-green scooter alone against the church wall, the bell sounding once, held.
- Emotion: Not heartbreak. A door left open.
- Lighting: Clean flat morning, no drama in it at all.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the handlebar-mounted shot of a street too narrow for a car, walls a hand's width from each mirror, cut on the beat.
- **The wave:** the grandmother at the window and *"I waved back like I lived here"* — the most shared three seconds in the film.
- **The challenge:** film your own version of the pre-chorus directions on any bike, in any town, and cut it to the clap-and-stomp post-chorus.
- **The caption line:** *"Half a tank of nowhere and a summer to use."*
- **The quote:** *"We're better than a promise we'd break anyway."*
- **The mirror:** the taped-up wing mirror holding the harbour lights — the object the video makes you care about.
