# Bad Idea, Good Night — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
Everyone in her life has voted against him — the best friend, the sister, the whole group chat, 7–0. He is, by every account, a bad idea. She agrees completely. Then she gets on the back of his scooter anyway and has the best night of her year: a skate park after dark, a terrible basement gig, chips bought with his last four coins, a roundabout at 2 a.m., a gas station curb at sunrise. The song never argues that he's good for her. It argues that one night doesn't have to be.

**Mood:**
Reckless, grinning, fast. Pop-punk joy with a self-aware wink. The girl who read all the reviews and went anyway.

**Message:**
"You're a bad idea, but a good night — and I'm allowed to have one."

**Genre:**
Pop-punk / power-pop with shouted gang vocals. Think: Olivia Rodrigo's *brutal* energy x Paramore's *Misery Business* drive x early Avril Lavigne attitude, with a modern polished low end.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 150 BPM, 4/4
- Key: E major (bright, shoutable, guitar-friendly)
- No key change; the final chorus lifts through double-time drums, a harmony guitar over the hook and the loudest gang shouts of the song instead.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, conversational and grinning in the verses, belted and slightly raspy in the choruses, wry and close in the bridge.
- Backing vocals: stacked harmonies on the chorus; a shouted gang vocal answering every "whoa oh" and carrying the post-chorus chant.

**Instrumentation:**
- Double-tracked distorted power-chord guitars (palm-muted verses, open choruses)
- Lead guitar octave line (pre-chorus builds, instrumental melody, final-hook harmony)
- Driving pick bass
- Fast live drum kit with open crashes, stutter builds, double-time on the last chorus
- Gang vocals and stadium claps
- One clean guitar for the bridge and outro
- Textures: phone buzz, a giggle, a stick count-in, skateboard wheels and a scooter engine buried low under the bridge, dawn birds and forecourt ambience under the last chord

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: the phone, the vote, her laughing at the verdict
- Verse 1: inventory of red flags, every one of them appealing
- Pre-chorus: the reviews, one star, she knows exactly what this is
- Chorus: the hook, huge and joyful — bad idea, good night
- Verse 2: the night itself — skate park, basement gig, chips, marker on her arm
- Instrumental: lead melody, drum breakdown, crowd whoa oh, full stop
- Bridge: the scooter at 2 a.m., quiet then building — maybe he ghosts, she'll still have this
- Final chorus: varied lines, biggest shouts
- Post-chorus: the chant — they were right, and it was still a good night
- Outro: sunrise, the gas station curb, "ask me tomorrow"

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – one palm-muted guitar, phone buzz, a giggle]

```
My best friend texted, "Do not do it,"
My sister sent a warning too.
The group chat voted, 7 to 0,
Every single one of them said you.
```

- Scene: Her bedroom, early evening, getting ready. Phone propped on the dresser.
- Shot: Close-up of the phone: message after message landing (composited) — "DO NOT", "girl no", a poll result 7–0. Her face in the mirror behind it, eyeliner half done, grinning.
- Cut to: Her reading the last message, laughing out loud, tossing the phone onto the bed.
- Her reaction: Delighted, not deterred. She finishes the eyeliner.
- Lighting: Warm bedroom lamp, the phone glow cool on her chin.

### [verse 1 – full band crashes in]

```
You got a scar above your eyebrow and a story to go with it,
Chipped black polish and a board with the trucks worn thin.
You said, "I know what they say," and I said, "Yeah, so do I,"
Then you held out your hand and I said, "Let's begin."
```

- Scene: A street corner at dusk. He is already there, skateboard under one foot.
- Shots: Close-up of the scar through his eyebrow, then his chipped black-painted nails on the board's edge, then the worn trucks. He says something; she answers; he holds out a hand.
- Cut to: Her taking the hand, a shrug to camera.
- Emotion: Two people who both know better and are doing it anyway.
- Lighting: Blue dusk, one sodium streetlight warming up.

```
You showed up on a scooter with a cracked headlight,
Said, "Hop on," and I looked at the sky like, "Really?"
Everyone I love has got a spreadsheet of your damage,
And I'm about to put my name on it, honestly.
```

- Scene: The scooter at the kerb, one headlight cracked and flickering.
- Shot: Her looking up at the sky, exasperated and amused, then swinging a leg over the seat.
- Cut to: A fast flash of a spreadsheet on a phone (composited, no readable text — just rows and a red column), then her thumb adding a row.
- Emotion: Eyes wide open. She is choosing this.
- Lighting: Headlight flicker on her face.

```
Your jacket smells like a gas station slushie,
Your playlist is loud and it's ninety percent screaming.
You laugh at your own jokes before you finish them,
And somehow every red flag has me leaning in.
```

- Scene: The scooter moving, her arms around his denim jacket.
- Shots: Her face pressed to his shoulder, nose wrinkling then smiling. A tinny speaker in his pocket blasting something screamy. Him half-turning to shout a joke and cracking up before the punchline.
- Cut to: Her leaning in closer instead of away.
- Lighting: Passing streetlights strobing across them.

### [pre-chorus – stutter build]

```
Yeah I read the reviews, one star, one star,
Every girl you dated left a warning in the parking lot.
I know exactly what this is,
And I'm doing it anyway, ready or not.
```

- Scene: A car park behind a strip of shops. Faceless girls in the background, backs turned.
- Shots: Quick cuts of one star, one star — hands holding up a single finger (composited stars, no text). Her walking straight past the warnings toward him.
- Her reaction: A knowing look at camera: yes, she read them.
- Lighting: Harsh white car-park floodlight, long shadows.

### [chorus – huge, gang vocals]

```
You're a bad idea, but a good night,
Wrong on paper, but the timing's right.
I'll regret you in the morning, that's the deal,
But tonight I'm gonna feel what I feel.
Whoa oh, tell my friends I'm sorry,
Whoa oh, tell my mum don't worry.
You're a bad idea, but a good night,
And the best ones always start with a red light.
```

- Scene: The scooter tearing through the city at night, then the skate park gate.
- Shots: Her arms out on the back of the scooter, screaming the hook into the wind. A crumpled paper (the "spreadsheet") flying out of her hand behind them. On each "whoa oh," a punch-in on her face, mouth wide, then his. On "red light," the scooter stopped at an empty junction, the red light glowing on both of them — then running it.
- Emotion: Pure, loud joy.
- Lighting: Neon and sodium streaks, the red traffic light as the hero colour.

### [verse 2 – the night itself]

```
Skate park after dark, you're teaching me to drop in,
I'm screaming on the ramp and you're screaming louder.
Concrete kissed my knee, you kissed it better,
Then you laughed and said, "I told you I was trouble."
```

- Scene: An empty concrete skate park under floodlights.
- Shots: Her at the lip of a small ramp on his board, terrified and laughing. She drops in, wobbles, he's screaming encouragement louder than she's screaming. She falls; a scraped knee; he crouches, kisses the graze theatrically and laughs.
- Emotion: Chaos, care, no dignity, no need for it.
- Lighting: Cold floodlights, warm faces.

```
Basement gig at midnight, your friend's band is terrible,
The ceiling's dripping and the amp keeps cutting out.
You pulled me in the pit and held onto my hoodie,
And I sang words I didn't know at the top of my mouth.
```

- Scene: A packed basement gig, low ceiling, a drip coming through it.
- Shots: A four-piece band that is genuinely terrible, the amp dying mid-song and the crowd cheering anyway. He drags her into the pit by the hoodie sleeve and keeps a fist in the back of it so she doesn't go under. Her shouting lyrics she doesn't know, eyes shut.
- Emotion: Sweaty, deafening, alive.
- Lighting: One red bulb, phone torches, sweat shine.

```
Chips from the van with your last four coins,
You said, "I'm broke," I said, "I noticed, I don't care."
Your friend's band's name in marker on my forearm,
Your number on yours, and half of it isn't there.
```

- Scene: A chip van outside the venue, steam and salt.
- Shots: Four coins counted onto the counter. One paper cone of chips shared. Marker scrawl on her forearm (an abstract mark, not text); a half-rubbed-off scrawl on his. Both holding their arms up to compare and laughing.
- Emotion: Broke and glowing.
- Lighting: The van's fluorescent strip, breath in the cold.

### [pre-chorus – bigger]

```
Yeah I read the reviews, one star, one star,
But the reviews never mentioned the way you laugh.
I know exactly what this is,
And I'm doing it anyway, don't do the math.
```

- Scene: Back on the scooter, the chips gone.
- Shots: The one-star fingers again in quick flashes, then his laugh, unguarded, head thrown back. Her face watching it. She shakes her head at the imaginary maths.
- Lighting: Streetlight strobing.

### [chorus]

(repeat)

- Scene: The night in motion — the skate park, the basement, the scooter, intercut on the beat.
- Shots: The board sliding across concrete on "bad idea," the pit jumping on "good night." "Whoa oh" — the whole basement crowd's hands up. "Red light" — another junction, another red light, run.
- Emotion: Bigger, wilder.
- Lighting: More saturated, more red.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: The night at full speed, no lyrics.
- Shots: The lead guitar melody over the scooter flying down an empty dual carriageway. The drum breakdown as the basement crowd claps in unison. The crowd "whoa oh" — the whole basement, phone torches up. His board kick-flipped under the floodlights in slow motion. The sudden stop: black, then the two of them frozen at a red light, engine idling.
- Lighting: Streaks, then black.

### [bridge – stripped, then building]

```
Two on a scooter, no helmets, sorry mother,
Doing thirty through the roundabout at two.
The streetlights blinking orange like they're judging us,
And I'm holding on and laughing, and so are you.
```

- Scene: An empty roundabout at 2 a.m., the lights switched to blinking amber.
- Shots: The scooter circling the roundabout twice for no reason. Her chin on his shoulder, no helmet, a mock-apologetic look to camera. The amber lights blinking over both of them.
- Emotion: Quiet joy, the night at its softest.
- Lighting: Amber blink, otherwise dark.

```
Maybe you'll ghost me by Thursday,
Maybe you'll forget my name.
But I'll have this, the wind and the sirens,
And the night I said yes to the flame.
```

- Scene: Still circling, slower.
- Shots: Her eyes closed, hair whipping. A distant siren passing on the ring road, blue flicker on the underpass wall. Her opening her eyes and smiling anyway.
- Emotion: She has already priced in the ending.
- Lighting: Amber, a passing blue.

```
They'll say, "You should've known better," and I did,
I knew better and I did it anyway.
Better never made me laugh until I couldn't breathe,
Better never had a scar and a story to say.
```

- Scene: The scooter pulling off the roundabout, the toms building.
- Shots: Her mouthing "I know" to camera. Him turning back with the scar and the grin. The guitars roar back in as they accelerate down the exit.
- Emotion: Resolve, and a dare.
- Lighting: Sodium picking up speed.

### [final chorus – double-time drums, harmony guitar, loudest shouts]

```
You're a bad idea, but a good night,
Wrong on paper, but the timing's right.
I'll regret you in the morning, if I do,
But right now the only plan I've got is you.
Whoa oh, tell my friends I'm sorry,
Whoa oh, tell my mum don't worry.
You're a bad idea, but a good night,
And the best ones always start with a red light.
```

- Scene: The greatest-hits of the night, faster.
- Shots: Every red light of the night, cut on the beat. Her on the ramp again, landing it this time. The pit, the chips, the roundabout. "Whoa oh" — her and the basement crowd. "The only plan I've got is you" — her hand tightening on his jacket.
- Emotion: Release.
- Lighting: The most saturated frames of the video.

### [post-chorus – chant over full band]

```
Bad idea, bad idea, good night,
Bad idea, bad idea, good night,
7 to 0, they were right,
Bad idea, but a good night.
```

- Scene: The phone poll again, now a screen of unread messages (composited).
- Shots: The 7–0 result. Her flipping the phone face-down on the scooter seat. Her and him shouting the chant at each other, nose to nose, laughing.
- Emotion: Conceding the vote, keeping the night.
- Lighting: The first grey of dawn.

### [outro – one clean guitar, sunrise]

```
Sun's coming up on a gas station curb,
Slushie for breakfast, blue tongue, no sleep.
Your board under my feet, your jacket on my shoulders,
Forty messages waiting and I'm not gonna read.
You said, "So was it worth it," and I said, "Ask me tomorrow,"
Then I kissed you like a secret I don't plan to keep.
```

- Scene: A gas station forecourt at sunrise, the two of them on the kerb.
- Shots: A blue slushie shared with two straws; she sticks out a blue tongue. His board under her sneakers, his denim jacket over her shoulders. The phone lit up with forty notifications (composited), placed screen-down on the concrete. He asks; she shrugs "ask me tomorrow"; she kisses him. Final frame: the sun clearing the forecourt roof, the two of them small on the kerb, the board rolling away a foot on its own.
- Emotion: Tired, blue-tongued, completely worth it.
- Lighting: Gold dawn, long shadows, clean.
