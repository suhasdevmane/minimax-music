# Better Without the Ending — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
The relationship was genuinely good. She is not rewriting history, pretending it was toxic, or making him a villain. She simply refuses to let the last twelve pages decide what the whole book was — so she keeps the good chapters, takes the pen back, and starts the next one.

**Mood:**
Warm, literate, generous. A moving-on song with no bitterness in it at all, which is what makes it hit. Big, open, singable.

**Message:**
"An ending is a door, it is not a verdict."

**Genre:**
Anthemic guitar pop. Think: Lucy Dacus x Kacey Musgraves x Coldplay — acoustic-led verses, claps and a stadium chorus, writing that trusts a plain sentence.

**Tempo & Key:**
102 BPM, 4/4, E flat major. No modulation. The chorus is already the biggest thing in the record the first time it lands; the final chorus grows by strings, choir, doubled drums and a returning eighth line rather than by key change.

**Vocal setup:**
Lead: young adult female, level and conversational in the verses, full-throated in the choruses, nearly spoken at the top of the bridge. Backing: stacked harmonies on every chorus, group claps and stomps on the post-chorus.

**Instrumentation:**
- Close-mic acoustic guitar (the spine); jangling electric doubling the choruses and taking the solo
- Warm electric bass
- Hand claps, tambourine, stomps
- Soft kick, shaker, rim click; full driving backbeat from the first chorus; rolling floor tom out of the bridge
- Upright piano from the second pre-chorus; strings and low choir pad on the bridge rebuild and final chorus
- Ambient: a typewriter carriage, pages turning, one page torn

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: a bad last page and a pen that ran out
- Verse one: how good it actually was, and the moment he started skipping to the back
- Pre-chorus: the red pen, and what she keeps
- Chorus: the thesis, sung at full size
- Verse two: the desk moved, the kind no, the market on her own
- Bridge: the last page was never the review of the book
- Final chorus: the same words, plus the line she could not sing before
- Outro: how she will answer when people ask

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro — one acoustic guitar, paper and typewriter noise]

```
Three whole years and a bad last page,
A pen that ran out in the middle of a line.
I'm not tearing up the whole thing,
I'm just taking back an end that isn't mine.
```

- Scene: A desk against a dim wall in a flat before the video's light arrives. A typewriter, a stack of pages, a dead pen.
- Shot: Macro of a pen dying mid-word on a page, the ink going grey and then nothing.
- Cut to: Her hand lifting the last page off the stack and setting it face-down beside her, deliberately, not violently.
- Emotion: Level. Nothing has been decided yet, but nothing is being destroyed either.
- Lighting: Low tungsten, the desk lamp only, everything beyond it dark.

### [verse 1 — kick, shaker, warm bass]

```
It was good, I won't pretend it wasn't good,
Sunday markets, your handwriting on my door.
You learned how I take my coffee, I learned your dad's jokes,
We were a decent book, and I'd read it once more.
Then somewhere in the middle you got tired of the plot,
Started skipping to the back to see how it would end.
And a story knows the minute a reader stops believing,
It goes quiet, and it doesn't pretend.
```

- Scene: The good years, shot as warmly and truthfully as the present.
- Shot: A Sunday market — hands over produce, a paper bag splitting, both of them laughing at it; a note in his handwriting stuck to a front door.
- Cut to: A kitchen where he makes her coffee correctly without asking; a table of his family laughing at a joke she now knows the ending of.
- Emotion: Real fondness with no varnish on it. He is not a villain in this video and must never be shot as one.
- Lighting: Warm daylight, slightly soft, but not the dreamy overexposure of a fantasy — this happened.

### [pre-chorus 1 — claps on the offbeat]

```
So I sat down with a red pen and two years,
And I crossed out the part where I got small.
Kept the markets, kept the jokes, kept the good bits,
Kept the story, let go of the fall.
```

- Scene: The editing, done properly, at a desk, with a red pen.
- Shot: Macro of a red line drawn through one paragraph — and only one — on a full page. The rest of the page is left alone.
- Cut to: Pages being sorted into two piles: a large one she keeps and a very small one she does not.
- Emotion: Craft, not rage. She is editing, not burning.
- Lighting: The desk lamp joined by the first daylight of the video from a window behind her.

### [chorus 1 — full band, claps, biggest thing in the record]

```
The story was good, I'm just better without the ending,
Better without those last twelve pages of pretending.
Keep the summer, keep the songs, keep how it began,
I'm taking back the pen and writing where I stand.
It didn't need a villain, it didn't need a war,
It just needed to be over, and it's over, I'm sure.
The story was good, I'm just better without the ending.
```

- Scene: The room and the woman both opening up.
- Shot: She pushes the desk across the floor to the window, the curtains going back, daylight flooding a room that has been dim for the entire video so far.
- Cut to: Wide of her standing in the light with the pages in her hands, then walking clean out of the edge of the frame and leaving the empty room and the open window in shot.
- Emotion: Release, sung at full size.
- Lighting: The single biggest lighting change in the piece, and it happens on the downbeat.

### [verse 2 — acoustic and rim click]

```
I moved the desk to the window on a Thursday,
Put a plant where the second chair had sat.
You texted, could we talk about it properly,
And I sent a kind no, and I left it at that.
I went back to the market on my own on Sunday,
Bought the loud yellow flowers you always said were wrong.
Nobody died. I came home. I made dinner.
And I ate the whole thing with the radio on.
```

- Scene: The ordinary week that follows a decision.
- Shot: A plant carried in and set down where a second chair used to be; the chair itself out on a landing with a note on it for whoever wants it.
- Cut to: A phone, a message arriving, a short reply typed and sent without a pause (composited); the same Sunday market alone, buying enormous yellow flowers.
- Emotion: Contentment that refuses to be dramatic. The flatness of *nobody died* is the joke and the point.
- Lighting: Bright, plain, honest. The flowers are the loudest thing in the frame.

### [pre-chorus 2 — piano joins the claps]

```
So I sat down with a red pen and a good lamp,
And I cut every line where I asked to be allowed.
Kept the market, kept the music, kept the mornings,
And I let the ending go, and I said it out loud.
```

- Scene: The second edit, deeper than the first.
- Shot: A page with a whole run of lines struck through, all of them beginning with the same apologetic phrase.
- Cut to: Her reading a line aloud to the empty room and nodding at it; the torn last pages going into a bin, unceremoniously.
- Emotion: Certainty arriving.
- Lighting: Late afternoon at the window, the lamp on anyway.

### [chorus 2 — octave guitar and choir pad]

```
The story was good, I'm just better without the ending,
Better without those last twelve pages of pretending.
Keep the summer, keep the songs, keep how it began,
I'm taking back the pen and writing where I stand.
It didn't need a villain, it didn't need a war,
It just needed to be over, and it's over, I'm sure.
The story was good, I'm just better without the ending.
```

- Scene: The flat becoming a place someone writes in rather than a place someone waits in.
- Shot: Pages pinned along a wall in order, a new stack starting beside the typewriter, her feet on the desk.
- Cut to: Her walking out of the front door into a street in daylight, filmed from inside the hallway so she exits into brightness.
- Emotion: Occupancy again, but louder.
- Lighting: Full daylight, no lamp needed for the first time.

### [instrumental — electric guitar solo, then paper alone]

- Scene: A day of writing, cut on the guitar phrases.
- Shot: The typewriter carriage returning; hands feeding a fresh page in; a bin filling with crumpled starts.
- Cut to: Her on the floor with pages spread around her in a rough circle, rearranging them; the window open, the curtain lifting.
- Final beat: A hard drop to a single bar of paper and typewriter noise on a macro of one blank page and a poised hand.
- Lighting: A whole day passing across the floor in four shots.

### [bridge — half-time, acoustic and voice]

```
For a while I let the last page grade the whole thing,
Like a bad review could reach back through the book.
It can't. The summer happened. The kitchen happened.
The way you said my name still happened, and it was good.
An ending is a door, it is not a verdict,
A place a story stops, not a place it failed.
So I keep the honest chapters and the mornings,
And I take back the pen the last one held.
```

- Scene: The argument of the song, delivered plainly, mostly to camera.
- Shot: Her sitting on the floor against the desk holding the last page, reading it and not reacting to it.
- Cut to: Three two-second warm flashes of things that genuinely happened — the market, the kitchen, a doorway with her name being said — none of them undone by what came after.
- Emotion: Generosity. This is the emotional high point and there is no anger in it.
- Lighting: Low and warm, the strings arriving with a shaft of late sun across the floorboards.

### [final chorus — strings, choir, doubled drums, the eighth line returns]

```
The story was good, I'm just better without the ending,
Better without those last twelve pages of pretending.
Keep the summer, keep the songs, keep how it began,
I've taken back the pen and I'm writing where I stand.
It didn't need a villain, it didn't need a war,
It just needed to be over, and it's over, I'm sure.
The story was good, I'm just better without the ending,
And I love the way this one begins.
```

- Scene: Out of the flat entirely, for the first and only time at scale.
- Shot: Her walking a bright street with the pages under one arm, people around her, the city ordinary and open.
- Cut to: A wide of her crossing a park with the yellow flowers, and a final push through an open doorway into a room full of light.
- Emotion: Celebration, earned, and completely free of spite.
- Lighting: The brightest sequence in the video, no shadow anywhere.

### [post-chorus — claps and stomps]

```
New page, new pen, new light on the table,
No epilogue, no note on the door,
The story was good, I'm just better without the ending,
And I'm not reading that last part anymore.
```

- Scene: The desk, transformed, in four bars.
- Shot: A fresh page going into the typewriter in a hard rhythmic cut; the lamp switched off because the daylight has it covered.
- Cut to: An empty door frame with no note on it; the bin, full, being taken out.
- Emotion: Percussive, physical, fun.
- Lighting: Midday.

### [outro — one acoustic guitar]

```
Somebody will ask me how it ended,
And I'll say the good parts never did.
I keep the markets and the mornings in a drawer,
And I left the last twelve pages where they lived.
```

- Scene: A future conversation, implied rather than shown.
- Shot: Her at a table with a friend off-camera, saying one short sentence and shrugging, warm and unbothered.
- Cut to: A drawer opening on a small ordered stack of kept pages, and closing again gently.
- Final frame: The desk at the window, a fresh page half-written in the typewriter, the room empty and full of light, held until the last chord stops ringing. No text.
- Emotion: Peace, and appetite for what is next.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the curtains going back and the whole room flooding with daylight on the downbeat of the first chorus.
- **The caption line:** *"An ending is a door, it is not a verdict."*
- **The challenge:** the red-pen edit — post one page of your own story with exactly one paragraph crossed out and the rest untouched.
- **The quote card:** *"It didn't need a villain, it didn't need a war."*
- **The comment bait:** *"Nobody died. I came home. I made dinner."* — the flattest line in the song and the one people will repeat.
