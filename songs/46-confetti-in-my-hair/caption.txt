Global Metadata
Basic Attributes: bpm is 126. key is C major, and scale is major. Party Pop with a live brass section, punchy live drums and a stadium-sized chorus, bright and warm, a morning-after record rather than a night-out record.
Global Emotional Progression: The track opens quiet and hungover, one clean electric piano chord ringing in a room with the reverb of an empty apartment, birds and traffic outside, the lead voice low and croaky and amused, no drums. The first verse brings a dry kick, a shaker and a soft palm-muted guitar under a conversational vocal that catalogues the wreckage of the room, everything close and slightly comic. The pre-chorus drops the drums to claps and a rising bass, holds one bar of near silence on the last line, then the chorus explodes into the full band with brass stabs, four-on-the-floor kick, tambourine, layered gang vocals and a bright, immediate hook that is more relief than triumph. A chanted post-chorus follows with claps and a crowd of voices. The second verse pulls back to a warmer, fuller groove with an added electric piano riff and a live bass as the song rewinds into the party itself, and it turns emotional in its last two lines when the reason for the party arrives. The second pre-chorus is bigger and shakier, laughter and tears in the same phrase. The second chorus adds a soaring brass counter-line and a higher harmony stack. The instrumental break is a full brass feature over a drum breakdown, then a filtered, distant version of the hook as if heard from another room, then a hard band re-entry. The bridge falls away to electric piano and voice with the drums gone, the most honest moment of the song, and rebuilds across the last two lines. The final chorus is the widest arrangement of the track with a full gang vocal, doubled brass and open hats, and the last post-chorus is pure chant. The outro thins to the opening electric piano and a single tambourine, calm and grateful, and ends on one unresolved chord.

Modern pop-R&B polish: controlled low end with weighty sub-bass and 808s, scooped low-mids for vocal clarity, crisp airy top end. Dynamics are contained in the verses and open dramatically in the choruses. Generous plate and hall reverb on atmospheric elements, with the lead vocal kept close and dry for intimacy.

Secondary: Palm-muted electric guitar and a melodic electric bass drive the verses. Tambourine and handclaps push the choruses. Gang vocals, six to ten voices in a room rather than a polished choir, join from the first post-chorus and take the final chorus almost entirely.
