# Call Me After Midnight — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
A woman who belongs to everybody between nine and six and to exactly one
person after twelve. The song is a boundary drawn as a flirtation: don't
text, call; don't get the daytime voice, wait for the real one. Verse two
turns it when she breaks her own rule in a stairwell at four in the
afternoon, and the bridge admits why the hour matters — the woman at noon is
a highlight reel, and the one at two in the morning is the whole thing.

**Mood:**
Low, warm, confident, unhurried. Nothing here is desperate. She is not
waiting by the phone; she has cleared a space in the night on purpose.

**Message:**
"Everybody gets the daylight. You get the rest."

**Genre:**
Synth-R&B with eighties production. Think: The Weeknd x Dua Lipa, with the
gated snare and polysynth pads of a mid-eighties Sade or Whitney record under
a modern R&B vocal.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 96 BPM, 4/4
- Key: C♯ minor (dark, neon, sits low in her chest voice)
- No modulation. The final chorus lifts by arrangement: the biggest gated
  snare and widest pads on the record, plus a soaring synth lead.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, clipped and slightly weary in verse one,
  warm and open in the choruses, almost spoken in the bridge.
- Backing vocals: stacked breathy harmonies under every hook; a stacked
  repeating phrase on the post-chorus.

**Instrumentation:**
- Warm analogue polysynth pads (first bar to last)
- Chorused fretless-feel electric bass carrying the hook figure
- Bright arpeggiated polysynth from the first pre-chorus onward
- Gated reverb snare — its first appearance is the last beat of pre-chorus one
- Light electronic kick, closed hats
- Tight muted electric guitar chop; chorused guitar lead in the break
- Saxophone-shaped synth lead: the break and the final chorus
- Electric piano: the bridge alone
- Distant dial tone, low city hum, the click of a handset lifted

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: midnight, the day being put down
- Verse 1: the daytime self, described from the outside
- Pre-chorus: the rule stated — call, don't text
- Chorus: the hook, the whole eighties palette open
- Verse 2: she breaks her own rule and he notices the difference
- Instrumental: synth lead and guitar trading over the arpeggio
- Bridge: half-time, one electric piano, why the hour matters
- Final chorus: the biggest version of the same promise
- Outro: six in the morning, the line still open

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – pad, dial tone, no drums]

```
Twelve o'clock, and the city drops a gear,
Laptop shut, kettle cold, nobody here.
Everybody had a piece of me today,
Now the light goes low and I'm not giving it away.
```

- Shots: A bedroom lit magenta and cyan by a window sign across the street. A laptop lid closing, the room going dark by one degree. An untouched mug of tea gone cold on a desk. Mahima sitting on the end of the bed in the neon, still in half of her work clothes, doing nothing at all.
- Emotion and lighting: The exhale at the end of a day; magenta key from the window, cyan fill from a lamp, no white light anywhere in the film.

### [verse 1]

```
From nine to six I'm a name on somebody's list,
Nodding in an elevator where the signal won't stick.
Six voices in my ear and a badge on a string,
And a daytime voice that doesn't mean a thing.
```

- Shots: Daylight for the only time in the video, and deliberately colourless. A lanyard badge against a shirt (blank, composited). An elevator interior, four people, her nodding at somebody talking, a phone in her hand showing no bars. A meeting room seen through glass with her mouth moving and no sound.
- Emotion and lighting: Competent and absent; flat office daylight, desaturated, the opposite of every other frame in the film.

```
By eleven I've said yes about a hundred times,
By half past I've run out of the good kind.
Then the hall light goes off and the day lets go,
And my shoulders come down and my voice goes low.
```

- Shots: Her saying yes in four different rooms, cut fast, the same nod each time. Her alone in a stairwell for two seconds with her eyes shut. A hallway light switching off at the end of a corridor. Back in the neon bedroom: her shoulders dropping visibly on the beat, one long breath out.
- Emotion and lighting: The changeover; the last daylight frame in the video ends here and the neon never leaves again.

### [pre-chorus]

```
Don't text me, I don't want it typed,
I want the low and lazy sound of you alive.
Let it ring twice, I'll get it on the third,
Nothing in the world I'd rather have than your word.
```

- Shots: A phone face-up on a duvet with a blank message screen (composited), her thumb hovering and then pushing it away. A corded landline handset on the bedside table, unusual and deliberate, the cord curling across the sheet. Her hand resting on the cradle. Two silent rings implied by the light changing.
- Emotion and lighting: Anticipation; the arpeggio arriving as the neon starts to pulse very slightly.

### [chorus – full eighties palette]

```
Call me after midnight, that's when I'm all yours,
When the office goes dark and they lock all the doors.
Nobody wants a thing from me at half past two,
Nothing left to give, and I'm giving it to you.

Call me after midnight, let the line run long,
Say it slow, say it wrong, keep the talking on.
Every hour before this one was the world's, not ours,
Call me after midnight, that's when I'm all yours.
```

- Shots: The handset lifted and held to her ear, close, the coiled cord swinging. Her lying back across the bed with the phone on her chest, the ceiling magenta above her. A wide of the window: the city dark, one office tower going out floor by floor. Her mouth close to the receiver, smiling at something we don't hear.
- Emotion and lighting: The record's warmest frames; deep magenta and cyan, gated-snare cuts on the beat.

### [verse 2]

```
Tuesday you were three time zones out of line,
And your midnight came down sideways into mine.
So I broke my own rule in a stairwell at four,
Whispered like a thief with my back to the door.
```

- Shots: A world clock on a wall with blank faces (composited). A fire-escape stairwell in afternoon light, her sitting three steps up with the phone to her ear and her back to the door, whispering. Somebody pushing the door and her going instantly silent, then carrying on.
- Emotion and lighting: The rule breaking; one shaft of afternoon light through a wired-glass window, the only warm daylight in the film.

```
You said, that's the day voice, and I said, it's not,
Then I dropped it half an octave and proved your point.
So the rule's got a hole in the shape of you,
And I'm in no hurry to make it new.
```

- Shots: Extreme close-up of her mouth in the stairwell, the smile arriving as she is caught out. Her deliberately dropping her voice, chin down, one hand cupped around the receiver. Back in the neon bedroom that night: her writing nothing down, just grinning at the ceiling.
- Emotion and lighting: Caught and delighted; the stairwell warm, the return to neon abrupt.

### [pre-chorus 2]

```
Don't text me, I don't want it typed,
I want the low and lazy sound of you alive.
Let it ring once this time, I'm already awake,
Been holding my real voice back for your sake.
```

- Shots: The landline in the dark before it rings, held for four full seconds. Her already sitting up, already looking at it. Her hand on the handset before the first ring finishes.
- Emotion and lighting: Faster, hungrier; the neon pulsing harder with the arpeggio.

### [chorus]

(repeat)

- Shots: Her walking the flat with the cord stretched to its limit. Her feet up the wall, upside down on the bed, phone at her ear. The window sign buzzing and flickering. Two shots of her laughing with no sound.
- Emotion and lighting: Bigger and freer; the same palette pushed harder, an octave synth lead reflected in a longer, wider camera move.

### [instrumental]

- Shots: The synth lead over the city: a slow pan across a skyline with almost every window dark. The coiled phone cord, macro, swinging. An ashtray-clean bedside table with only the phone on it. Her asleep-adjacent, eyes half shut, phone still at her ear. A brief drop to near-black with only the window sign on.
- Emotion and lighting: Suspended; slow drifting camera, no cuts on the beat.

### [bridge – half-time, one electric piano]

```
The girl at noon is a highlight reel and a good coat,
She says the right thing twice and never the rest.
The one at two in the morning has a rougher throat,
And she'll tell you the whole thing straight from her chest.
So if you want the real one, you know when to call,
And nobody in daylight ever gets her at all.
```

- Shots: A split idea done in cuts, not a split screen: the daytime version — flat light, good coat, a corridor, a professional smile — cut against the night version, neon, hair down, no makeup, telling the truth into a handset. Four alternations, each one longer than the last, ending on the night version held for eight full seconds.
- Emotion and lighting: The turn; the only place in the film the two grades sit side by side, and the daylight loses.

### [final chorus – biggest arrangement on the record]

```
Call me after midnight, that's when I'm all yours,
When the office goes dark and they lock all the doors.
Nobody wants a thing from me at half past two,
Nothing left to give, and I'm giving it to you.

Call me after midnight, let the line run long,
We can fall asleep together with the phone on.
Every hour before this one was the world's, not ours,
Call me after midnight, that's when I'm all yours.
```

- Shots: The widest frames in the film. The window thrown open, the city cold outside and the room hot. Her dancing slowly on her own with the handset at her ear and the cord fully extended. The bed with the phone lying on the second pillow.
- Emotion and lighting: Full release without leaving the room; brighter neon, the synth lead mirrored by a rising crane move.

### [post-chorus]

```
After midnight, after midnight, that's when I'm all yours,
Let it ring, let it ring, till the morning is ours.
After midnight, after midnight, that's when I'm all yours,
Call me, call me, the whole night is ours.
```

- Shots: Four repeating beats: the handset, the window sign, her mouth, the cord. Same four, same order, on each line.
- Emotion and lighting: Rhythmic and hypnotic; hard cuts on the gated snare.

### [outro – pad, arpeggio, dawn]

```
Six in the morning and the line is still on,
You fell asleep first and I let it run long.
The city's coming up and I'm still on the floor,
Call me after midnight, and I'll answer once more.
```

- Shots: Grey-blue first light arriving through the window and killing the neon. Mahima on the floor with her back against the bed, handset still at her ear, eyes closed, listening to somebody breathing. The window sign switching itself off. Final shot: the handset lying on the carpet beside her, the cord running out of frame, the line still open.
- Emotion and lighting: Peaceful; the first neutral light in the film since verse one, and this time it is welcome.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the coiled landline cord swinging as the handset comes up on *"call me after midnight."*
- **The challenge:** the two-voices cut — post the bridge and invite people to film their noon voice and their two-in-the-morning voice back to back.
- **The caption line:** *"Everybody gets the daylight. You get the rest."*
- **The quote:** *"The girl at noon is a highlight reel and a good coat."*
- **The still:** the handset on the carpet at dawn with the line still open.
