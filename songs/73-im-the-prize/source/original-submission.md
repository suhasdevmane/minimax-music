# I'm the Prize — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
The quiet end of being kept as an option. A woman who was on somebody's shortlist, who learned to read silence as if it were a language, decides she is not a contestant in her own life. She does not argue, does not insult anyone, and does not ask to be chosen. She puts on gold, walks out onto the most ordinary street in the city, and treats it as a runway. The correction is not "you'll regret this" — it is "you were never grading me."

**Mood:**
Slow, certain, unbothered. Cold light and warm skin. Confidence at walking pace, never at shouting volume. There is a little dry humour in the verses and none at all in the bridge, which is where the song stops being about him.

**Message:**
"I'm not the game, baby, I'm the prize."

**Genre:**
Trap-pop with melodic-rap verses and a fully sung chorus. Think: Summer Walker's low-register calm x Doechii's cadence control x Normani's finish — 808s, a bare slightly detuned upright piano, rolling hats, and a top line that never raises its voice.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 96 BPM, 4/4
- Key: B minor (dark and roomy under a bright, patient top line)
- No modulation. The lift is arrangement only: octave doubles and low brass-like stabs in chorus two, layered harmonies and a raised top line in the final chorus, and the hook's last line rewritten each time it returns.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female. Close and observational in the intro, rhythmic and pocketed in both melodic-rap verses (dry, low in the chest, landing just behind the beat), rising into melody across the pre-choruses, fully sung and wide in the choruses, near-spoken in the bridge.
- One voice only. There is no feature and no answering vocal; the rap is a delivery change inside the same performance.
- Backing vocals: stacked female harmonies on every chorus, layered male and female harmonies on the final hook, a clipped chant on the post-chorus, wordless echoes drifting through the outro.

**Instrumentation:**
- Slightly detuned upright piano (the four-note street figure; muted stabs under the rap; full chords in the choruses; alone in the bridge and outro)
- Deep 808 with long portamento glides; sub-bass drone under the intro
- Rolling trap hi-hats with triplet rolls; snappy rimshot clap
- Bright bell-like synth counter-melody (choruses); low brass-like stabs (second and final choruses)
- Rising string pad (instrumental break, final chorus); pitched vocal chop of the hook (break, post-chorus)
- Textures: distant traffic, wet tyre hiss, the electrical hum of a shop sign, footsteps on wet pavement

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: an ordinary street in gold light; he has been running the numbers on her
- Verse 1 (melodic rap): the shortlist, the latched door, the dialect of waiting
- Pre-chorus: she puts the gold on and walks out
- Chorus: the correction, sung, wide, no insult in it
- Verse 2 (melodic rap): her trophy shelf — a rent receipt, a scar, a plant, a repaired friendship
- Pre-chorus: the same street, the same walk, and she is not late
- Instrumental: half-time switch, then one bare piano note
- Bridge: the turn — a prize is not something two women fight over
- Final chorus: fullest arrangement, the last line rewritten
- Post-chorus: four-beat chant, slow walk, gold light
- Outro: the same corner shop, the same tarmac, and the only new thing is her

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – piano and sub drone, street ambience, close and unhurried]

```
Gold light on an ordinary street,
Corner shop, wet tarmac—my own two feet.
You've been running the numbers on me all this time,
Like the risk is mine.
```

- Scene: A plain residential street after rain, an hour after sunset. A corner shop with a lit sign is the only bright thing in the frame.
- Shot: Macro on the tarmac — the shop sign broken up and reassembling in the water as a foot enters frame.
- Cut to: A wide from ground level, the street running away to a vanishing point, her small at the far end and not yet moving.
- Her reaction: None. She looks at the street the way you look at a room you already own.
- Lighting: Sodium orange and shop-sign white on black wet ground. Everything gold is a reflection; nothing gold is a light source yet.

### [verse 1 – melodic rap, dry and pocketed]

```
You had me on a shortlist with a couple of maybes,
Door left on the latch and your cards to your chest.
Told your boys you were "seeing where it went,"
Told me nothing at all, and I called that respect.
```

- Scene: A flat at night, months earlier. Her phone face-up on a table.
- Shot: A door left very slightly open onto a dark landing, the chain hanging loose — held long, no movement in the frame at all.
- Cut to: A pub table seen from behind a faceless man's shoulder, three faceless friends laughing; then her at home, sitting upright on the edge of a sofa in a coat she has not taken off.
- Emotion: She is not crying. She is waiting efficiently, which is worse.
- Lighting: One overhead in the flat, deliberately unflattering. The pub warm and out of focus.

```
I used to read your silence like it was a language,
Learned a dialect of waiting that nobody should learn.
Made my whole week a window you could stand at,
And you came when it suited and you left when it turned.
I'm not doing an interview, I'm not doing a trial,
I'm not auditioning for a part in my own life.
```

- Scene: The waiting, filmed as study, and then the hard turn where it stops mid-frame.
- Shot: Macro on a phone screen going dark, lighting for a notification that is not him, then dark again — three times, cut on the bar. Then her at a window from the outside, standing in the lit rectangle with the street black around her; the same window empty; then lit again, same lock-off, three nights.
- Cut to: Her closing the blind in one movement and the lit rectangle going out; then, inside, the chained door pushed properly shut and the chain lifted off and dropped.
- Emotion: Recognition rather than pain across the first four lines — this is where she works out what she was doing — then flat, decided, almost bored on the last two.
- Lighting: Interior warm, exterior cold, and she is always on the wrong side of the glass; on the turn the frame loses its only warm source and holds on black for half a beat.

### [pre-chorus]

```
So I got up slow and I put the gold on,
Walked out into a night that wasn't yours.
Every ordinary street I've been down
Turned into a runway under lamps and open doors.
```

- Scene: Getting ready, filmed as armour rather than glamour.
- Shot: Gold hoops going in, one at a time. A gold chain settling on a collarbone. A gold-toned coat lifted off a hook.
- Cut to: The front door of her building opening onto the street, the camera already retreating ahead of her.
- Emotion: Slow. Nothing about this is hurried, and the slowness is the whole point.
- Lighting: The first real gold light source of the video: a hallway bulb behind her, then streetlamps coming up as she passes under them.

### [chorus – sung, wide, no insult in it]

```
I'm not the game, baby, I'm the prize,
Not the maybe, not the almost, not the compromise.
I'm not the thing you win for turning up late,
I'm the thing you lose by making somebody wait.
Take your time, take your shot, take your chances elsewhere,
I've got gold light and a street and my own self.
I'm not the game, baby, I'm the prize,
And I'm done being somebody's nice surprise.
```

- Scene: The runway. An ordinary street treated exactly like a fashion show.
- Shots: A steady dolly retreating in front of her at walking pace. Shop shutters, a bus stop, wheelie bins, a chip shop — all lit as if they were staging. Two teenage girls on a wall look up as she passes and one of them straightens without knowing why.
- Emotion: Pride with no aggression in it. She never looks back down the street.
- Lighting: Streetlamps as key. Every lamp she passes throws gold across her and then releases her; the rhythm of the light matches the bar.

### [verse 2 – melodic rap, warmer, the hats halved]

```
There's a shelf in my place that I call the trophy room,
A rent receipt, a scar, and a plant I didn't kill,
A friendship I nearly wrecked and then repaired,
A job I said yes to before I had the skill.
None of it is shiny and all of it is mine,
And it took a lot of quiet to get here.
So when you ask me what I bring, I laugh,
Because I'm standing on a decade you weren't near.
```

- Scene: Her flat, morning, the "trophy room" — one shelf, badly lit, absolutely serious — and then the decade behind it, compressed.
- Shot: A slow macro pan along the shelf in the exact order of the lyric: a rent receipt under a stone, a forearm scar seen as she reaches past, a leggy houseplant that has clearly been kept alive by effort, a chipped mug, a lanyard on the corner.
- Cut to: A phone call taken at a kitchen table, a friend's name answered on the second ring; then four one-second inserts, each a different year in the same flat — different curtains, different kettle, same shelf, nobody else in any of them; then her sitting on the floor with her back to the shelf, laughing once, genuinely, at nothing anyone said.
- Emotion: Amused, unsentimental. These are trophies because she says they are, and the laugh is the only one in the video — keep it small and real.
- Lighting: Grey morning through one window, no styling at all, the most ordinary lighting in the film on purpose; the window light warms one degree per insert across the four cuts.

### [pre-chorus]

```
So I got up slow and I put the gold on,
Walked past every doorway I used to wait.
Every ordinary corner in this city
Turned into a runway, and I'm not late.
```

- Scene: The same getting-ready ritual, faster to cut, and then the doorways.
- Shot: Gold hoops again, same framing as before, but her hands are steadier.
- Cut to: A tracking shot past three specific doorways in a row — the flat's landing, a bar entrance, a car passenger door standing open — none of which she stops at.
- Emotion: Momentum. She is not avoiding the doorways; she has stopped seeing them.
- Lighting: Warmer than pre-chorus one. Doorway light spills out and she walks straight through it.

### [chorus]

(repeat)

- Scene: The runway again, wider and busier — the street now has real people on it who have nothing to do with her.
- Shots: The dolly is further back and lower. A bus passes between camera and subject and she is still there when it clears. A faceless man at a corner half-turns to look; the camera does not.
- Emotion: The same walk, but the world has caught up to it.
- Lighting: More sources — a taxi's headlights, a shop window, a phone torch — all gold-toned, all passing across her and letting her go.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: The half-time switch. No lyrics, no walking.
- Shots: Slow motion, a gold hoop swinging back to still. Slow motion, water thrown up by a passing wheel and lit orange. Her standing entirely still in the middle of the empty road, arms down, letting the street move around her. A hand on the shelf, straightening the plant's pot by a centimetre. Then a bare frame: the corner shop sign switching off, one bank of letters at a time, on the single piano note.
- Lighting: Everything soft and slowed, then a hard drop to one remaining light.

### [bridge – near-spoken, piano and pad, then the kit]

```
Here's the part I didn't understand for years,
A prize is not a thing two women fight about.
It isn't on a shelf and it isn't in a ring,
It's the quiet in my chest when the noise runs out.
I don't need you to lose for me to win,
I hope she's kind, I hope you're kind, I hope it's real.
And the winning was the part I built alone,
I'm the whole of it, and this is how it feels.
```

- Scene: The turn. The song stops being about him and it must look like it too, and then it turns generous without a trace of smugness.
- Shot: Her sitting on a low wall on the same street, coat open, not performing for anyone, filmed static and slightly too wide. Then a hand deleting nothing and blocking nobody — just a phone put screen-down on the wall beside her.
- Cut to: Another woman walking past on the far pavement with her own life going on. They do not interact: no look, no rivalry beat, no slow motion. She turns a corner and is gone, and the empty pavement holds. Then the shelf again, one insert, seen from the doorway of an empty flat.
- Emotion: The most honest passage in the video — direct the actor to do nothing for the first four lines. Then relief: the strings and the kit come back under the last two and she stands up on the downbeat.
- Lighting: The lamps behind her only, her face half in shadow and staying that way, until the last two lines bring the first warm frontal light on her face in the whole video.

### [final chorus – fullest arrangement, last line rewritten]

```
I'm not the game, baby, I'm the prize,
Not the maybe, not the almost, not the compromise.
I'm not the thing you win for turning up late,
I'm the thing you lose by making somebody wait.
Keep your time, keep your shot, keep your chances elsewhere,
I've got gold light and a street and my own self.
I'm not the game, baby, I'm the prize,
And I've stopped waiting to be recognized.
```

- Scene: The runway at its fullest. The street is now unmistakably staged and nobody pretends otherwise.
- Shots: The widest dolly of the video, the whole road in frame. The two teenage girls from chorus one now walking behind her, then three more people, then eight — not a crowd scene, a slow accumulation, nobody dancing.
- Emotion: Certainty. She looks into the lens once, on "I've stopped waiting to be recognized," and does not hold it long.
- Lighting: Every lamp on the street at full, plus a warm frontal source that has no diegetic explanation and does not need one.

### [post-chorus – clipped chant]

```
Slow walk, gold light, chin up, eyes ahead,
No rush, no proof, no test,
I'm not the game, baby, I'm the prize,
And I always was.
```

- Scene: Four hard cuts on the chant, half a second each.
- Shots: Boots hitting wet tarmac. A gold hoop catching a lamp. A chin lifting. The vanishing point of the street, empty and lit.
- Shot length: half a second, on the word, no exceptions.
- Lighting: Alternating cold street and warm lamp, one per cut.

### [outro – piano and drone, calm, resolved]

```
Corner shop, wet tarmac, gold across the glass,
Same street I have walked a thousand times.
Nothing here is new about tonight but me,
And that was always it, and that was always mine.
```

- Scene: The opening street, the opening framing, nothing changed except her.
- Shots: The macro of the reflected shop sign in the tarmac, matched exactly to shot one. The corner shop window with gold across the glass and her reflection in it. The ground-level wide of the street, and this time she is walking toward camera from the vanishing point.
- Final shot: She passes camera and out of frame, and the shot holds on the empty gold-lit street. Locked-off, held through the fade. No text.
- Lighting: Identical to the intro, one stop warmer.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the retreating dolly on the ordinary street with *"I'm not the game, baby, I'm the prize"* on screen — a bus stop and wheelie bins lit like a runway.
- **The challenge:** the runway walk. Film your most boring street at night, walk it at half speed, and cut on the streetlamps. No filter, no location, no budget — the point is that the street is ordinary.
- **The caption line:** *"I'm the thing you lose by making somebody wait."*
- **The quote:** *"A prize is not a thing two women fight about."*
- **The rap clip:** *"I used to read your silence like it was a language, / Learned a dialect of waiting that nobody should learn."*
- **The last frame:** the empty street still lit gold after she has gone past camera.
