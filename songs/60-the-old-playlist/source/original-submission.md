# The Old Playlist — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
A woman in her early twenties gets on the last bus home, opens a playlist she
built at nineteen and hasn't touched in three years, and hits shuffle. Every
track drops her back into a specific room, car, job or person. It is not a
song about wanting her old life back — it is a song about meeting the girl who
made the playlist and finding out she likes her.

**Mood:**
Warm, dusty, late-night. Nostalgia with the lights low. Fond rather than sad,
with one honest ache in the middle of it.

**Message:**
"I wouldn't go back. I'd just like five minutes on this bus with her."

**Genre:**
Lo-fi pop / nostalgia pop with a big cinematic chorus. Think: Clairo x
Gracie Abrams, with a Joji-style tape haze underneath.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 88 BPM, 4/4
- Key: D major (open, warm, sits low in her chest voice)
- The choruses lose the vinyl noise and open into full-fidelity width; the
  final chorus adds strings and a guitar countermelody without changing key.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, close and almost speaking in the verses,
  full and slightly raspy on the hook.
- Backing vocals: stacked harmonies on the hook, a chopped and pitched vocal
  fragment through the instrumental, wordless harmony on the last line.

**Instrumentation:**
- Rhodes electric piano with tremolo and tape flutter (the spine)
- Dusty sampled kick and rimshot, shaker, tambourine, round filtered bass
- Tape-warped clean electric guitar loop; soft muted trumpet in verse two
- Warm analogue pads; light strings (violas, cello) for the final chorus
- Textures: vinyl crackle, tape hiss, bus ambience, engine hum, door hiss,
  a very short tape-stop between sections

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: last bus, low battery, a playlist name she stopped saying
- Verses: three tracks and three memories, then the song she always skipped
- Pre-choruses: she has opened something she meant to leave shut
- Chorus: the hook, full width, homesick for a year and not a place
- Instrumental: the bus, the window, the sleeping passengers
- Bridge: the turn — she would not go back, she only wants five minutes
- Final chorus and post-chorus: gratitude, then one percent of battery
- Outro: the doors open, she adds a new song to the old playlist

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro — bus ambience, vinyl, Rhodes, no drums]

```
Last bus out and the windows are sweating,
Twelve percent of battery to spare.
I push the old white earbuds in and settle,
And a name I stopped saying is there.
```

- Scene: The upper deck of a night bus, almost empty, windows fogged.
- Shot: Wide from the back of the deck, her alone six rows down, streetlights strobing across the ceiling. Cut to: Her phone, low-battery warning, thumb scrolling past newer playlists to an old one.
- Emotion: Tired, unguarded, not expecting anything.
- Lighting: Green-blue bus interior strip light, sodium orange rushing past the glass.

### [verse 1 — dusty kit enters]

```
Track one is a summer of working the doubles,
Grease on my sleeve and the walk home at three.
Track two is a back seat with somebody's cousin,
All of us shouting the part we can't reach.

Skip, and it lands on a blue kitchen table,
A boy who was leaving before the leaves turned.
I made this in a bedroom with a map on the ceiling
And a whole lot of nerve I hadn't earned.
```

- Scene: Four memory inserts, warm and grainy, each triggered by a track change, ending in the bedroom where the playlist was built.
- Shot: A fast-food back kitchen at closing, her in a cap wiping a counter, a grease stripe up her forearm; then her walking home alone under sodium lights with the same earbuds in; then a car at night, four teenagers, all shouting the high note, someone's cousin driving with one hand.
- Cut to: A blue formica kitchen table, two mugs, a boy's hands only, a rucksack already by the door; then her teenage bedroom, a paper world map taped across the ceiling, a laptop on the duvet, the playlist being typed. Her thumb moves to skip, hovers, doesn't.
- Emotion: Delight before recognition, then a flinch, then something softer.
- Lighting: Memories warm and slightly overexposed with 8mm grain; the kitchen cold white; the bedroom one warm lamp and a laptop glow.

### [pre-chorus 1 — filter opens, shaker in]

```
I only meant to check the time and get home,
I did not mean to open up a door.
But the first four seconds hit me like a hallway
I have not walked down in three years or more.
```

- Scene: Present, tightening.
- Shots: Her lock screen and the time; then her sitting up straighter, pressing an earbud in harder; the fogged window with her fingertip drawing and stopping. Cut to: A one-second flash of a school corridor, empty, lights on, nobody in it.
- Emotion: Caught out by herself.
- Lighting: Bus light dimming as the bus enters an underpass, then blooming back.

### [chorus 1 — full width, hi-fi, harmonies]

```
Hit shuffle on the old playlist and I'm nineteen again,
Cheap perfume, borrowed denim, the whole night to spend.
Every song is a door I never got around to closing,
Every chorus is a room with all the lights still glowing.
Nothing bad has happened yet inside a three-minute track,
I know how it ends, and I still want it back.
So let the window fog, let the long way be the plan,
Hit shuffle on the old playlist and I'm nineteen again.
```

- Scene: The chorus is where the bus becomes the memory. The seats fill with the people from the songs.
- Shots: The bus window reflection showing her at nineteen instead of now. A borrowed denim jacket appearing on her shoulders in a match cut. A long tracking shot down the aisle where every pair of seats is a different lit room from a different year. Her walking that aisle, touching the seat backs.
- Emotion: Wide open, almost happy, one degree from crying.
- Lighting: Warm practicals inside each seat-row memory; the aisle itself still cold blue.

### [verse 2 — kit back, muted trumpet]

```
Track eleven is the one I always used to skip,
He was the only reason it was ever on the list.
Tonight I let it play the whole way to the ending,
And it's only a good song. That's the whole of what I missed.

There's a voice memo buried near the bottom of the album,
Somebody laughing, half a sentence, then it's gone.
I can't tell you what was funny anymore,
But I can tell you who I was when I put it on.
```

- Scene: The skipped track allowed to run, then an accidental recording nobody meant to keep.
- Shot: Extreme close-up of her thumb resting beside the skip arrow, not pressing; the progress bar crossing the middle; her face changing very slightly at the end, an eyebrow going up, because it was just a song.
- Cut to: A waveform on the phone screen (composited), then a memory insert of friends on a stairwell, someone starting a sentence, everyone laughing over the end of it, then back to her holding her breath through the silence at the end of the clip.
- Emotion: Relief arriving as anticlimax, then grief with no target, then affection.
- Lighting: One overhead bus light directly above her, everything else dark; the stairwell memory in flat fluorescent, deliberately unglamorous and real.

### [pre-chorus 2 — warmer, fuller]

```
I only meant to ride until my stop,
I did not mean to fall through thirty tracks.
But a bassline from a song I half remember
Hands me that whole summer, all of it, right back.
```

- Scene: The bus passing her actual stop.
- Shots: The stop request button unpressed; the stop sliding by the window; her not moving. Close-up of her foot tapping a bassline against the seat frame.
- Emotion: A small, deliberate act of staying in it.
- Lighting: Streetlight rhythm speeding up across her face.

### [chorus 2 — biggest low end]

```
Hit shuffle on the old playlist and I'm nineteen again,
Cheap perfume, borrowed denim, the whole night to spend.
Every song is a door I never got around to closing,
Every chorus is a room with all the lights still glowing.
Nothing bad has happened yet inside a three-minute track,
I know how it ends, and I still want it back.
So let the window fog, let the long way be the plan,
Hit shuffle on the old playlist and I'm nineteen again.
```

- Scene: The aisle-of-rooms idea, pushed further and faster.
- Shots: The rooms now spill into the aisle: a beach towel, a birthday cake, a wet festival field, a hospital waiting-room chair, a bedroom floor covered in clothes. She walks faster through them. On the last line the bus is empty again and she is back in her own seat.
- Emotion: Overwhelmed in the good way, then the drop back.
- Lighting: Each room a different colour temperature; the return to the seat noticeably colder.

### [instrumental] — scene block only

- Scene: The bus keeps going. No memories. Just the night.
- Shots: The driver's mirror, his eyes, the empty deck behind. Rain starting on the glass and the reflected phone screen dimming. A sleeping passenger downstairs with his head on the window. Her own reflection, present-day, looking back at her without a costume change. Her hand wiping a clear circle in the fog and the city sliding through it.
- Emotion: Quiet. The comedown after the montage.
- Lighting: Only the bus strip light and passing signage; the coldest passage in the video.

### [bridge — half-time, Rhodes and voice]

```
I wouldn't go back, and I want that on the record,
I like my life, I like my quiet, I like my bed.
I only want five minutes on this bus beside her,
Just enough to tell her what's ahead.
That it's going to cost her more than she has planned,
And she still gets there. Tired, but she lands.
```

- Scene: The two of them, same seat, different years.
- Shots: The seat beside her, empty; then a cut and the nineteen-year-old version is in it, headphones on, not looking over. Present-day her talking to the window rather than to her. On the last line the girl turns her head for the first time. Do not resolve it into eye contact.
- Emotion: Tenderness, not pity.
- Lighting: One warm practical over the pair; everything else falls away.

### [final chorus — strings, countermelody, fullest]

```
Hit shuffle on the old playlist and I'm nineteen again,
Cheap perfume, borrowed denim, the whole night to spend.
Every song is a door I never got around to closing,
Every chorus is a room with all the lights still glowing.
Nothing bad has happened yet inside a three-minute track,
I know how it ends, and I don't need it back.
So let the window fog, let the driver make the bend,
Hit shuffle on the old playlist and I get to be her friend.
```

- Scene: The same aisle, now lit and moving with her rather than at her.
- Shots: The rooms return but she is not walking through them any more, she is looking in from the aisle and letting them go past. Both versions of her at the front window watching the road. On the last line, only one of them is there.
- Emotion: Gratitude. Genuinely light.
- Lighting: The warmest of the whole video; the bus interior finally reads gold instead of green.

### [post-chorus — stripped beat, chant]

```
Nineteen again, nineteen again,
Two stops out and the battery's at one.
Nineteen again, nineteen again,
Let it play, let it play till it's done.
```

- Scene: The battery running out.
- Shots: One percent on the screen (composited). Her cupping the phone with both hands as if that helps. The screen dying mid-track and the earbuds going silent, ambient bus sound flooding back in.
- Emotion: Comic on the surface, panicky underneath, then fine.
- Lighting: The phone glow going out; her face lit only by the window.

### [outro — Rhodes, crackle, doors]

```
Doors hiss open and the cold comes in,
Earbuds out, I put the old thing away.
But I added one more song before I stood up,
Something from tonight, for whoever's listening one day.
```

- Scene: Her stop, three stops late.
- Shots: Doors folding open, cold air visible. Her winding the earbud cable around two fingers. A last close-up: the playlist, one new track added at the bottom (composited). Her stepping down onto a wet pavement and walking out of frame, the bus pulling away behind her.
- Emotion: Settled. A small private joke with her future self.
- Lighting: Warm shop light on the pavement, the bus interior going dark as it leaves.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the bus window reflection swapping present-day her for the nineteen-year-old, in one unbroken pan — the single most shareable shot in the video.
- **The aisle of rooms:** every pair of bus seats a different lit memory. Cheap to shoot, endlessly remixable, made for a vertical cut.
- **The challenge:** post your own old playlist, screen recording only, with the caption line over it — "every chorus is a room with all the lights still glowing."
- **The caption line:** *"I know how it ends, and I still want it back."*
- **The quote:** *"I wouldn't go back. I only want five minutes on this bus beside her."*
- **The last frame:** one new song added to a three-year-old playlist. A whole story in a screenshot.
