# Hometown Radio — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
A woman drives back into the town she grew up in, and two hours out the local station pulls out of the static playing the same songs it played when she was seventeen. The Dairy Barn is a vape shop, the field where they parked is a subdivision, and her mother has kept her room exactly as it was. Her old best friend is still behind the counter at the diner and tells her he moved to Dallas and married a nurse, which is good news and is also worse. She drives out to her mother's porch, and by the time the sun goes down behind the silo she has worked out that the song was never theirs — it was hers, and he was only ever in the passenger seat.

**Mood:**
Warm, dry, unsentimental. Nostalgia with a spine. It hurts in the second chorus and heals in the third, and the healing is not about him.

**Message:**
"Hometown radio still plays our song — and I'm finally okay that we moved on."

**Genre:**
Country-pop with a modern rhythm section. Think: Kacey Musgraves' plainness x Lori McKenna's detail x the harmony stack of a Chris Stapleton chorus. Acoustic guitar, banjo-style plucks, pedal-steel, fiddle, family harmonies.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 96 BPM, 4/4
- Key: G major (open, singable, country-radio)
- Final chorus lifts a whole step to A major on the word "healing" — the only key change in the song, and it lands on the line that changes from *let it hurt* to *let it heal*.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, conversational and dry in the verses, wide and open in the choruses, near-spoken in the third verse and the bridge.
- Backing vocals: stacked family-style harmonies in the choruses, a full clapped stack on the post-chorus chant.

**Instrumentation:**
- Dry strummed acoustic guitar (main spine)
- Banjo-style plucked figure above it, leading the instrumental
- Warm upright-feel bass
- Pedal-steel-style slide guitar answering the phrasing
- Fiddle from the first pre-chorus
- Sustained organ pad under the bridge, low string pad under verse three
- Light brushed kit into a full band backbeat, hand claps from chorus two
- Ambient textures: car interior, tyre noise, an AM signal pulling out of static, a radio dial turning, a diner door bell, a coffee pot, a screen door spring

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: two hours out, the signal clears, the same voice on the dial
- Verse 1: the town's inventory — what changed and what didn't
- Pre-chorus: she swore she'd just drive through
- Chorus: the hook, big and hurting
- Verse 2: the diner, the friend, the news about him
- Verse 3: her mother's porch, the sweet tea, the sentence that unlocks the bridge
- Bridge: the silo, the empty lot, the turn — the song was hers
- Final chorus: a whole step up, *let it heal*
- Post-chorus: clapped, same three chords, different heartbeat
- Outro: the signal fading past the county line, and she leaves it on

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – guitar, banjo, car ambience, the dial clearing]

```
Two hours out and the static clears,
Same voice on the dial I haven't heard in years.
He says it's a warm one, folks, "drive safe tonight,"
And I roll the window down and let it back inside.
```

- Scene: A two-lane highway at the end of the afternoon. Fields, a grain elevator in the distance, nothing else.
- Shot: From the passenger footwell up to her hand on the wheel; then a macro of a car radio display (composited) with the numbers settling as the static resolves.
- Cut to: Her face in the rear-view, listening, then the window going down and the air taking her hair.
- Emotion: Recognition. She wasn't expecting the voice.
- Lighting: Late gold, sun low and directly through the driver's window.

### [verse 1]

```
Water tower's still got your letters and mine,
Faded to a whisper, but I know every line.
The Dairy Barn's a vape shop, the church is the same,
And the field where we parked has a subdivision name.
```

- Scene: The town's inventory, shot from the car as she drives in.
- Shots: A water tower with initials painted on it decades ago, weathered almost to nothing (never legible). A closed dairy stand with a new bright shopfront in it. A white clapboard church, untouched. A field's worth of identical new houses with a sign at the entrance we never read.
- Emotion: Cataloguing, not grieving. She's counting.
- Lighting: Low sun, long shadows across everything.

```
Mama kept my room like I'd be back by June,
Posters on the wall, glow stars, crooked moon.
Passed your old truck at the light on Main,
Different plates, but my chest didn't get the memo, same old lane.
```

- Scene: A childhood bedroom, then back in the car.
- Shots: A bedroom door opening on a room that stopped in a particular year: posters, a shelf of trophies, glow-in-the-dark stars on the ceiling with one crooked. Then a truck the same make and colour as his, two cars ahead at a red light on Main Street.
- Her reaction: A physical jolt she is annoyed by, then the plates being different, then the light going green.
- Lighting: Bedroom dim and warm, curtains closed; the street hard and bright.

### [pre-chorus]

```
I swore to myself I'd just drive through,
Gas and a coffee and I'm past you.
But the dial lands right on that opening chord,
And I'm seventeen with my hand out the door.
```

- Scene: A gas station forecourt at the edge of town.
- Shot: Her filling the tank, phone in her back pocket, looking at the road out. Then the song starting on the car speakers as she gets back in, and her hand going still on the key.
- Cut to: Two seconds of her at seventeen, hand out of a car window in a rushing summer.
- Emotion: Ambush.
- Lighting: Forecourt fluorescent against dusk; the memory over-bright and grainy.

### [chorus – big, hurting, full band]

```
Hometown radio still plays our song,
Like nobody told it that we moved on.
Every streetlight's humming the words we knew,
Every mile of this town is a mile of you.
Everything changed and nothing did,
I'm a grown woman feeling like a kid.
Turn it up, let it hurt, sing along,
Hometown radio still plays our song.
```

- Scene: Driving the town at dusk with the song on.
- Shots: Streetlights coming on one after another down a long road. The high-school parking lot, empty. The bleachers. A railway crossing. Her singing along without meaning to, and her face doing something she doesn't intend.
- Emotion: The hurt she drove three hundred miles to avoid.
- Lighting: Blue hour, sodium streetlights, headlights.

### [verse 2]

```
Slid in the booth at the diner on Fifth,
Cracked red vinyl, sugar packets, the whole gift.
My old best friend behind the counter, she screams,
Says "girl, you look expensive, but you still look sixteen."
```

- Scene: The diner. The best scene in the video.
- Shots: A booth in cracked red vinyl, a caddy of sugar packets, a laminated menu (never legible). Her old best friend seeing her from behind the counter and shrieking, coming out around it, the hug knocking a napkin dispenser over.
- Emotion: Uncomplicated joy, the only pure one in the film.
- Lighting: Warm diner interior, night outside the window.

```
She says he moved to Dallas, married a nurse,
Says it like it's good news, and it is, and it's worse.
Then that song comes on the speaker and she catches my eye,
Tops my coffee off and says, "honey, some things don't die."
```

- Scene: The same booth, the friend sitting down opposite with the coffee pot still in her hand.
- Shots: Her taking the news well, on the outside. Her hands on the mug. The corner speaker. The friend catching her eye across the table and not saying the obvious thing.
- Emotion: The news is genuinely good and it still lands like a slap.
- Lighting: Unchanged — the point is that nothing about the room reacts.

### [pre-chorus]

```
I told her I'd only stop on through,
Hug and a refill and I'm past you.
But she turns the speaker up and she's out on the floor,
Two girls in a diner, seventeen, hands out the door.
```

- Scene: The friend turning the corner speaker up and coming out from behind the counter.
- Shot: The two of them dancing badly between the tables while two other customers watch. The cook in the hatch, unimpressed.
- Cut to: Two seconds of the same two girls at seventeen doing the same thing in the same place.
- Emotion: The town giving her something back.
- Lighting: Diner warm, the memory over-bright.

### [chorus]

```
Hometown radio still plays our song,
Like nobody told it that we moved on.
Every streetlight's humming the words we knew,
Every mile of this town is a mile of you.
Everything changed and nothing did,
I'm a grown woman feeling like a kid.
Turn it up, let it hurt, sing along,
Hometown radio still plays our song.
```

- Scene: The chorus montage again, now with the friend in the car.
- Shots: Two of them driving, windows down. The water tower again from below. The subdivision at night, all the windows lit.
- Emotion: Bigger and less lonely, and it still hurts.
- Lighting: Full night, headlights, streetlights.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: No lyrics. The band feature. The town at night.
- Shots: The diner sign going off. A dog crossing an empty Main Street. The high-school field with the lights on for nobody. A porch swing moving in the wind. A grain elevator against the last blue. The car pulling out of town and turning the other way — toward her mother's.
- Lighting: Sodium, headlights, the last blue in the west.

### [verse 3 – quiet, near-spoken, guitar and pad]

```
Drove out to Mama's the long way, took it slow,
Screen door, sweet tea, and the dog I used to know.
She didn't ask about him once, she asked about my week,
Asked about my sister, asked if I still eat.
She said, "you can love a town and still drive on,"
And she hummed the harmony to that same old song.
```

- Scene: A farmhouse porch at the very end of the light. The quietest scene in the film.
- Shots: A screen door with a spring. Two glasses of sweet tea sweating on a rail. An old dog getting up slowly. Her mother's hands, her mother's face, both fully seen — she is the one person in the video the camera looks at directly. The radio audible through the kitchen window.
- Her reaction: She realises her mother has not mentioned him once, and that this is on purpose.
- Emotion: Being known. The line that unlocks the bridge is delivered as an aside, not a lesson.
- Lighting: The last of the day, a kitchen light behind them through the screen.

### [bridge – guitar and organ, drums out]

```
Sun going down behind the silo, gold,
The whole sky the color of a story I've told.
I'm parked in the lot with the engine still on,
Waiting on the second verse to come along.
Maybe it never was ours, maybe it was mine,
You were only ever riding on the passenger side.
This town didn't miss you, it kept the porch light on for me,
Water tower, diner, and the kid it let me be.
```

- Scene: An empty lot at the edge of town, the car facing a silo, the sun going down behind it.
- Shots: The silo black against gold. Her hands off the wheel. The empty passenger seat, held. The radio display (composited). Then quick, warm returns: the water tower, the diner window, the porch light, and her own face in the mirror.
- Emotion: The turn, and it is a relief rather than a triumph.
- Lighting: The most beautiful frame in the video, and it is a parking lot.

### [final chorus – whole step up, fullest, "let it heal"]

```
Hometown radio still plays our song,
And I'm finally okay that we moved on.
Every streetlight's humming the words I knew,
Every mile of this town is a mile I grew.
Everything changed and nothing did,
I'm a grown woman, still that kid.
Turn it up, let it heal, sing along,
Hometown radio still plays our song.
```

- Scene: The same roads as chorus one, driven differently.
- Shots: Every location from chorus one, re-shot with her singing on purpose instead of by accident. The high-school lot. The bleachers. The crossing. The streetlights. Her hand out of the window at the same angle as the seventeen-year-old's.
- Emotion: Release. She is not over the town; she is done being hurt by it.
- Lighting: Night, but brighter — more streetlights lit, headlights on full.

### [post-chorus – clapped chant]

```
Same three chords, same four streets,
Same girl behind the wheel, different heartbeat.
Same three chords, same four streets,
Ooh, I'm home, I'm home, I'm home.
```

- Scene: Four streets, four cuts, on the claps.
- Shots: Main Street, the road past the school, the road to her mother's, the highway out. Then her face, driving, saying the last line.
- Emotion: Ownership.
- Lighting: Headlights and streetlights, high contrast.

### [outro – guitar and radio, the signal fading]

```
Signal's fading out past the county sign,
DJ says "goodnight, folks," says "take your time."
I keep the window down and I sing along,
Hometown radio, still playing our song.
Still playing our song.
```

- Scene: The county line at night, the town behind her.
- Shots: A county sign passing (never legible). The station degrading back into static on the radio display. Her singing along to a song she can barely hear any more. Final shot: the tail lights going away down a straight dark road, the window still down. Hold.
- Emotion: Peace, and no hurry.
- Lighting: Tail lights and one road sign reflector; everything else dark.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the radio dial pulling out of static with *"Hometown radio still plays our song"* on screen.
- **The challenge:** post the song your hometown station will not stop playing, or the building that used to be something else.
- **The caption line:** *"Everything changed and nothing did."*
- **The diner dance:** two women dancing badly between the tables — the clip that travels furthest.
- **The turn:** *"Maybe it never was ours, maybe it was mine, you were only ever riding on the passenger side."*
- **The quote:** *"This town didn't miss you, it kept the porch light on for me."*
