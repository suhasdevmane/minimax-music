# The Hundredth Song — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
The catalogue's closer. A song about all the songs — the leaving ones and the staying ones, the parties, the kitchens, the airports, the bad nights someone played on repeat — and the one voice that carried all ninety-nine of them. Not a farewell. A woman counting what a hundred songs actually did, and finding that the answer is not chart positions but a stranger in a hallway saying one of them got her through.

**Mood:**
Anthemic, grateful, unsentimental. Big without being triumphant. The last night of a tour, or the last page of a long book you don't want to finish. Starts as one person alone in a rehearsal room and ends as a stadium singing back a line that was written for nobody.

**Message:**
"A hundred songs is not a career, it's a promise kept. And the last one is a funny way of saying the next one."

**Genre:**
Anthemic cinematic pop, female and male duet. Think: Florence + the Machine x Hozier, with the closing-credits scale of a Coldplay finale and the plain-spoken male section of a Nick Cave spoken word. Piano and voice at the top, choir and timpani at the bottom.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 98 BPM, 4/4
- Key: F♯ minor — deliberately the same home key as song 1
- The final chorus modulates up into A major, the largest lift in the catalogue, choir and timpani arriving with it

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal (Singer A): young adult female, close and nearly unaccompanied in the intro, belted and cracking at the top of the choruses, near-whispered in the bridge
- Singer B: male, warm grounded baritone, storyteller rather than singer; verse two alone, unison on pre-chorus two, spoken word before the final chorus with no singing at all
- Both voices in octaves on the final chorus; the post-chorus trades phrases line by line; the outro is her alone with his voice on the very last word

**Instrumentation:**
- Solo piano — the spine, and the only instrument in the first eight bars and the last eight; felt-piano double under the verses
- Low sustained cello under verse one, taking the first solo in the break; orchestral strings from pre-chorus one onward
- Warm electric bass and ringing electric guitar from chorus one; upright bass under the male verse
- Brushed drums, then live kick and snare, then no drums at all in the bridge and the spoken word, then half-time in the post-chorus
- Full choir from chorus two through the final one; timpani and suspended cymbal only in the break and the last chorus
- Ambient textures: room tone, a chair moving, a piano stool creaking, a distant crowd recorded from the back of a hall, tape hiss under the spoken word

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: one person, one room, a borrowed microphone, a door that won't close
- Verse 1 (her): the ninety-nine, counted as people rather than titles
- Pre-chorus: the last note isn't the ending, it's the room going quiet
- Chorus (both, her on top): the hook, huge and unhurried, gratitude and not victory
- Verse 2 (him): the man who came in on one verse and never left the floor
- Instrumental: cello, then strings and piano, then full band and timpani, then one held note
- Bridge (her, whispered): the woman in the hallway, the smallest moment on the record
- Spoken word (him): the arithmetic of ninety-nine nights, said plainly over piano and sub
- Final chorus into post-chorus and outro: A major, choir, both voices, then everything gone but the piano and an empty stage

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – solo piano, room tone, her alone]

```
It started in a room with a borrowed microphone,
A cable on the floor and a door that wouldn't close.
I had no plan, I had a hundred things to say,
And I've said 99 of them, so here goes.
```

- Scene: A bare rehearsal room before anyone else arrives. One upright piano, one mic stand, a folding chair, a radiator.
- Shot: Extreme close-up, macro, of a hand pushing an XLR plug into a microphone until it clicks; then the cable running loose across bare floorboards.
- Cut to: The door of the room, wedged half open, refusing to close, the corridor beyond it dark.
- Emotion / Lighting: She sits, exhales once, puts her mouth close to the mic before she sings a note. One overhead work light, cold and unflattering. Nothing is staged.

### [verse 1 – her, felt piano and cello]

```
There's a girl in the first one learning how to leave,
And a girl in the fortieth still learning how to stay.
There were kitchens and taxis and a bus at six,
And every one of them was somebody's whole day.
I sang about a summer like it was a person,
I sang about a doorway I was frightened to walk out.
I gave away my worst night so yours would feel smaller,
And that's how you put a heavy thing down.
```

- Scene: The songs as rooms. She walks a long backstage corridor and each doorway she passes contains a different world from the catalogue, lit and alive for two seconds: a rain-lit window, a kitchen with string lights and two people in socks, a taxi's back seat, a night bus, a bus stop in early light.
- Shot: Tracking behind her the length of the corridor; then her hand trailing along the wall, touching each door frame as she passes it, the warm light from each room crossing her fingers.
- Cut to: One dark doorway with no world in it at all, only black, and her stopping to look into it; then a hard cut back to the rehearsal room and her face at the microphone, eyes closed.
- Emotion / Lighting: Not nostalgia — inventory. She is counting people, not tracks, and the last two lines land as relief rather than sorrow. Corridor cold and blue, each doorway warm and saturated, the only colour in frame.

### [pre-chorus – her, his hum under the last line, strings enter]

```
The last note of a song isn't an ending,
It's the room going quiet enough to hear.
If you came in late, if you only know the one,
Pull a chair to the front, we're nearly there.
```

- Scene: The rehearsal room begins to fill. A cellist walks in and sits down behind her without a word.
- Shot: A rack of stage lights coming up one at a time behind her in a slow rise, her still unlit, a silhouette against them.
- Cut to: A single empty folding chair carried into the front row of an otherwise dark hall and set down.
- Emotion / Lighting: An invitation — she is talking to whoever just arrived. The first warm light of the video, creeping in from behind.

### [chorus – both, her on top, full band]

```
This is the hundredth song, and I'm still singing,
Same two hands, same crack in the same high note.
Ninety-nine goodbyes, a hundred ways to begin,
And a voice you carried further than I could alone.
Sing the part you know, I'll hold the rest,
This is the hundredth song, and I'm still singing.
```

- Scene: Hard cut from the rehearsal room to a full stage mid-show, the band around her.
- Shot: Her at the front of the stage with her arms open; then her two hands on the microphone in exactly the grip of the first shot of the video.
- Cut to: The crack in the high note played straight, her face held through it with no cutaway; a wide from the back of the hall over a dark sea of raised arms; her pulling the mic off her own mouth and pointing it at the room, and the room singing.
- Emotion / Lighting: Gratitude at full volume, not a victory lap. Gold and deep amber, hard backlight, haze in the beams. He sings beneath the hook from the side of the stage, half in shadow.

### [verse 2 – him, upright bass and soft kit, strings out]

```
I came in on a verse, I was only meant to stay,
Then a hundred rooms went by and I never left the floor.
I've watched a stadium learn a private thing by heart
And hand it back to her a little louder than before.
Some nights the sound was awful and the crowd was nine,
And she sang it like the room was gold, and made it true.
That's the whole trick, if anybody asks,
You sing the small ones like they matter, and they do.
```

- Scene: The male lead alone on the stage in the afternoon, before doors, sitting on a road case with a bass across his knee and a coil of cable over one shoulder, talking more than singing.
- Shot: His boots on the stage floor, gaffer tape marks layered a hundred deep in a hundred colours; then his POV from the wing, her small in the middle of a huge lit room with the crowd's mouths moving in time.
- Cut to: A tiny back room above a pub. Nine chairs, four of them empty, a beer sign on the wall, and her singing to them at total commitment; one man at the back who has stopped drinking to listen.
- Emotion / Lighting: Devotion without romance — he is the witness of this record, and the thesis in the last two lines is delivered flat, like a fact. Grey afternoon daylight with dust in it for him; the ugliest and warmest frame in the video for the nine.

### [pre-chorus – both in unison, choir hum enters]

```
The last note of a song isn't an ending,
It's the sound of a room breathing in the dark.
If you came in late, if you only know the one,
That's enough, that was always enough to start.
```

- Scene: A full arena between songs, in the moment of held silence.
- Shot: The two of them side by side at one microphone, singing the same notes.
- Cut to: The crowd in total silhouette with no light on any face, absolutely still, then a slow inhale of noise; the folding chair from the first pre-chorus now with somebody finally sitting in it.
- Emotion / Lighting: Two voices agreeing on something. Almost dark, one white spot on the pair of them.

### [chorus – both, choir, octave counter-line]

```
This is the hundredth song, and I'm still singing,
Same two hands, same crack in the same high note.
Ninety-nine goodbyes, a hundred ways to begin,
And a voice you carried further than I could alone.
Sing the part you know, I'll hold the rest,
This is the hundredth song, and I'm still singing.
```

- Scene: The chorus opens out into the catalogue — the same hook heard in every room a song ends up in.
- Shot: The stage wider than before, the choir walking on behind the band.
- Cut to: A kitchen with string lights, two people slow-dancing in socks and singing the hook at each other. A festival field at dusk from above, a hundred thousand arms up. A taxi at night with the window down and the driver singing it badly. A night bus, one pair of headphones, lips moving against the glass. An airport gate at four in the morning, one person asleep across three seats with an earbud in.
- Emotion / Lighting: The song leaving her hands and belonging to other people. Each world in its own grade, cut hard against the gold of the stage.

### [instrumental – cello, then strings and piano, then full band and timpani]

- Scene: No lyrics. The years, in objects and rooms.
- Shot: A cellist alone in one hard pool of light on the black stage. A backstage wall covered in a hundred taped-up set lists. A time-lapse of a crew striking and rebuilding the same stage, night into day into night. Her from behind, walking out into the middle of an empty stage in flat daylight with nobody in the room.
- Cut to: The timpani hit and the suspended cymbal, then everything falling away to one held string note over one held frame of the mic stand alone.
- Emotion / Lighting: Scale, then sudden smallness — the break has to earn the whisper that follows it. Single-source and hard, then house lights up and unglamorous, then a slow fade to one lamp.

### [bridge – her almost whispering, solo piano]

```
I used to think the point of this was being heard,
Then a woman in a hallway said, "that one got me through."
I've never written anything as useful as that sentence,
So I keep the microphone and I keep making room.
```

- Scene: A concrete corridor outside a dressing room, ten minutes after a show.
- Shot: A woman in a dark wool coat, filmed entirely from behind, face never in frame, saying something short to her; the audio drops to nothing but room tone.
- Cut to: Her face receiving it, with no performance in it at all; then the woman gone down the corridor and her still standing in the same spot after.
- Emotion / Lighting: The smallest and most important moment on the record. One buzzing strip light overhead, hard shadows, concrete.

### [spoken – him, no singing, piano and sub only]

```
99 of these. Think about that.
99 nights somebody played one on the wrong side of a hard week.
99 kitchens, airports, back seats, bus stops.
She didn't write a hundred songs. She wrote one song a hundred times,
And everyone who heard it was certain it was theirs.
That is not a career. That is a promise kept.
So here it is, the last one, which is a funny way of saying the next one.
```

- Scene: Him alone, centre stage, house lights half up, speaking to a room that has gone completely quiet.
- Shot: One very slow push-in from the back of the hall, no cuts, all the way to close-up.
- Cut to: One-beat cutaways on the count — a bedroom ceiling at three in the morning with a phone face-down, a kitchen tap running, a departure board, a seatbelt across a chest, a bus stop in rain — then a lateral track along five faces in the front row, each lit only by stage spill and each one clearly somewhere else.
- Emotion / Lighting: Plain, unraised, the most-quoted forty seconds of the video. Half house light, one low front wash, tape-hiss texture in the grade.

### [final chorus – both in octaves, modulation to A major, choir and timpani]

```
This is the hundredth song, and I'm still singing,
Same two hands, same crack in the same high note.
A hundred ways of leaving, a hundred coming home,
And a voice that was never only mine to hold.
Take the part you love, leave the rest behind,
This is the hundredth song, and I'm still singing.
```

- Scene: Everything at once. The key change is the visual event of the video.
- Shot: Every light in the rig hitting on the downbeat of the modulation, locked-off wide; then the two of them shoulder to shoulder at the front of the stage, confetti crossing the beams.
- Cut to: The choir at full voice behind the band; her stopping singing entirely and letting the room carry a line with her hands off the microphone; the crowd front to back, the widest frame in the catalogue.
- Emotion / Lighting: The biggest sound on the record, and it is about other people. A major is warm white and gold; the grade lifts a full stop on the modulation and holds there.

### [post-chorus – trading phrases, half-time]

```
Still singing, still here, still yours,
Still finding new ways to say the same true thing.
Still singing, still here, still ours,
A hundred is a doorway, not a wall.
```

- Scene: Half-time. The two of them alternate lines and the crowd answers.
- Shot: Her line, his line, her line, matched close-ups cut on the trade, then pulling back to a two-shot with both mouths moving.
- Cut to: A slow push toward the wings on the last line — an actual doorway at the side of the stage with white light coming through it, nobody in it yet.
- Emotion / Lighting: Settled, open-ended, deliberately not a goodbye. Warm, dropping a step with each line as the rig cools down.

### [outro – her alone, solo piano, his voice on the last word]

```
An empty stage, one light, a microphone still warm,
And a cable on the floor where all of it began.
Whoever picks it up next, I hope the room is loud,
This is the hundredth song, and I'm still singing.
```

- Scene: After. The hall empty, the crew gone, the house lights off, one working light on a stand at centre stage.
- Shot: The same loose cable as the first shot of the video, running across the stage floor — a different room, the same cable.
- Cut to: A hand laying the microphone back into its clip and letting go, then leaving frame; then the empty stage, held long, nobody in it.
- Emotion / Lighting: Not sadness. A room left ready for somebody else. The same cold work light as the intro, closing the circle.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** the modulation into the final chorus, the whole rig hitting at once, with *"this is the hundredth song, and I'm still singing"* on screen — the single most postable second in the catalogue.
- **The caption line:** *"A hundred is a doorway, not a wall."*
- **The quote:** *"She didn't write a hundred songs. She wrote one song a hundred times."*
- **The hundred challenge:** people post the one song of the hundred that got them through, over the spoken-word section, cutting on the count of ninety-nine.
- **The last frame:** the empty stage with one microphone, held silent — an end-card that needs no text and works as the final post of the whole campaign.
