Global Emotional Progression: The track opens with sea ambience, a ceiling fan and a single muted nylon guitar figure played very close, no drums, the lead voice low and slow as if she has only just woken up. The first verse brings a soft rounded kick, a light shaker and a warm bass under a conversational vocal that inventories a hotel room at the end of a week, with a steel-drum synth answering the ends of lines. The pre-chorus lifts gently with a rising bass and a brushed snare, holding on the last line before the hook. The chorus opens into the full arrangement, wide steel-drum synths, a bright plucked guitar, a soft four-on-the-floor kick and layered harmonies, warm and unhurried rather than euphoric, and it never gets louder than a good mood. A two-line post-chorus follows, half spoken, almost lazy. The second verse pulls back to bass and guitar as the outside world arrives in the lyric, an inbox and a cold city, and a soft string pad enters under the last two lines when the answer comes back from the front desk. The second pre-chorus is fuller and the second chorus adds a countermelody on the steel drums. The instrumental break is a nylon guitar solo over the groove with the steel drums washing behind it, then a long dubby section with tape delay on a single guitar note and the sea coming back up in the mix. The bridge drops to guitar, one sustained pad and a bare vocal, the most direct moment of the song, and rebuilds under the last line. The final chorus is the fullest of the track with an added string pad and doubled harmonies but still restrained, and the last post-chorus fades into the sea. The outro is nylon guitar, a soft shaker and the lead voice alone, ending on a warm unresolved chord over water and a distant plane.
