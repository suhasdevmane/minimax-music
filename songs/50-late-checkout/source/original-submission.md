# Late Checkout — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
The last morning of a holiday. Eleven o'clock, a room neither of them has started packing, a shell on the nightstand and a towel on the balcony rail that has not dried all week. She phones the front desk and asks for a late checkout, and the whole song is that small request: one more hour before the version of them that only exists on this balcony has to get on a plane.

**Mood:**
Warm, slow, sunlit, with an ache under it. Never euphoric — the record never gets louder than a good mood. Romantic and completely PG.

**Message:**
"I'm not asking for forever. I'm asking for an hour."

**Genre:**
Chill tropical pop. Think: Khalid x Rex Orange County x early Jack Johnson — steel-drum synths, muted nylon guitar recorded very close, a soft rounded kick.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 100 BPM, 4/4
- Key: G major (open, guitar-friendly, sits easily in her low conversational range)
- No modulation; the final chorus fills out with a string pad rather than lifting.

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: young adult female, low and just-woken in the verses, open and warm in the choruses, almost spoken in the post-chorus.
- Backing vocals: wordless harmonies under the post-choruses; doubled harmonies on the final hook.

**Instrumentation:**
- Muted nylon-string guitar, very close, fingers audible (opens the song, carries the verses, takes the solo, closes the outro)
- Soft steel-drum synths (answering the verse lines, wide across the choruses)
- Warm rounded bass, soft four-on-the-floor kick, rim click, shaker, brushed snare
- Bright plucked guitar, light electric piano, soft string pad
- Textures: sea wash, a ceiling fan, gulls, hotel-pool noise, a telephone handset, tape delay, a key card, a door latch, a plane crossing high

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: shutter light on the floor, no drums, the sea
- Verse 1: the room at the end of a week, catalogued
- Pre-chorus: the phone by the bed and the hand on her back
- Chorus: the ask, warm and unhurried
- Verse 2: real life arriving, and the answer from the front desk
- Bridge: the honest version of the request
- Final chorus and outro: the hour they got, then a key card on a counter

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro — nylon guitar, sea, ceiling fan, no drums]

```
Eleven in the morning and the light comes through the slats,
Falls across the floor and stops right where your bag is.
And the sea is doing what it always does.
```

- Scene: A small hotel room with wooden shutters, high above or beside a sea.
- Shot: Hard bars of light through shutter slats lying across a tiled floor, dust moving in them, static.
- Cut to: The bars of light reaching the edge of a packed holdall by the door, and stopping there.
- Emotion: Stillness that both of them are protecting.
- Lighting: White eleven o'clock sun outside, cool shadow inside, very high contrast.

### [verse 1 — soft kick, shaker, steel drums answering]

```
The fan is still turning and the room is still warm,
There's a shell on the nightstand from the second day.
Your shirt is on the chair with the salt still in it,
And my case has been open since we got here anyway.
```

- Scene: The inventory of a week.
- Shot: A ceiling fan turning slowly, shot from directly below, the blades cutting the light.
- Cut to: A shell on a nightstand beside a phone; a shirt over the back of a chair with a tidemark of salt on the collar; an open suitcase with nothing folded in it.
- Emotion: Fond and slow. Every object is a day of the week.
- Lighting: Warm interior, one hard shutter stripe across each object.

```
The towel on the rail has not dried out all week,
And the sea keeps on saying the same thing to me.
```

- Scene: The balcony.
- Shot: A towel on a balcony rail, damp, moving slightly in the wind, the sea behind it out of focus.
- Cut to: Mahima at the balcony door with a coffee, looking out, not going out.
- Emotion: The first hint of the clock.
- Lighting: Blown-out sea behind her, her face in soft shadow.

### [pre-chorus 1 — rising bass, brushed snare, a held last line]

```
There's a number by the bed for the front desk,
And your hand on my back saying, go on, ask.
It's a small thing to want and a small thing to say,
So I picked it up and asked her if we could stay.
```

- Scene: The phone call.
- Shot: An old hotel telephone on the nightstand, macro, her hand next to it and not on it.
- Cut to: Kai's hand flat on her back, one small push, seen from behind her.
- Emotion: The comedy of how hard a tiny request is.
- Lighting: Warm lamp side, cool window side.

### [chorus 1 — full arrangement, wide steel drums]

```
Give me a late checkout, one more hour of you,
Sun on the sheets and the sea coming through.
I will pay for the hour, I will pay for the day,
Anything to keep the eleven o'clock away.
Give me a late checkout, let the coffee go cold,
Leave the shutters wide and the suitcase in the hall.
There's a plane with our names on it, I know, I know,
Give me a late checkout before we have to go.
```

- Scene: The room, alive.
- Shot: The shutters pushed fully open, the room flooding with white light and sea noise, wide.
- Cut to: The two of them on the balcony with the coffee going cold on the rail, talking, laughing, no urgency at all.
- Emotion: Warm, easy, protected. This is what they are trying to keep.
- Lighting: The brightest the room gets in the video, entirely natural.

### [post-chorus 1 — half spoken]

```
One more hour, one more hour,
Everything else can wait downstairs.
```

- Scene: Two lazy cuts.
- Shot: A coffee cup with a full skin on it, macro; a bag by the door, untouched.
- Lighting: Flat bright, no drama.

### [verse 2 — bass and guitar, then a string pad]

```
The woman at the desk said that she'd have to go and check,
And we sat down on the end of the bed like it was a test.
I know there's an inbox with a hundred things unread,
And a coat in a closet in a city where it's wet.
```

- Scene: Waiting on hold, and the world outside the room.
- Shot: The two of them sat on the end of the bed, side by side, phone between them on speaker, looking at the floor.
- Cut to: Two cold flash inserts, two seconds each: a laptop inbox in a grey office (composited), and a heavy coat hanging on the back of a door in a rainy flat.
- Emotion: The joke and the dread in the same shot.
- Lighting: The room stays warm; the inserts are grey, flat and blue.

```
I know the girl who gets off that plane tomorrow morning
Is careful and quiet and busy again by ten.
Then the phone clicked back on and she said we've got till two,
And I have never loved a stranger like I loved her then.
```

- Scene: The answer.
- Shot: Her face while she waits, close-up, the sea sound the only thing in the mix.
- Cut to: Both of them reacting to the answer at once, undignified, delighted, a small quiet celebration on the end of a bed.
- Emotion: Absurdly large joy over an absurdly small thing. Play it completely straight.
- Lighting: Warm again, the string pad's arrival matched by the sun moving onto the bed.

### [pre-chorus 2 — fuller]

```
There's a number by the bed for the front desk,
And your hand on my back saying, ask her again.
It's a small thing to want and a small thing to say,
So I picked it up and asked her if we could stay.
```

- Scene: The comic repeat.
- Shot: Her hand on the telephone again, his hand over his eyes, both laughing.
- Emotion: They both know she is going to try for more.
- Lighting: Sun higher, harder, the shutter stripes narrower.

### [chorus 2 — steel-drum countermelody]

```
(the chorus again, fuller)
```

- Scene: The hour they bought, spent as slowly as possible.
- Shot: A slow lateral track along the balcony: two chairs, two cups, the sea, both of them, nothing happening.
- Emotion: Contentment with a countdown under it.
- Lighting: Full midday, blown-out sea, deep shade under the balcony roof.

### [instrumental — nylon guitar solo, then dubby tape delay]

- Scene: The room emptying, slowly.
- Shots: A shirt folded and put in the case. The shell picked up, considered, and put into a side pocket. The towel taken off the rail, still damp. The fan slowing after the switch goes off. A wide of the room, made neutral again, with the light bars now high on the wall instead of on the floor.
- Lighting: The light moving up the wall is the clock of this section.

### [bridge — guitar, one pad, bare vocal]

```
I'm not asking for forever, I'm just asking for an hour,
But if the two of us out here on this balcony
Can make it to the airport, we can make it through the rest.
So give me sixty minutes and a door we haven't shut,
And I'll go quietly, I promise, when it's up.
```

- Scene: The most direct thing she says in the song.
- Shot: The two of them on the balcony floor with their backs against the wall, shoulders touching, looking out through the railings.
- Cut to: Her face in profile, saying it to the sea rather than to him.
- Emotion: Not desperate. Clear-eyed. She knows exactly what she is asking for.
- Lighting: Shade, with the blown-out sea beyond; her face is the darkest and softest it gets.

### [final chorus — string pad, doubled harmonies]

```
(the chorus, with "She gave us until two and she did not have to")
```

- Scene: The last hour, used.
- Shot: The two of them going back down to the water in their clothes for twenty minutes because they can, running the last few steps.
- Cut to: Wet feet on hot stone coming back up the hotel stairs, macro, leaving prints that dry behind them.
- Emotion: The best hour of the week and they know it.
- Lighting: High hard sun, hot stone, real colour.

### [post-chorus 2 — fading into the sea]

```
(the post-chorus again, quieter)
```

- Scene: The room, done.
- Shot: Two cases by the door and the shutters closed, the light bars gone, the room in shade for the first time.
- Lighting: The only shaded interior in the video.

### [outro — nylon guitar and voice]

```
Key card on the counter and the door clicks shut,
The sea is still going and it doesn't know we left.
Somewhere over water I'll be holding on to your hand,
Still asking anybody for a late checkout.
```

- Scene: Leaving.
- Shot: A key card set down on a reception counter, macro, a hand lifting away from it.
- Cut to: The room door swinging shut on an empty made bed, from inside, going dark.
- Cut to: An aeroplane window with the sea underneath it, her hand and his on the armrest between the seats.
- Final shot: The empty balcony from outside, the towel gone, the rail bare, the sea still going, locked-off and held through the fade.
- Emotion: Not sad. Grateful, and already planning the next one.
- Lighting: Clean, natural, no grade tricks.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** her on the phone by the bed with his hand on her back, on *"go on, ask."*
- **The challenge:** the late-checkout ask — film the moment the front desk says yes. Everyone has made that phone call.
- **The heart moment:** two people celebrating on the end of a bed because a stranger gave them three extra hours.
- **The caption line:** *"I'm not asking for forever, I'm just asking for an hour."*
- **The last frame:** an empty balcony, a bare rail, and a sea that carries on.
