# Don't Tell the Neighbours — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, before
cleanup for the lyric body. Kept as source material.

**Theme:**
A two-person dance party in a thin-walled south London flat, held at whisper volume because the walls are made of paper and Number Twenty-Four goes to bed at eleven. Chain on the door, curtains pulled, sofa pushed back with a foot, both of them shushing each other and failing. The neighbour is the comedy and then, quietly, the point.

**Mood:**
Flirty, giggling, conspiratorial. Late-night and warm rather than sultry. Everything mixed and shot as if it were being heard through a wall.

**Message:**
"I spent my whole life keeping the volume down. Not any more."

**Genre:**
Reggaeton-pop with a soft dembow and plucked synths, sung in a British pop voice. Think: Rosalía's restraint x Dua Lipa's phrasing x a Central Cee-adjacent London flatness in the spoken lines.

**Tempo & Key:**
- 100 BPM, 4/4
- Key: A minor, so it stays sly rather than sweet
- The lift is the hook inverting in the final chorus — "turn it down" becomes "turn it up"

**Vocal setup:**
- Lead vocal: female, right on the microphone, whispered in the intro and post-chorus, playful and rhythmic in the verses
- Backing vocals: clipped whispered answers through the choruses, a stacked group vocal on the second chorus's last two lines and the whole final chorus
- The bridge is voice, one pad and a nylon guitar

**Instrumentation:**
- Plucked synth figure (the signature, first bar to last, with a solo in the break)
- Soft dembow: muted kick, rim, shaker; short round sub-bass
- Low brass-like synth stab punctuating the chorus lines
- Soft pad and nylon guitar carrying the bridge with no drums at all
- Ambience: lamp buzz, corridor tone, a distant lift, a door chain, three knuckle knocks in tempo, an alarm through a wall

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: half eleven, curtains, one lamp, a phone put face down
- Verse 1: the chain, the sofa pushed back, the floorboards giving them away
- Pre-chorus: keeping it under the beat and failing
- Chorus: the hook, the wall, Number Twenty-Four
- Verse 2: three knocks, a note under the door, the stairs the next morning
- Instrumental: the dance at full size, played at half volume
- Bridge: a lifetime of being quiet for other people
- Final chorus: the hook inverted, turn it up
- Post-chorus: the whispered chant
- Outro: four in the morning, an alarm through the wall, not sorry

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – plucked synth, room tone, no drums]

```
Half eleven and the block has gone quiet,
One lamp on and the curtains pulled tight.
Nothing that happens in this flat leaves this flat,
So put your phone face down and come here for that.
```

- Scene: A low-rise south London block from the outside at half eleven, one window lit.
- Shot: Static wide of the block from the walkway opposite, every window dark except one on the second floor.
- Cut to: Inside — a hand pulling curtains across, a single lamp with a warm shade, a phone being turned face down on a table.
- Emotion: Conspiracy. Both of them are already grinning before anything happens.
- Lighting: Sodium orange outside, one warm practical inside, everything else in shadow.

### [verse 1 – the chain and the sofa]

```
Chain across the door and my shoes in the hall,
Curtains doing shapes on the back of the wall.
You pushed the sofa with your foot and you said, "right,"
And the floorboards in this flat haven't settled since eight.
Up on my toes trying to keep the noise low,
And you're doing that thing with your shoulders, and I go.
```

- Scene: Clearing a dance floor out of a living room the size of a car.
- Shot: A door chain sliding across, close; two pairs of shoes kicked off in a narrow hall; curtain shapes moving on a wall in lamp light.
- Cut to: A sofa shoved back with one foot, catching on a rug; her up on her toes in socks, exaggeratedly careful; him doing a small shoulder move that immediately ends her restraint.
- Her reaction / Emotion: Trying to be quiet, failing, laughing into her own hand.
- Lighting: One warm lamp, hard shadows, everything just under-exposed on purpose.

### [pre-chorus – keeping it under the beat]

```
Keep it low, keep it slow, keep it under the beat,
Whisper it, don't shout it, put your hand on the heat.
If the ceiling starts shaking then we've gone too far,
And I don't want to stop, and you know what you are.
```

- Scene: The four bars before it stops being careful.
- Shot: Both of them dancing in a space about two metres square, deliberately small movements.
- Cut to: A ceiling light fitting swinging half a centimetre; a glass on a shelf ticking against the wall.
- Emotion: The precise moment where caution becomes a game.
- Lighting: Lamp only; the ceiling fitting is unlit and moving in shadow.

### [chorus 1 – the hook and the wall]

```
Turn it down, don't tell the neighbours what we do,
Thin walls, low lamp, half eleven, me and you.
One more song and I promise we're through,
Turn it down, don't tell the neighbours what we do.
Palm across my mouth and my back against the door,
Bass through the boards and I'm not stopping anymore.
Number Twenty-Four is banging on the wall again,
Turn it down, don't tell the neighbours where we've been.
```

- Scene: The dance at full commitment and half volume.
- Shot: Handheld inside the small space, the camera part of the dance rather than watching it; the whole flat in one continuous move — hall, living room, kitchen doorway, back.
- Cut to: His palm across her mouth, both of them silent-laughing; her back against the front door; a fist on the other side of the party wall, seen from her side only.
- Emotion: Reckless and completely happy.
- Lighting: Lamp, plus the blue of a muted television as a second source for the first time.

### [verse 2 – three knocks and the stairs]

```
Half twelve, three knocks, and the wall goes bang bang bang,
And we froze like two kids caught halfway through a plan.
A note under the door in proper joined-up pen,
Saying, "some of us have work," and we laughed and did it again.
Saw him on the stairs this morning with his bin bag and cap,
Said, "sorry about the noise, mate," and I did not mean that.
He said, "no bother, love," and he pressed the lift call,
Then he smiled at the floor like he remembered it all.
```

- Scene: The neighbour arrives as comedy and leaves as something else.
- Shot: Three hard knocks on the party wall, the whole frame flinching; both of them frozen mid-move with completely straight faces.
- Cut to: A folded note pushed under the door in beautiful old-fashioned handwriting (composited); then morning on a concrete stairwell — a man in his seventies with a bin bag and a flat cap, entirely unbothered.
- Her reaction / Emotion: Mock contrition that fools nobody, then a small genuine warmth when he smiles at the floor.
- Lighting: Night section is lamp and television blue; the stairwell is flat grey daylight with a strip light still on.

### [pre-chorus 2 – the same four bars, less careful]

```
Keep it low, keep it slow, keep it under the beat,
Whisper it, don't shout it, put your hand on the heat.
If the ceiling starts shaking then we've gone too far,
And I don't want to stop, and you know what you are.
```

- Scene: The same four bars, the next night, with none of the caution left.
- Shot: Repeat the two-metre-square dance framing exactly; the movements are twice the size.
- Cut to: The same ceiling fitting swinging noticeably more; the same glass, now moved to the middle of the shelf.
- Emotion: Escalation played completely straight.
- Lighting: Same lamp, same blue, slightly more contrast.

### [chorus 2 – bigger]

```
Turn it down, don't tell the neighbours what we do,
Thin walls, low lamp, half eleven, me and you.
One more song and I promise we're through,
Turn it down, don't tell the neighbours what we do.
Palm across my mouth and my back against the door,
Bass through the boards and I'm not stopping anymore.
Number Twenty-Four is banging on the wall again,
Turn it down, don't tell the neighbours where we've been.
```

- Scene: The flat used properly — hallway, kitchen, the bit of floor by the window.
- Shot: A long handheld take following both of them through every room in the flat without a cut.
- Cut to: The view from the walkway outside: two silhouettes moving behind a closed curtain, the only lit window on the block.
- Emotion: Two people who have stopped pretending they are going to stop.
- Lighting: Warm from inside, sodium orange outside, the curtain acting as a screen.

### [instrumental]

- Scene: No lyrics. The dance at full size, still at half volume.
- Shots: The plucked-synth solo over a locked-off wide of the living room with both of them dancing badly and brilliantly; knuckles knocking on the party wall in tempo, in close-up, becoming the percussion; the sofa slowly sliding another few inches; feet in socks on floorboards; the lit window from outside, held.
- Emotion: Sustained silly joy.
- Lighting: Lamp and television blue, one match cut to the sodium exterior.

### [bridge – no drums, pad and nylon guitar]

```
I've spent my whole life keeping the volume down,
Small in the corridor and small in a crowd.
Made myself easy so nobody complained,
Kept half of my laugh in the back of my throat and stayed.
Then you came round on a Tuesday with a bag and a plan,
And I haven't been quiet since, and I don't think I can.
```

- Scene: The turn. The song stops being about a neighbour.
- Shot: Cold flashes — her on a night bus taking up as little room as possible; her at a table of loud people saying nothing; her laughing and then immediately covering her mouth.
- Cut to: Back to the flat, both of them sitting on the floor with their backs against the sofa, out of breath, saying nothing; then the front door, and a memory of him arriving on a Tuesday with a carrier bag.
- Emotion: The only serious eight bars in the song, and the reason it exists.
- Lighting: The flashes are cold, flat and public. The flat is warm, low and private.

### [final chorus – the hook inverted]

```
Turn it down, don't tell the neighbours what we do,
Thin walls, low lamp, half eleven, me and you.
One more song, and I'm not promising we're through,
Turn it up, let the neighbours hear it too.
Palm across my mouth and my back against the door,
Bass through the boards and I'm not stopping anymore.
Number Twenty-Four can put the kettle on again,
Turn it up, let the neighbours know where we've been.
```

- Scene: The volume goes up on purpose for the first time.
- Shot: Her hand on a speaker dial, turning it the other way; the curtains pulled fully open instead of closed.
- Cut to: The block from outside with the window now open and light spilling onto the walkway; and one shot of Number Twenty-Four on the other side of the wall, putting the kettle on, entirely calm, not banging on anything.
- Emotion: Release without a fight. Nobody is being defeated.
- Lighting: Warm light escaping the flat for the first time; the exterior grade lifts a stop.

### [post-chorus – the whispered chant]

```
Shh, shh, don't tell the neighbours,
Shh, shh, thin walls, low lamp.
Shh, shh, don't tell the neighbours,
One more song and then we'll stop, I promise.
```

- Scene: The chant, whispered, played as choreography.
- Shot: A finger to lips in close-up on each "shh", three times, from three different angles.
- Cut to: A locked-off wide of the living room with both of them doing the same two-count step, exactly in time.
- Emotion: The whole song in four bars, and the easiest thing to copy in it.
- Lighting: Lamp only, faces fully lit, background dark.

### [outro – plucks, room tone, an alarm]

```
Four in the morning and the buzzer's gone still,
Two glasses in the sink and the lamp on the sill.
Somebody's alarm going off through the wall,
And we're still up, and I'm not sorry at all.
```

- Scene: Four in the morning, everything settled, nobody asleep.
- Shot: Two glasses in a sink; the lamp moved onto the window sill; grey light beginning behind the open curtain.
- Cut to: Final shot — both of them on the floor with their backs against the sofa, an alarm going off faintly through the wall, her looking at the wall and shrugging. Held to the fade.
- Emotion: Contentment with no apology in it.
- Lighting: Warm lamp against the first cold blue of morning, the two sources meeting in the middle of the frame.

## Viral moments

- **The hook frame:** his palm across her mouth on "don't tell the neighbours", both silent-laughing, shot from her side of the frame.
- **The challenge:** the post-chorus "shh" — finger to lips, one two-count step, filmed as a locked-off wide so the whole move is learnable from one clip.
- **The caption line:** "Nothing that happens in this flat leaves this flat."
- **The turn quote:** "I've spent my whole life keeping the volume down."
- **Number Twenty-Four** putting the kettle on instead of banging on the wall, in the final chorus — the joke resolving into kindness, on screen for two seconds.
