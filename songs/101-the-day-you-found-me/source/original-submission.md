# The Day You Found Me — original submission

The song, direction and scene-by-scene video notes as submitted, revised
into an improved version. The lyric body in `lyrics.txt` is the cleaned
render copy. This file is the concept and the direction, kept as source
material. The lyrics quoted here are the same lines, with the same
punctuation as `lyrics.txt`.

**Theme:** A father who has been getting by on quiet and endurance is found
by his young daughter one ordinary morning. She does not rescue him with
anything grand. She walks into the kitchen at ten past six, asks for a plan,
and gives him a drawing. The song is his admission that the thing he thought
he was doing for her, she was doing for him.

**Mood:** Tender, tired, warm. A grey kitchen before the light comes up,
turning slowly toward a small, bright, ordinary joy. Never sugary, never
tearful for effect. The sadness is real and it is quiet.

**Message:** "Everybody thinks I saved you, but I've got it upside down."

**Genre:** Acoustic folk-pop ballad, intimate and domestic, with a warm
string-and-piano swell for the lifts. Reference comparisons are for the sound
only, never for words: a close, confiding male baritone in the warm
singer-songwriter folk-pop register, the kind of record where the guitar is
the spine and the voice sits on the microphone; a soft, widescreen final
chorus with a wordless choir, in the manner of the family-friendly acoustic
ballads that build with strings rather than drums.

**Tempo & Key:** 72 BPM, 4/4, G major. Stays in G throughout. The song lifts
by pitch and texture, not by key change: the pre-choruses lift the vocal line
a third higher, the final chorus opens the arrangement, and nothing modulates.

**Vocal setup:** Singer A, lead male baritone, late thirties, close to the
microphone and nearly spoken in the verses. A single low male harmony a third
beneath the lead joins the choruses. A soft wordless pad of female and
child-register voices joins the final chorus only. Airy unison doubles drift
beneath the outro.

**Instrumentation:** fingerpicked steel-string acoustic guitar (the spine);
upright piano (from verse 1); warm low strings (pre-choruses and choruses);
solo cello (second half); soft kick and brushed snare, upright bass (choruses
only); shaker and snare-rim brush (verses); wordless choir pad and soft brass
(final chorus); kitchen hum, kettle click, crayon scratch and paper fold (texture).

**Mood arc:**
- Intro: one guitar, a kitchen before dawn, the voice close and half-speaking.
- Verse 1: the same whisper, a piano and shaker added, a memory of a hard morning, the lift when the child appears in the doorway.
- Pre-chorus 1: the first real lift, warm strings under a sustained note.
- Chorus 1: opens rather than explodes, the emotional admission, plain and honest.
- Verse 2: pulled back to guitar and piano for the drawing, the most domestic passage, one high string note under the last couplet.
- Pre-chorus 2: the same lift, now carrying the drawing in the lyric.
- Chorus 2: wider, the harmony doubled, a cello underneath.
- Instrumental: sixteen bars of solo piano over held strings, no percussion.
- Bridge: nearly spoken over one piano, the imagined future, building steadily through the last lines.
- Chorus 3 (final): the full arrangement, strings, soft brass and a wordless choir, lifting on "you find me every day".
- Post-chorus: the drums drop out entirely, the kettle and the hallway, the quiet line that lands the song.
- Outro: back to the opening guitar alone, almost a whisper, a long decaying reverb, the kettle from the top of the song.

## Lyrics + Video Direction (scene-by-scene)

### [intro – the kitchen before dawn]

```
The kettle clicks at ten past six,
The window's holding grey,
I'm counting up the things to fix
Before I drive away.
```

- Scene: A kitchen before dawn. A kettle on a gas ring, a mug of tea going cold, the window still dark blue-grey, a car keys ring on the worktop.
- Shot: Close and locked-off on the kettle, then a slow push to the man's hands wrapped round the mug, then his face in soft grey light.
- Cut to: The kettle clicking off, the steam rising into the grey light from the window.
- Emotion: Tired, self-occupied, the list in his head before the day starts. Not yet sad, just weighed down.
- Lighting: Pre-dawn grey from the window, one warm pendant over the table, no other light.

### [verse 1 – the crate, then the doorway]

```
I used to carry mornings like a crate I couldn't set down,
Keys in one hand, trouble in the other, both my shoulders down.
I kept a running total of the ways I came up short,
And I called that being careful, and I called that being tough.
Then a door came open slowly at the far end of the hall,
And two bare feet went running on a floor that's always cold.
You had your hair gone sideways and one sock up to your knee,
And you asked me if the morning had a plan for you and me.
You climbed up on the counter where you're never let to climb,
And you told me it was going to be a good one, this time.
```

- Scene: The first stanza is his routine, handled as a tired, practical blur: the keys, the bag, the mental ledger. The second stanza is the doorway: a long hallway, a small figure at the far end in pyjamas, one sock up, hair sideways.
- Shot: Wide and still on the hallway, then the child walking the length of the cold floor toward the camera, bare feet, the man turning to see her. Over-the-shoulder on her climbing onto the kitchen counter.
- Cut to: The child on the counter, the man's hands steadying her by the waist, both in the kitchen light.
- Emotion: The weight comes off his shoulders the moment she appears. Relief first, then something like amusement, then a small surprise at how simple it is.
- Lighting: The hallway light is cool and grey, then the kitchen warms as she enters. The counter scene is the first warm light in the song.

### [pre-chorus 1 – the reasons]

```
I had been hunting for my reasons
In the hardest hour of day,
Then a small voice in a doorway
Took the heavy part away.
```

- Scene: The man at the sink or on the step outside, hands in his pockets, looking for a reason to keep going, then the child's voice from the doorway.
- Shot: Medium on him at the sink, then a reverse on the doorway with the small figure framed in it, the light behind her.
- Cut to: The child in the doorway calling to him, him turning, the first real lift in the picture.
- Emotion: Searching, then caught off guard into being lifted.
- Lighting: Cool at the sink, a warm backlight from the hall around the child in the doorway.

### [chorus 1 – the admission]

```
Everybody thinks I saved you,
But I've got it upside down.
I was the one who went missing,
Standing right here, not around.
I was tired and I was quiet
And I called it getting by,
Till you found me in the hallway
At ten past six one day.
```

- Scene: The man sitting at the kitchen table, the child on the floor with a crayon box, the morning light coming up. He is facing the room, talking to nobody in particular and to her.
- Shot: Static, a slow dolly from the table to the child on the floor. Gentle kick and brushed snare arrive on the first line.
- Cut to: The child looking up from her drawing at him, the morning light on her face.
- Emotion: Honest, plain, open. He admits it without self-pity. The chorus opens; it does not explode.
- Lighting: Morning grey turning gold through the window across the kitchen floor.

### [verse 2 – the drawing]

```
You drew a house with seven windows and a door too small to use,
And a yellow sun above the roof the sky could never lose.
You gave the clouds a purple edge, you put a fence around the lawn,
And a man beside a little girl, and the man was holding on.
You held it up and waited there and watched me while I read,
And you said that I could keep it if I kept it by my bed.
So I folded up your masterpiece and slid it in my coat,
And it rode there through the whole long day, a small and folded note.
Nobody in that building knew the reason I could stand,
There was paper in my pocket with a sun you drew by hand.
```

- Scene: The drawing itself, a child's crayon house with seven windows, a yellow sun, a purple-edged cloud, a fence, and a tall figure holding a small one by the hand. The most domestic passage of the record: a kitchen table, the crayons, the paper.
- Shot: Macro on the drawing, the crayon strokes and the paper grain, then the child holding it up to the camera. Then a slow move to the man reading it, then the folded paper going into the inside pocket of a plain cardigan.
- Cut to: The man at a desk in a car park, the paper in his coat, his hand resting on the pocket.
- Emotion: Tender, gently funny, then quietly moved. The drawing is the one thing he carries through the day.
- Lighting: Soft morning light across the table, the paper warm and bright, the car park scene in flat daylight.

### [pre-chorus 2 – the same lift, with the drawing]

```
I had been hunting for my reasons
In the hardest hour of day,
Then a small voice in a doorway
Took the heavy part away.
```

- Scene: A repeat of pre-chorus 1, but now the man's hand is on the folded drawing in his pocket, and the doorway is the office door, where he hears the echo of her voice in his head.
- Shot: Medium on his hand over the pocket, then a reverse to the office door, a corridor light behind.
- Cut to: A wider shot of him standing still in the corridor, a breath out.
- Emotion: Steadier. The lift is quieter the second time because he knows where it comes from.
- Lighting: Fluorescent office light, cool, with the warmth coming from his hand on the paper.

### [chorus 2 – the wider chorus]

```
Everybody thinks I saved you,
But I've got it upside down.
I was the one who went missing,
Standing right here, not around.
I was tired and I was quiet
And I called it getting by,
Till you found me in the hallway
At ten past six one day.
```

- Scene: The car park outside the office block at lunchtime, him sitting in his car with the drawing on the passenger seat, looking at it through the windscreen.
- Shot: Static through the windscreen of the car, then slow push-in on his face and the drawing. The harmony doubles and the cello comes in under the second half.
- Cut to: Close on his face, his mouth moving with the words, the drawing in the corner of the frame.
- Emotion: Wider and more open than chorus 1. The admission becomes a kind of gratitude.
- Lighting: Flat daylight, grey-blue sky, the car interior warm from the sun through the glass.

### [instrumental – sixteen bars, piano and strings]

- Scene: The man sitting in the car in the car park, engine off, sixteen bars of solo piano over held strings, no percussion. The sound of a man sitting still before going back inside.
- Shot: Static wide of the car in the car park, then the slow dolly to the man's hands on the wheel, then to the drawing on the seat.
- Cut to: The man getting out of the car, the drawing in his hand, walking towards the office door.
- Emotion: Stillness, gathering. Nothing said.
- Lighting: Grey afternoon, the car interior dim, the building lights just coming on.

### [bridge – the future, nearly spoken]

```
One day this hall will be a city
And the door you open, yours alone,
And the feet that came out running to me
Will be running down a road of your own.
I'll be glad. I'll carry boxes.
I'll stand waving at the kerb.
And I'll keep a folded drawing
In my coat, the way I learned.
You won't know you ever did it,
You'll just think you grew up fine.
```

- Scene: A flash-forward, not a flashback: the same hallway, now empty and dim, the child grown and leaving through a door, the man at the kerb with a moving van, the drawing folded in his coat.
- Shot: A single piano, a static frame on the hallway floor in dim light, then the child seen only from behind, walking out of frame. Then the man at the kerb, small in the frame, a hand raised.
- Cut to: Back to the present, the man in the hallway, the drawing in his hand, the light on the floor.
- Emotion: Nearly spoken, almost breathless. Proud, sad in a quiet, adult way. He chooses to be glad.
- Lighting: Dim hall, the light from an open door a long strip across the floor, then warm evening light on the kerb.

### [chorus 3 – the final chorus, full arrangement]

```
Everybody thinks I saved you,
But I've got it upside down.
You're the one who came and got me,
You're the reason I'm around.
I was tired and I was quiet
And I called it getting by,
Then you found me in the hallway,
And you find me every day.
```

- Scene: The full household in warm light: the child at the kitchen table with her drawings, the man beside her, the kettle on, a late-afternoon bright room. The wordless choir and the brass bed lift under the line about being found every day.
- Shot: Wide, the room, both figures at the table, a slow move in as the arrangement opens. The strings swell and the choir enters on the last line.
- Cut to: The child laughing at something on the page, the man laughing too.
- Emotion: Warm, grateful, the song at its most open. The emotional statement made fully.
- Lighting: Warm late-afternoon light through the kitchen window, soft gold.

### [post-chorus – the kettle and the hallway]

```
Ten past six, the kettle clicking,
Grey light coming through the floor,
You found me in the hallway
And you don't even know.
```

- Scene: The same kitchen at ten past six the next morning, the kettle on the ring, grey light on the floor. The drums are gone entirely; only the voice and a single guitar.
- Shot: Static on the kettle, then on the child's bare feet in the hallway, then the man at the counter turning to her.
- Cut to: The child walking into the kitchen, the man turning, the small smile of the day beginning.
- Emotion: Quiet awe at how ordinary the miracle is. Not a dramatic line, just noticed.
- Lighting: Grey dawn coming through the window onto the floor, the kettle steam catching the light.

### [outro – the step]

```
So sleep now, there's no hurry,
Let the morning do the rest.
You found a man who'd gone missing
And you walked him to the step.
And he isn't going back there,
Not while you still say his name.
```

- Scene: Night, the child asleep in her bed with the door open a crack and the hall light on. The man at the front step in the cold, the drawing in his coat, looking back at the lit window of her room.
- Shot: Slow static on the hall light, then a long push to the child's bed, a small shape under the blanket, then wide on the front door at night.
- Cut to: Morning, the front door open, the man walking out to the car, the sun on the step.
- Emotion: Calm, secure, the best kind of tired. He is home, and he is staying.
- Lighting: Warm hall light, cold blue night outside, then clean warm morning sun on the step.

## Viral moments

- The hook frame: a kitchen at ten past six, a kettle clicking, a child's bare feet on a cold hall floor, the first lift of the song. Caption-ready and instantly recognisable to any parent.
- The challenge: "Draw me something that found you today." Parents and kids post a crayon drawing and a short clip of the moment the child handed it over, tagged with the song's first chorus.
- The caption line: "Everybody thinks I saved you, but I've got it upside down."
- The quote: "You found me in the hallway, and you don't even know."
